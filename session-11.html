Guessing meaning from context

There is no dictionary in the exam, and every Reading passage contains words you don’t know. You don’t need all of them. You need a fast way to work out the important ones from the words around them, and the confidence to skip the rest.

Part A. The method

1

What it means

Guessing meaning from context means working out what a word means, or at least getting close, from the words around it and from the topic of the paragraph. You have about 20 minutes per passage, so you can’t stop at every new word.

ExampleIt had been raining hard through the night, so the ground was saturated.
1
What do I know? It rained all night, and it rained hard.
2
What must be true? The ground must be wet, probably very wet.
3
My guess: saturated = completely wet. Check: “so the ground was completely wet” makes sense.
2

Four steps, about ten seconds

Part B. Five kinds of clue

3

Where the meaning hides

DefinitionThe writer explains the word: “…arid, meaning extremely dry.” SynonymA similar word appears nearby: “The house was dilapidated, in very poor condition.” ExampleExamples show the meaning: “Citrus fruits such as oranges and lemons…” ContrastA word like but, although, unlike signals the opposite: “Unlike his talkative sister, Tom was reticent.” Cause and resultWhat happens tells you what the word means: “Lisa was so famished that she ate three plates.”
