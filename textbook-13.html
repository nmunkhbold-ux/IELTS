Module 13: The overview paragraph

The overview is the most important paragraph in a Task 1 answer. It tells the examiner, in two or three sentences, what the visual is really about. A clear, accurate overview is the biggest single step towards Band 7 in Task Achievement. An answer without one is usually limited to Band 5 for Task Achievement, however good its grammar is.

13.1 What makes an overview effective

Quality What it means
Selective It names the two or three biggest features, not all of them
General It uses almost no figures; if a range or an extreme is the main feature, one or two figures are acceptable
Comparative It says how things relate: which is highest, what changed most, what stayed the same
Accurate Every claim is true for the whole visual, not for part of it
In your own words It does not repeat the introduction or the task
Complete It covers every category or part of the visual, at least by grouping

13.2 How to find the two or three biggest features

Ask four questions, in this order:

  1. Direction or size. What is the biggest change, or the biggest difference?
  2. Ranking. Which item is highest or lowest, or has changed place?
  3. Grouping. Which items behave in the same way?
  4. The exception. Which one does not fit the pattern?

Then apply the so-what test to each candidate: if a reader knew only this sentence, would they understand something important about the visual? A figure such as “Northbury was 8% in 2000” fails the test. “The city with the lowest figure in 2000 had the highest by 2020” passes it.

13.3 What to leave out

Leave out Why
Detailed figures They belong in the body paragraphs
Reasons and causes The task never asks for them
Opinions and predictions The visual does not support them
In conclusion An overview comes at the start, and it is not a conclusion
Every feature An overview that tries to include everything says nothing
A repeat of the introduction It wastes your word count and adds nothing

13.4 What the overview contains, by type of visual

Type Main ingredients
Dynamic chart (line, grouped or stacked bars, table with years) The direction of the main trends; which item ends highest or lowest; any change in ranking
Static chart (bar, pie, table at one date) The largest and smallest items; the main grouping; the range
Mixed charts The main feature of each visual, the link between them, and the exception
Map or floor plan The main kind of change, two or three biggest changes, and what stays the same
Process The number of stages, the start and the end, and one special feature (a by-product, a loop or a backup)
Life cycle The number of stages, that the cycle is continuous, and the biggest change
Flow chart The number of decisions and the possible outcomes
Diagram with numbers The largest branch, and the key fact about the detail

13.5 Weak, average and strong overviews for every visual type

The three levels mean:

  • Weak (about Band 5): it lists figures, restates the task, or is too vague to tell the reader anything.
  • Average (about Band 6): it is correct but general; it describes each item separately and makes no comparison.
  • Strong (Band 7 and above): it selects the main features, compares them, and includes the exception.

Each set refers to a visual earlier in this textbook, so you can check every claim against the data.

Visual Weak Average Strong
Line graph (cycling, Module 2) Northbury was 8% in 2000 and 28% in 2020. Eastfield was 20% and 21%. Southport was 14% and 10%. Northbury increased, Southport decreased, and Eastfield went up and then down. Overall, cycling became far more popular in Northbury, whereas it declined in Southport, and Eastfield ended close to where it began. As a result, the city with the lowest figure in 2000 had the highest by 2020.
Bar chart (leisure, Module 3) The chart shows how many hours teenagers spent on five activities in 2010 and 2025. Some activities went up and some went down. Overall, teenagers spent much more time on social media and video games in 2025, whereas television and reading both declined sharply. Social media, the least popular activity in 2010, had become the most time-consuming by 2025.
Pie charts (electricity, Module 4) Coal was 45% in 2000 and 18% in 2020. Coal decreased and wind and solar power increased. Overall, the country relied much less on coal and much more on gas and on wind and solar power by 2020. Gas replaced coal as the largest source, while nuclear and hydroelectric power changed very little.
Table (internet use, Module 4) 97% of 16- to 24-year-olds used social media, and 96% of 25- to 44-year-olds used email. Younger people use the internet more than older people. Overall, age had a strong influence on internet use. Younger people favoured social media and streaming, people aged 25 to 44 favoured banking and shopping, and email was the only service used by a clear majority of every age group.
Stacked bars (housing, Module 5) Private renting was 10% in 1990 and 26% in 2020. Renting went up and social housing went down. Overall, private renting became much more common, while social housing declined steadily. The share of households that owned their homes stayed within a narrow range of 60% to 66% throughout the period.
Mixed charts (visitors and temperature, Module 6) The bar chart shows visitors and the line graph shows temperature. Visitors and temperature both rise in the summer. Visitor numbers followed the temperature closely: both were lowest in winter, rose towards the summer and peaked in August. The only clear exception was December, when visitor numbers increased even though temperatures fell.
Manufacturing process (olive oil, Module 7) There are some stages in making olive oil. First the olives are picked, and in the end the oil is put into bottles. The process consists of seven stages, beginning with harvesting and ending with bottling. The olives are first cleaned and crushed, and the oil is then separated from the solid waste, which is used as fuel.
Life cycle (frog, Module 7) A frog lays eggs. The frog changes during its life. The cycle has six stages and is continuous. The most important change occurs in the middle stages, when the tadpole, which breathes through gills, develops lungs and becomes a young frog.
Flow chart (refund, Module 7) A customer asks for a refund. The shop decides whether to give the refund. The procedure includes two decisions and can end in one of three ways: the request is refused, the customer receives a full refund, or the refund is reduced by a postage fee.
Map (village, Module 8) There was a farm in 1995 and a housing estate in 2025. The village changed a lot between 1995 and 2025. The village became much more developed: farmland was replaced by housing, the shopping and school facilities were expanded, and a new road and a park were added. However, the main road, the church and the river remained unchanged.
Floor plan (community centre, Module 8) The community centre has a hall, a kitchen and some other rooms. Some of the rooms in the building were changed. The building was reorganised to create more space for public use: the main hall became much larger, a café was added, and one room was given a new function. The entrance hall, kitchen and toilets stayed in the same positions.
Mechanism (solar heater, Module 9) The sun heats a fluid and water goes to the taps. The system uses sunlight to make hot water. The system works in two connected parts: a closed loop in which a fluid is heated by the sun and carries its heat to a storage tank, and a separate water supply that is warmed in the tank. An electric heater is also available when there is little sunlight.

13.6 Why the strong version is stronger

Feature Where it appears in the strong overviews
It names a change in ranking Cycling (lowest to highest), leisure (least popular to most), electricity (gas replaces coal)
It groups, so it covers everything Leisure (two up, two down), table (younger, middle, all), floor plan (changed and unchanged rooms)
It includes the exception Cycling (Eastfield), mixed charts (December), flow chart (three outcomes)
It says what stays the same Map (road, church, river), floor plan (three rooms), electricity (nuclear and hydroelectric)
It counts or names the shape Process (seven stages), cycle (six stages, continuous), flow chart (two decisions)

13.7 An overview that is not accurate

An overview must be true for the whole visual. These examples fail that test.

Inaccurate overview The problem Corrected
Overall, cycling increased in all three cities. Southport fell (14% to 10%) and Eastfield was close to its starting level Overall, cycling became far more popular in Northbury, whereas it declined in Southport.
Overall, all five activities took more time in 2025. Television and reading fell Overall, social media and video games took more time, whereas television and reading took less.
Overall, coal was replaced by renewable energy. Gas was the largest source in 2020, and hydroelectric power (also renewable) hardly changed Overall, coal was replaced mainly by gas and by wind and solar power.

13.8 A three-step routine for the overview

  1. Find the two or three features (direction, ranking, grouping, exception) and apply the so-what test.
  2. Write two sentences: the main feature or contrast first (with whereas or while), and the exception or the unchanged part second.
  3. Check that it contains almost no figures, that it is true for the whole visual, and that it is not a repeat of your introduction.

13.9 Where the overview goes

Write the overview as its own paragraph directly after the introduction. Examiners look for it early, and a separate paragraph makes it easy to find. Never put it at the end as a conclusion: a conclusion repeats and an overview selects, and a late overview can be missed.
