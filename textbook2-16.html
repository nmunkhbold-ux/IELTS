A. Ten rules for Task 2

  1. Read the question twice and use TFIL (topic, focus, instruction, limits).
  2. Number the main parts. Every part needs its own developed paragraph.
  3. Choose your position first and write it in one sentence.
  4. Plan for five minutes. Two ideas, each with an explanation and an example.
  5. Write four paragraphs: introduction, two body paragraphs, conclusion (five paragraphs for a three-part question).
  6. Open each body paragraph with a topic sentence that states the main idea.
  7. Develop every idea with the IEER pattern: idea, explanation, example, result.
  8. Join sentences with because, which, although; do not start every sentence with a linker.
  9. Use a formal register and precise collocations. No contractions, no kids, no a lot of.
  10. Check for five minutes: parts answered, position the same at both ends, topic sentences, your three usual errors, length and key spellings.

B. The 40-minute plan (recap)

Minutes Step
0–3 Read, TFIL, number the parts
3–8 Plan: position, two ideas with explanation and example
8–12 Introduction
12–20 Body paragraph 1
20–28 Body paragraph 2
28–32 Conclusion
32–35 Spare time: finish or improve
35–40 Check

In the whole 60-minute test, Task 1 takes about 20 minutes and Task 2 about 40. Task 2 is worth two thirds of the Writing mark, so do not take time from it.

C. The full checklist for marking your own essay

Tick each statement that is true. Be honest: a tick for something you hoped you did is worth nothing.

Limit checks. If any of these is false, that criterion is limited to about Band 5, whatever else the essay does well.

  • ☐ I answered every main part of the question (a part only partly answered limits Task Response).
  • ☐ My essay has clear paragraphs (missing paragraphing limits Coherence and Cohesion).
  • ☐ The essay is an essay, not a letter or a list (the wrong format limits Task Response).
  • ☐ My sentences are not almost all simple (almost no complex sentences limits Grammar).
  • ☐ My errors do not make the reader stop and re-read (frequent errors that cause difficulty limit Grammar; noticeable spelling and word-form errors limit Vocabulary).

Task Response

  1. I answered every main part, and I can point to the sentence that does it.
  2. My position is clear in the introduction.
  3. My position is the same in the conclusion.
  4. Each main idea is explained (I wrote because, this means or in other words).
  5. Each main idea has an example or a specific detail.
  6. Every idea stays on the focus of the question.

Coherence and Cohesion

  1. The essay has an introduction, body paragraphs and a conclusion in separate paragraphs.
  2. Each body paragraph has one main idea and a topic sentence.
  3. The paragraphs follow a logical order.
  4. Fewer than half my sentences start with a linking word.
  5. I used reference words (this trend, these problems, they), and each one is clear.
  6. My linking words show real relationships (reason, result, contrast).

Lexical Resource

  1. I used precise topic words, not thing, good, bad or big.
  2. I used at least six natural collocations.
  3. I avoided informal words and contractions.
  4. I did not repeat the same key word more than four times.
  5. My spelling of common words is correct.
  6. My word forms are correct (economic not economy growth).

Grammatical Range and Accuracy

  1. I used simple, compound and complex sentences.
  2. I used at least five of the ten patterns from Module 12.
  3. At least two thirds of my sentences are error-free (count them).
  4. My verbs agree with their subjects and my tenses are consistent.
  5. I used articles correctly in most places.
  6. My punctuation is correct (no comma splices, commas after introductory phrases).

Scoring (a teaching estimate only). Count the ticked statements out of 24.

Ticks Estimated level
22–24 About Band 7 or above
19–21 About Band 6.5
15–18 About Band 6
11–14 About Band 5.5
Fewer than 11 Below Band 5.5

If you fail a limit check, drop the estimate by at least half a band. Counts cannot replace an examiner's judgement, so ask a teacher to mark at least one essay in every four.

D. Phrase bank by job

Use these as a reference, not a script. Choose the phrase that fits your idea.

Job Phrases
Starting an introduction It is often argued that... / In recent years,... has become increasingly common. / There is an ongoing debate about whether... / The question of whether... is a matter of much discussion.
Giving your position I strongly agree that... / I largely agree, although... / I disagree with this view because... / This essay will discuss both views, but I believe that...
Topic sentences The main reason for... is that... / One important factor is... / Another point worth considering is... / A further benefit is...
Explaining This is because... / In other words,... / This means that... / The reason for this is that...
Giving an example For instance,... / For example,... / A good example of this is... / Take... as an example.
Showing a result As a result,... / Consequently,... / This leads to... / Therefore,...
Counter-argument Admittedly,... / It is true that..., but... / Some people argue that... However, this overlooks the fact that...
Contrast On the other hand,... / In contrast,... / While..., ... / Whereas..., ...
Solutions One way to tackle this problem is to... / A practical step would be to... / Governments could... / If..., ... would...
Weighing (outweigh) Despite these drawbacks,... / On balance,... / Overall, the benefits are greater because...
Predictions ... is likely to... / ... may well... / It seems unlikely that...
Hedging (careful claims) tends to, may, can, is likely to, in most cases, to some extent, generally
Concluding In conclusion,... / To sum up,... / Overall,... / On balance,...
Avoid contractions (don't), etc., and so on, kids, a lot of, Nowadays, in the modern world, In my opinion, I think, you

E. Sentence builders

Fill in the blanks to make the skeleton of a paragraph. Then replace the frames with your own words.

Opinion essay

Introduction: [Background in your own words.] Some people believe that [the view]. I [strongly agree / partly agree / disagree] with this view, because [reason 1] and [reason 2].

Body 1: The main reason for [supporting / rejecting] this idea is that [reason 1]. [Explanation: This is because...] [Example: For instance,...] [Result: As a result,...]

Body 2: Another reason is that [reason 2]. [Explanation] [Example] Some people argue that [the other side]. However, [answer].

Conclusion: In conclusion, I believe that [position], because [reason 1] and [reason 2].

Discussion essay

Introduction: [Background.] Some people believe that [view A], while others argue that [view B]. This essay will discuss both views, but I believe that [your opinion].

Body 1: Those who favour [view A] argue that [reason]. [Explanation] [Example] [Result]

Body 2: On the other hand, supporters of [view B] believe that [reason]. [Explanation] [Example] For this reason, I believe that [your opinion].

Conclusion: In conclusion, although [view A has a strength], I believe that [your opinion], since [main reason].

Problem and solution essay

Introduction: [Background.] This trend causes [problem 1] and [problem 2], but I believe that [type of solution] can reduce them.

Body 1: The first problem is that [problem 1]. [Explanation and result.] A second problem is that [problem 2]. [Example.]

Body 2: Several measures could help. Firstly, [actor] should [solution 1], because [reason]. Secondly, [actor] could [solution 2]. If these steps were combined, [result].

Conclusion: In conclusion, [problems in brief]. However, [solutions in brief].

Advantages and disadvantages essay (outweigh)

Introduction: [Background.] Although this trend has some drawbacks, I believe that the advantages are greater overall.

Body 1: The main advantage of [topic] is [advantage]. [Explanation] [Example] In addition, [advantage 2].

Body 2: On the other hand, there are real disadvantages. [Disadvantage 1.] [Disadvantage 2.] However, I believe that these problems are less serious than the benefits, because [reason: they can be reduced / affect few people].

Conclusion: In conclusion, [advantages in brief]. Although [disadvantages in brief], I believe that the advantages outweigh the disadvantages.

Two-part essay

Introduction: [Background.] There are several reasons for [trend], and I believe that it is [mainly positive / mainly negative].

Body 1: The first reason is that [reason 1]. [Explanation and example.] A second reason is [reason 2]. [Explanation.]

Body 2: I consider this trend [positive / negative] for several reasons. For [group 1], [effect]. For [group 2], [effect]. It is true that [limit]. However, [answer].

Conclusion: In conclusion, [answer to question 1]. In my view, [answer to question 2], because [main reason].

F. The mock test

Write the four essays in four separate sessions, under exam conditions: 40 minutes each, no dictionary and no phone. Do not read the marking guides until you have finished. Mark each essay with the checklist in part C, and record the results in your progress log (Module 1).

Mock test 1: opinion

Some people believe that employers should pay for their employees to study for further qualifications. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Mock test 2: discussion

Some people think that people should live in the same area all their lives. Others believe that moving to different places is better.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Mock test 3: problem and solution

Young people today spend less time with their families than in the past. What problems might this cause? What can be done?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Mock test 4: advantages and disadvantages

More and more tourists visit historical sites and natural parks. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Marking guide. Each answer should contain the points below. Your ideas may differ and still be good.

Test Main parts A Band 7 answer includes Ideas that fit Likely traps
1. Opinion 1. Position (how far you agree) A clear position in the introduction and conclusion. Two developed reasons. Optional counter-argument For: staff become more skilled and productive; employees stay longer; the company benefits. Against: the cost; staff may leave after being trained; employees should invest in themselves. A middle position: pay for courses that relate to the job Writing about education in general. Ignoring pay
2. Discussion 1. Staying in one area 2. Moving 3. Your opinion A paragraph for each view with reasons and examples. A clear opinion Staying: family and friends, community, familiar surroundings, low costs. Moving: new jobs and education, wider experience, meeting new people Describing both views and giving no opinion. Giving one view much less space
3. Problem and solution 1. Problems 2. Solutions At least two problems and two matched solutions. Solutions with actors and reasons Problems: weaker family bonds; less guidance for children; loneliness for older relatives. Solutions: family time rules (shared meals); employers offering flexible hours; schools involving parents Describing the causes of the situation. Vague solutions (people must change)
4. Outweigh 1. Advantages 2. Disadvantages 3. Judgement Advantages and disadvantages developed, and a weighed judgement (outweigh) Advantages: income for local people, education about heritage, funds for protecting sites. Disadvantages: damage and crowding, pollution, loss of authenticity, high prices for residents. Weighing: can the disadvantages be managed (visitor limits, entry fees)? Listing points without weighing. Not saying which side is heavier

After the mock test. Compare the four results. Which essay type gave you the most ticks? Which gave you the fewest? Repeat the weakest type with two new questions from Modules 5 to 10. Then re-mark your starting-point essay from Module 1 and compare it with your best mock essay.

G. The last week before the test

Day What to do
7 days before One timed essay on a question you have not seen. Mark it with the checklist
6–5 days Read your error log and the last ten rows twice. Practise planning for five minutes on four new questions
4 days One timed essay. Practise counting your words (by line on paper, or typing speed on a computer)
3 days Review the phrase bank and your topic vocabulary. Write introductions only, for six questions
2 days Rest. Read one model essay from Modules 5 to 10. Do not write
1 day Read the ten rules in part A. Prepare your pens or your keyboard routine. Sleep early
Test day Read the question twice. Number the parts. Plan for five minutes. Do not memorise an essay

Two reminders. First, do not try to use a memorised answer. A totally memorised response can receive no credit, and phrases forced into the wrong place weaken your vocabulary score. Second, if you take the computer-delivered test, practise typing your essays in the final weeks, so that typing speed does not slow you down. The test shows a running word count, so you can check your length as you write.
