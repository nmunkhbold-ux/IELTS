Session 4

Speaking Part 1: talk about yourself

Part 1 is the first 4–5 minutes of the Speaking test: easy questions about your life. The skill is not the content but the English: answer directly, add one or two details, and sound natural. Today you will learn how, then practise with more than 380 real-style questions.

Part B. What makes a good answer?

3

Five mistakes to avoid

Very short answers: “Yes, I do.”
Answer, then add one or two details.
Very long answers that ramble for a minute.
Two to four sentences, then stop.
Memorised answers that sound like a speech.
Natural, spontaneous answers. Examiners notice memorised language.
Overthinking and long silences.
Say what is true for you, simply.
Panicking at unusual questions.
Say something simple and move on.
4

Which answer is best?

The examiner asks: “Do you like music?” Decide what is wrong or right with each answer.

5

Unusual questions

Sometimes a question surprises you. Don’t panic: give a simple, honest answer. Click each question to see a sample.

Useful starters: “That’s not something I’ve thought about much, but…” / “I’m not really sure, but I’d say…” / “To be honest, I don’t… because…”

Part C. Seven ways to extend your answer

6

Answer + one extra detail

Q: Do you like music?
A: Yes, I do.
Q: Do you like music?
A: Definitely. I often go to live concerts because my friend plays in a band, and at home I relax with classical music.

The extended answer uses three of the seven strategies below. Click each strategy to see how it works.

7

Build an answer

Question: “How often do you socialise with friends?” Start with the direct answer, then click strategies to add details. A good Part 1 answer is 2–4 sentences.

8

Name the strategy

Question: “Do you like reading?” Which strategy does each extension use?

    Part F. The question bank

    12

    All questions by topic

    Prepare ideas, not memorised answers. Open a topic and practise every question with a partner or alone.

    13

    Homework

    • Answer “How often do you socialise with friends?” in three different ways, each using a different pair of strategies.
    • Choose two topics from the question bank. Record yourself answering every question in each, then write out one answer and underline the strategies you used.
    • Do one mock Part 1 test and note any question that made you stop and think. Practise it again.
