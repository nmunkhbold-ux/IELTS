1

The four-paragraph plan

1
Introduction: one sentence. Say what the chart shows, in your own words.
2
Overview: one or two sentences. The big picture, with no numbers.
3
Details 1: three or four sentences. One group of similar information, with numbers.
4
Details 2: three or four sentences. The other group, with comparisons.
Aim for 160–190 words in 20 minutes. Report facts only: no opinions, no reasons, and no “In conclusion”.

    The details: group, then compare

    Group. Put similar information together. Which paragraph does each fact belong in?

    Compare. A list of numbers is not a comparison. Choose the sentence that compares.

    Model detail paragraphsIn the United States and Canada, daily routines are very similar. American pupils sleep for 8.5 hours, study for 8 and relax for 7.5. Canadian pupils spend almost the same amount of time on each activity: 8 hours sleeping, 8.5 studying and, again, 7.5 relaxing.

    Australia, by contrast, stands out. Pupils there study for 13 hours a day, roughly five hours more than in the other two countries. As a result, they sleep the least, at 7 hours, and relax for only 4, about half the time of American and Canadian pupils.

    Your turn: a full report

    Task (20 minutes)The pie chart below shows the native languages spoken by students at Cambridge College in 2007. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.
    English 45%Hindi 30%Chinese 13%Urdu 10%French 2%
    The data is from 2007, so use the past tense: “English was the most common language.” It is a pie chart, so use proportion and percentage, not “number”.
    20:00

    0 words

      Model report (open after you have written yours)

      The pie chart illustrates the proportion of students at Cambridge College in 2007 who spoke each of five native languages.

      Overall, English was the most common first language, followed by Hindi, and together these two languages accounted for three quarters of all students. In contrast, French was spoken by only a very small minority.

      English was the native language of 45% of students, almost half of the college population. Hindi was the second most widely spoken language, at 30%, which was two thirds of the figure for English. Taken together, speakers of these two languages greatly outnumbered all of the other groups.

      The remaining quarter of the student body was shared among three languages. Chinese and Urdu were spoken by fairly similar proportions of students, at 13% and 10% respectively. French, by contrast, accounted for just 2%, which made it by far the smallest group, at less than a fifth of the figure for Urdu.
