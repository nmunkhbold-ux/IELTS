Session 2

The overview and detail paragraphs

Last session you wrote the introduction. Today you will write the rest of the report: an overview that gives the big picture, and two detail paragraphs that compare the numbers.

SleepingStudyingRelaxing

The daily routines of school pupils in three countries (hours per day).

1

Which overview is best?

An overview tells the reader the most important thing about the chart, in one or two sentences, with no detailed numbers. Choose the best one.

2

Find the big picture

Before you write the overview, answer these questions about the chart. Say your answer, then click to check.

3

Build the overview

Choose the right words to complete the overview.

Australian pupils devote time to studying than those in the other two countries, pupils in the United States and Canada have a much more day.

4

Group the information

Do not describe the chart country by country, bar by bar. Put similar information together. Here, the United States and Canada are similar, and Australia is different. Decide which detail paragraph each fact belongs in.

5

Compare, don’t list

A list of numbers is not a comparison. Choose the sentence that compares.

6

Link the details

Complete detail paragraph 2 with the right linking words.

Australia stands out. Pupils there study for 13 hours a day, is roughly five hours more than in the other two countries. they sleep the least, at 7 hours, have only 4 hours to relax, which is around the relaxation time of American and Canadian pupils.

7

Present or past?

Look at the time shown in each chart and choose the correct verb.

8

Your turn: the full report

Task sentenceThe pie chart below shows the native languages spoken by students at Cambridge College in year 2007. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.
English 45%Hindi 30% Chinese 13%Urdu 10%French 2%

Plan first: Detail paragraph 1 can cover the two biggest languages (English and Hindi). Detail paragraph 2 can cover the three smaller ones.

0 words

    Model report (open after you have written yours)

    The pie chart illustrates the proportion of students at Cambridge College in 2007 who spoke each of five native languages.

    Overall, English was the most common first language, followed by Hindi, and these two languages together accounted for three quarters of all students. In contrast, French was spoken by only a very small minority.

    English was the native language of 45% of students, which was almost half of the college population. Hindi was the second most widely spoken language, at 30%, which was two thirds of the figure for English. Taken together, speakers of these two languages greatly outnumbered all of the other groups.

    The remaining quarter of the student body was shared among three languages. Chinese and Urdu were spoken by fairly similar proportions of students, at 13% and 10% respectively. French, by contrast, accounted for just 2%, which made it by far the smallest group and less than a fifth of the figure for Urdu.
