Module 9: Two-part (direct) questions

Some Task 2 questions give a short statement and then ask two separate questions. There is no "discuss both views" or "to what extent" to guide you. Each question is a main part, and each needs its own developed paragraph. Students who answer the first question well and the second in one sentence keep Task Response at Band 5.

A. Understanding the question

Common pairs of questions

Pattern Example questions What each part needs
Why + evaluate Why is this happening? Is this a positive or negative development? Q1: two reasons. Q2: a clear judgement with reasons
Why + predict Why is this happening? Do you think it will continue? Q1: two reasons. Q2: a clear prediction with reasons
Opinion + reason Do you think this is a good idea? Why? A position, then reasons (close to an opinion essay)
Cause + effect What are the causes of this? What are the effects? Q1: causes. Q2: effects. Keep them separate
How + who How does this affect people? Who is responsible? Q1: effects. Q2: a person or group, with reasons

Two questions mean two main parts. Underline each question. Write a number beside it. At the top of your plan, write Part 1 and Part 2.

"Positive or negative?" This question needs a judgement. You may say "mainly positive" or "mainly negative", with reasons and one limit. You should not write "both" without saying which is greater. It is a short opinion essay inside the second paragraph.

"Do you think it will continue?" This question needs a prediction. Use careful language (is likely to, may well, is unlikely to) and give reasons for your prediction. A prediction without reasons is only a guess.

B. Planning the answer

The plan follows the questions. One body paragraph per question is the safest design.

Part Paragraph Content
Introduction 1 Topic in your own words, a signal that you will answer both questions, and (if the question asks for a judgement) your position
Question 1 2 Two reasons or causes, each with an explanation or example
Question 2 3 Your answer first, in one sentence. Then two supporting reasons, and (optionally) one limit
Conclusion 4 Both answers in brief

Finding reasons for a "why" question. Think about four kinds of cause.

Kind Questions to ask
Economic Is it cheaper, richer, more efficient?
Social Have lifestyles, families or attitudes changed?
Technological Has new technology made it possible or necessary?
Personal What do individuals want?

Choose the two kinds that give you the clearest ideas and the best examples.

A completed plan

Many adults continue to study for new qualifications throughout their lives. Why do people do this? Do you think it is a positive development?

Notes
Question T: adult education. F: reasons for returning to study, and whether the trend is positive. I: two-part, 2 parts. L: throughout their lives
Introduction Past: study ended in the twenties. Now adults return at any age. Position: mainly positive
Body 1: reasons 1. Work changes quickly (technology makes skills old; example: bank clerk). 2. Personal interest (longer, healthier lives; learning after retirement)
Body 2: evaluation Positive. Individuals: active mind, confidence, less loneliness (example: retired teacher). Society: skilled workforce, less unemployment. Limit: cost and time; answer: online and evening courses
Conclusion Reasons: changing jobs, interest. Judgement: positive for individuals and the economy

C. Writing structure

Paragraph Content Frames
Introduction Topic, a link to both questions, position There are several reasons for this change, and I believe that it is mainly a positive development.
Body 1 Reason 1 with explanation and example. Reason 2 with explanation The first reason is that... / A second reason is... / This is largely due to...
Body 2 A one-sentence answer, then reasons I consider this trend positive for several reasons. / For individuals,... For society,... / It is true that..., but...
Conclusion Both answers In conclusion,... mainly because... In my view, this is...

Language for reasons (Question 1)

One reason for this is that... / This trend is largely due to... / This can be explained by... / Another factor is... / The main cause is... / This is driven by...

Language for judgements (Question 2)

This is mainly a positive development because... / On balance, the benefits outweigh the risks. / Although there are some drawbacks, ... is beneficial to... / I regard this trend as worrying, because...

Language for predictions

It is likely that... / ... will probably continue to... / It seems unlikely that... will disappear completely. / In the future,... may well...

Common mistakes in two-part questions

Mistake Fix
Answering question 2 in a single sentence Give question 2 a full paragraph
Mixing the answers to both questions in the same paragraph One paragraph for each question
Not choosing (it is both positive and negative) Choose the heavier side
Predictions without reasons Add because and one example
Reasons that are all the same kind (three economic reasons) Choose two different kinds of reason
A long personal story Use a short, general example

D. Band 7+ model essay

Many adults continue to study for new qualifications throughout their lives. Why do people do this? Do you think it is a positive development?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Model answer · 280 words

In the past, most people finished their education in their twenties, but today many adults return to study at almost any age. There are several reasons for this change, and I believe that it is mainly a positive development.

The first reason is that the world of work changes quickly. New technology can make old skills less useful, so employees often need new qualifications to keep their jobs or to change careers. A bank clerk whose work is now done by online systems, for example, may study accounting or computing in order to find a new position. A second reason is personal interest. People now live longer and stay healthy for more years, so many of them want to learn languages, art or history after retirement, simply because it makes life more interesting.

I consider this trend positive for several reasons. For individuals, learning keeps the mind active and increases confidence, and older learners often feel less lonely when they study with others. A retired teacher who joins an art class, for instance, often makes new friends as well as new skills. For society, a workforce with up-to-date skills helps the economy to grow and reduces unemployment. Employers benefit too, because staff who keep learning are usually more productive. It is true that adult study can be expensive and may take time away from families. However, online courses and evening classes now make learning cheaper and more flexible, so these problems are becoming smaller.

In conclusion, adults return to study mainly because their jobs are changing and because they enjoy learning. In my view, this is a positive trend, because it benefits both individuals and the economy as a whole.

E. The model essay explained

Paragraph by paragraph

Paragraph What it does Why it works
1. Introduction (39 words) Gives the background (past and present), signals that reasons will follow, and states the position on question 2 The examiner sees both parts from the start
2. Body 1: Why? (94 words) Reason 1 (work changes), with an example (bank clerk). Reason 2 (personal interest), with a reason (longer, healthier lives) Two different kinds of reason (economic and personal), both explained
3. Body 2: Positive? (111 words) A one-sentence answer, then benefits for individuals and for society, with examples, then one limit (cost, time) and its answer The judgement is clear and supported. The limit is acknowledged and answered
4. Conclusion (36 words) Both answers in brief No new ideas. The position matches the introduction

The four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the essay What would push it further
Task Response Both questions answered in their own paragraphs. The position is clear. Ideas are extended and supported with examples A more specific example for the second reason
Coherence and Cohesion Clear signals: The first reason..., A second reason..., For individuals... For society..., It is true that... However.... Reference: this change, this trend, these problems A wider range of linking inside paragraph 3
Lexical Resource qualifications, up-to-date skills, keeps the mind active, increases confidence, flexible, workforce, grow the economy More topic nouns around education (lifelong learning, adult learners)
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Relative clauses (whose work is now done by..., who joins an art class, staff who keep learning). Passive (is now done). Modals (can make, may study, may take). Comparatives (cheaper and more flexible). Gerunds as subjects (learning keeps the mind active) More participle clauses (Module 12)

F. Try it yourself

Fewer people read printed newspapers than in the past. Why is this happening? Do you think printed newspapers will disappear completely?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

  1. Analyse (3 minutes). T, F, I (two-part: why and predict), L.
  2. Plan (5 minutes). Two reasons for Question 1. A clear prediction with two reasons for Question 2.
  3. Write (27 minutes) and check (5 minutes).
  4. Compare with the sample plan.
Show the sample plan

Your plan may differ and still be good.

  • Analysis. T: printed newspapers. F: reasons for the decline, and whether they will disappear. I: two-part, 2 parts. L: completely.
  • Introduction. The decline is clear; reasons are technology and habits; position: newspapers will not disappear completely but will become rare.
  • Body 1 (why). Technology: news is free and instant on phones (example: a news alert within minutes of an event). Cost and habit: printed papers cost money and arrive a day late; younger readers rarely buy them.
  • Body 2 (will they disappear?). Unlikely to disappear completely, because some readers value paper (older readers, collectors of weekend editions) and printed papers can be seen as more trustworthy and easier to read for long articles. But they will become smaller, with fewer pages and higher prices. Limit: some local papers may close.
  • Conclusion. Technology and habit explain the decline; a small printed market will probably survive.

More two-part questions for practice

  1. Many people prefer to take holidays in their own country. Why is this? Is it a positive or negative trend?
  2. In many countries, people are getting married later than in the past. Why is this? Is it a positive or negative development?
  3. More and more people are choosing to live alone. What are the reasons? Is this a good or bad trend?
  4. Some people choose to do volunteer work in their free time. Why do they do this? Do you think volunteering benefits the volunteer or society more?
  5. Public libraries are losing visitors. Why is this happening? Do you think libraries will still be needed in the future?
  6. Many young people choose not to learn to drive. Why is this? Do you think it is a positive development?
