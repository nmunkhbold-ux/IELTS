Module 12: Paraphrasing the question

The introduction of a Task 1 answer restates the task in your own words. It takes one or two sentences, and the goal is to change the words and the structure without changing the meaning. A good paraphrase shows the examiner that you understand the visual, and it uses vocabulary and grammar that are your own.

12.1 Five techniques

Technique What you do Example (original, then paraphrase)
Synonyms Replace key words with words of the same meaning percentage → proportion, share; households → homes; shows → illustrates, compares
Word forms Change a noun to a verb, a verb to a noun, an adjective to a noun the increase in sales → sales increased; different → differences
Sentence structure Change the order, or turn a phrase into a clause the number of tourists who visited → how many tourists visited, or visitor numbers
Active and passive Change who is the subject how paper is recycled → the process of recycling paper
Grammar patterns Replace a phrase with a clause, a relative clause or a noun phrase between 2000 and 2020 → over a twenty-year period

Most good paraphrases combine two or three techniques. Changing one word is not enough, and changing every word is a risk.

12.2 What must not change

Keep Why
Numbers, years and units They are facts, not wording
Names of places and people They cannot be paraphrased
The measure: percentage, number, rate, amount, average These words have different meanings (see below)
Key technical words If there is no exact synonym, keep the word (hours, teenagers, sediment)
The type of visual Bar chart, pie chart and table are not interchangeable

Words that look like synonyms but are not.

Do not swap Because
number and percentage or proportion A number is a count; a percentage is a share of a total
amount and number Amount is for uncountable nouns, number for countable ones
average and total An average is per person or per unit; a total is the sum
rate and number A rate is a share per person or per 1,000, not a count
tourists and visitors Visitors include people who are not tourists, so keep the original if the task is specific
increase and rise with an object Rise has no object (they increased prices, not they rose prices)

12.3 Twenty-four task statements, paraphrased

Each original statement is written for this textbook. Compare the paraphrase with the original and read what changed.

No. Original task statement Paraphrase What changed and why it works
1 The graph below shows the percentage of households with internet access in four countries between 2000 and 2020. The line graph compares the proportion of homes that had internet access in four countries over a twenty-year period. Shows → compares; percentage → proportion; households → homes; between 2000 and 2020 → over a twenty-year period; a relative clause is added
2 The chart shows the number of tourists who visited five cities in 2023. The bar chart illustrates how many visitors went to five cities in 2023. Shows → illustrates; the number of tourists who visited → how many visitors went to (a new structure)
3 The pie charts show the main sources of energy used in one country in 1990 and 2020. The two pie charts compare the principal types of energy consumed in one country in 1990 and in 2020. Main → principal; sources → types; used → consumed; the number of charts is stated
4 The table gives information about average monthly spending on food, housing and transport by three age groups. The table provides data on how much people in three age groups spend each month, on average, on food, housing and transport. Gives information about → provides data on; spending (noun) → spend (verb); the order of the phrases is changed
5 The diagram shows how paper is recycled. The diagram illustrates the stages in the recycling of paper. A passive clause becomes a noun phrase (the recycling of paper); how → the stages in
6 The maps show a town centre in 1990 and today. The two maps illustrate the changes that have taken place in a town centre between 1990 and the present. Shows → illustrates the changes that have taken place; today → the present; the present perfect fits today
7 The floor plans show the ground floor of a library before and after refurbishment. The plans show how the layout of the ground floor of a library changed when it was refurbished. Noun (refurbishment) → passive verb (was refurbished); layout is added because a plan shows layout
8 The flow chart shows how a university decides whether to admit an applicant. The flow chart outlines the procedure that a university follows when it assesses an application for admission. Decides whether to admit → assesses an application for admission; outlines the procedure names the type
9 The line graph shows changes in the price of oil from 2005 to 2025. The line graph illustrates how oil prices changed over the twenty years from 2005 to 2025. Noun phrase (changes in the price of oil) → clause (how oil prices changed); a period replaces from … to …
10 The bar chart shows the average number of hours students spend studying each week in six countries. The bar chart compares weekly study time among students in six countries. A relative clause is compressed into a noun phrase (weekly study time); each week → weekly
11 The graph shows the percentage of people who owned a car in three cities from 1980 to 2010. The graph illustrates what proportion of the population in three cities owned a car over a thirty-year period. Percentage of people → proportion of the population; from 1980 to 2010 → over a thirty-year period
12 The table shows the results of a survey about why people use public transport. The table presents the findings of a survey that asked people for their reasons for using public transport. Results → findings; why people use → their reasons for using; a relative clause is added
13 The charts show the proportion of men and women working in five industries. The charts compare the shares of male and female employees in five industries. Proportion → shares; men and women working → male and female employees
14 The diagram shows the life cycle of a butterfly. The diagram illustrates the stages through which a butterfly passes during its life. A noun phrase (life cycle) becomes a relative clause with a fronted preposition (through which … passes)
15 The line graph shows the number of visitors to a museum each month in 2022. The line graph tracks monthly visitor numbers at a museum during 2022. Shows → tracks; each month → monthly; the number of visitors → visitor numbers
16 The pie chart shows how a family spent its monthly income in 2023. The pie chart shows the breakdown of a family's monthly spending in 2023. A clause (how … spent its income) → a noun phrase (the breakdown of … spending)
17 The chart shows how much money was spent on advertising in four media in 2010 and 2020. The chart compares spending on advertising in four types of media in 2010 and 2020. A passive clause becomes a noun phrase; media → types of media
18 The graph shows how unemployment rates changed in two countries between 1995 and 2015. The graph compares trends in unemployment in two countries over the twenty years from 1995 to 2015. Rates changed → trends in; the period is restated. (Unemployment keeps the measure.)
19 The diagram shows how electricity is generated in a wind turbine. The diagram explains the process by which a wind turbine produces electricity. Passive → active (is generated → produces); how → the process by which
20 The map shows the proposed development of a seaside town. The map shows plans to develop a seaside town. A noun (development) → a verb (to develop); proposed → plans
21 The bar chart gives information about the main reasons why students choose to study abroad. The bar chart presents the principal reasons that lead students to study overseas. Main → principal; abroad → overseas; choose to → that lead … to
22 The table shows the percentage of the population in five countries who were aged over 65 in 2000 and 2020. The table compares the proportion of people over 65 in the populations of five countries in 2000 and 2020. Percentage → proportion; the clause who were aged over 65 → over 65; years are kept
23 The graphs show changes in the number of male and female students at one university from 2000 to 2020. The graphs illustrate how the numbers of men and women studying at one university changed over two decades. Changes in → how … changed; male and female students → men and women studying; from 2000 to 2020 → over two decades
24 The bar chart shows the average number of hours of free time people have each week in six countries, and the table shows how much they spend on leisure. The bar chart compares weekly leisure time in six countries, while the table gives the amount of money spent on leisure activities. Two visuals are joined with while; hours of free time each week → weekly leisure time; how much they spend → the amount of money spent, active to passive

12.4 A paraphrase checklist

Before you move to the overview, check your introduction:

  • Does it say the type of visual and what it measures?
  • Have you changed at least two kinds of thing (words and structure)?
  • Are the numbers, years and names exactly the same as in the task?
  • Could a reader who has not seen the task tell what the visual is about?
  • Is it one or two sentences, with no figures or opinions?

12.5 Three mistakes to avoid

Mistake Example Fix
Changing the meaning The chart shows the percentage of tourists → The chart shows the number of tourists Keep the measure the same
Copying the task The graph shows the percentage of households with internet access in four countries between 2000 and 2020. Change the verb, the noun forms and the time phrase
Over-paraphrasing The depiction illustrates the proportionate quantity of dwellings possessing digital connectivity. Use ordinary words that are accurate: proportion, homes, internet access
