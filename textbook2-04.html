Module 4: Essay and paragraph structure

A clear structure helps three of the four criteria at once. It shows the examiner your position (Task Response), it makes your argument easy to follow (Coherence and Cohesion), and it gives you a pattern to fill with language (Lexical Resource and Grammar). This module teaches the four-paragraph essay and the job of every sentence in it.

A. The four-paragraph essay

No official rule says how many paragraphs an essay must have, but paragraphs are expected, and each one should have a single clear topic. The four-paragraph essay is the safest plan for 40 minutes.

Paragraph Its job Words Time
1. Introduction Introduce the topic in your own words. Give your position or outline the essay 40–55 4 min
2. Body 1 One main idea: explain it, give an example, show the result 80–100 8 min
3. Body 2 A second main idea, built in the same way 80–100 8 min
4. Conclusion Restate your position. Summarise the main reasons 30–45 4 min

When to use five paragraphs. A five-paragraph essay (introduction, three body paragraphs, conclusion) is sometimes useful. In a discussion essay you may give each view a paragraph and write your opinion in a third. In a problem and solution essay you may want separate paragraphs for problems, solutions and one extra point. But make each body paragraph at least 70 words. Four strong paragraphs are better than five thin ones.

B. The introduction

The introduction has two or three sentences. Together they should take about 50 words.

  1. Sentence 1: the topic in your own words. Paraphrase the statement in the question (Module 2, part E). Do not copy it.
  2. Sentence 2: the view or the problem. Say what people think or what the issue is.
  3. Sentence 3: your position or an outline. Say what you believe, or say what the essay will discuss.

The last sentence changes with the question type:

Type Last sentence of the introduction
Opinion I strongly agree with this view because... / I partly disagree, because...
Discussion This essay will discuss both views before explaining why I believe that...
Problem and solution This essay will explain the main problems and suggest some practical solutions.
Advantages and disadvantages This essay will examine the main benefits and drawbacks of... and argue that...
Two-part question There are several reasons for this trend, and I believe it is mainly a positive/negative development.

Weak and strong introductions

Weak Why Stronger
Nowadays, in the modern world, homework is a very big topic. A memorised opening with no content. Very big topic is vague Homework for young children is a subject on which parents and teachers often disagree.
Some people think that primary school pupils should not be given homework. I will write about this. Copies the question. No position Some people argue that primary school pupils should not be given any homework at all. I partly disagree with this view.
In my essay I will talk about homework and the good and bad things and what I think about it and my family. Long, unclear and personal This essay will examine the benefits of homework before explaining why short tasks are the best solution.

Introduction mistakes to avoid: copying the question; writing more than 60 words; giving examples or reasons (save them for the body); using memorised openings such as Nowadays, in the modern world.

C. Body paragraphs

Each body paragraph has one main idea and four steps. These match the IEER pattern from Module 3.

Step Job Frames
1. Topic sentence States the main idea of the paragraph The main reason for... is that... / Another important point is that...
2. Explanation Says why the idea is true This is because... / In other words...
3. Example Proves it with a case For instance... / A good example is...
4. Result Links back to the question As a result... / For this reason... / This shows that...

The topic sentence. It is the most important sentence in the paragraph. A reader who reads only topic sentences should understand your argument. A good topic sentence:

  • names the main idea, not just the topic (Homework helps children practise, not Homework)
  • is a full statement that you can then explain
  • connects to your position

Paragraph unity. Every sentence in the paragraph must support the topic sentence. If one idea has two separate examples, keep both in the same paragraph. If you have two different ideas, they need two paragraphs.

The one-idea test. Write the topic of each paragraph in five words. If you cannot, the paragraph has two ideas or none.

Where does a counter-argument go? In an opinion essay, you may add a counter-argument to show you have considered the other side. Put it in the second body paragraph, introduced with Admittedly, Although some people argue that... or It is true that..., and then answer it. Keep the counter-argument short (one or two sentences).

Length. A body paragraph of 80 to 100 words has four to six sentences. If your paragraph has two sentences, the idea is not developed. If it has eight, you may have two ideas.

D. The conclusion

The conclusion is short (30 to 45 words). It does three things:

  1. Restates your position in new words (In conclusion, I believe that...).
  2. Summarises the main reasons (...because... and...).
  3. Stops. Do not add new ideas, new examples or questions.
Do Do not
Use In conclusion, To sum up, Overall Write In my opinion three times
Restate your answer to the question Introduce a new argument
Use different words from the introduction Copy the introduction
Match your position in the introduction Change your mind

E. The structure in action

This essay answers an opinion question. Read it, then study the sentence-by-sentence map below it.

Some people think that primary school children should not be given homework. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Model answer · 272 words

Homework for young children is a subject on which parents and teachers often disagree. Some people argue that primary school pupils should not be given any homework at all. I partly disagree with this view, because short tasks at home help children to learn, although too much homework can do real harm.

The main reason for keeping homework is that it allows children to practise what they have learned. Lessons are short, and most pupils need to repeat a new skill many times before they remember it. A child who reads for fifteen minutes every evening, for example, will improve his vocabulary much faster than a child who reads only in lessons. Short mathematics exercises work in the same way. As a result, children who practise at home also develop study habits that will be valuable when they move to secondary school.

However, I accept that large amounts of homework are harmful at this age. Young children need time to play, to rest and to be with their families, and long tasks can leave them tired and unhappy. In some schools, for instance, pupils of eight or nine are given more than an hour of homework every night, and many of them begin to dislike school. Parents who work long hours may also be unable to help, so children from busier families can fall behind. For this reason, teachers should set short, interesting tasks instead of long written exercises.

In conclusion, primary school children should receive homework, but only a small amount. Short tasks improve learning and build good study habits, whereas long tasks damage children's health and their interest in education.

F. Try it yourself

Exercise 1. Name the job of each sentence in this body paragraph: topic sentence, explanation, example or result.

(1) One advantage of cycling to work is that it improves health. (2) Regular exercise strengthens the heart and helps people to control their weight. (3) Office workers, for example, spend most of the day sitting, so a daily ride gives them exercise they would otherwise miss. (4) As a result, they feel more energetic and take fewer days off sick.

Show the answer key

(1) Topic sentence. (2) Explanation. (3) Example. (4) Result.

Exercise 2. Rewrite each weak topic sentence so that it states a clear main idea.

  1. Another thing is the environment.
  2. Many people use phones.
  3. I will talk about the cost.
Show the answer key

Answers vary. Models:

  1. Cycling also benefits the environment because it produces no exhaust gases.
  2. Smartphones have changed the way young people communicate with their friends.
  3. A second reason is that public transport is much cheaper than owning a car.

Exercise 3. Put these sentences in the right order to make a paragraph.

  • (a) As a result, students in remote areas can follow the same courses as students in large cities.
  • (b) For example, a student in a small village can watch lectures from a leading university without travelling.
  • (c) Online learning gives people in remote areas access to good education.
  • (d) Many villages have no university nearby, and travelling to a city is expensive.
Show the answer key

c, d, b, a. (c is the topic sentence, d explains the problem, b gives the example, a states the result.)

Exercise 4. Write an introduction (two or three sentences, about 45 to 55 words) for each question.

  1. Some people think that cities should ban cars from the centre. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  2. Some people prefer to work for a large company, while others prefer a small business. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
  3. Many people now shop online. What are the advantages and disadvantages?
Show the answer key

Your wording will differ. Models:

  1. Traffic in city centres is a growing concern for many governments, and some people believe that private cars should be banned from these areas. I agree with this view to a large extent, because a car-free centre would reduce pollution and make streets safer.
  2. Choosing between a large organisation and a small business is a difficult decision for many graduates. This essay will discuss the benefits of both types of employer before explaining why I believe that a small company is the better choice for young people.
  3. Buying goods on the internet has become routine for millions of consumers. This essay will examine the main benefits of online shopping and the problems it can cause.
