Module 13: Developing weak ideas and fixing weak arguments

Most essays that score Band 5.5 or 6 have the right structure and the right topic. What they lack is development. The official descriptors say that at Band 6 some ideas are "not sufficiently developed", and that at Band 7 main ideas are extended and supported, although support may be general or lose focus. This module teaches you to diagnose a weak idea and to repair it, so that your paragraphs move from Band 6 towards Band 7.

A. Seven kinds of weak idea

Before you can repair an idea, you need to know what is wrong with it.

# Weakness Example What is missing
1 Too general Technology is good for everyone. Who? How? In what way?
2 Unexplained Exercise is important. Why is it important?
3 Unsupported Public transport reduces pollution. This is a benefit. An example or specific detail
4 Off the focus (Question: free museums.) Museums contain many interesting objects. A link to the question
5 Repeated Free museums are good. Museums that are free are very good for people. A new point, not the same one again
6 Illogical Cars are fast, so they are bad for the environment. A reason that proves the claim
7 Over-claimed Everyone hates homework. A limit (many, some, most) and a reason

B. Five repair tools

Tool 1: Ask three questions. After any idea, ask Why? So what? For example? Each answer becomes a sentence (Module 3).

Tool 2: Narrow the claim. Add who, where, when or how often. Use careful words: many, most, often, can, tend to.

Tool 3: Add the missing link. A reason is only useful if the reader can see why it proves the claim. Add a bridge sentence: This matters because..., In other words....

Tool 4: Replace a vague example with a concrete one. In some places becomes In cities with free museums, school groups can visit several times a year.

Tool 5: Answer the sceptic. Imagine a reader who disagrees. State their best point in one sentence and answer it. This adds depth (It is true that..., but...).

Before and after

Weak idea Repaired idea
Technology is good for everyone. Technology can help older people to stay in touch with their families, because video calls make it easy to see grandchildren who live far away.
Exercise is important. Regular exercise is important because it strengthens the heart and reduces the risk of diseases such as diabetes, so people who exercise tend to need less medical treatment.
Public transport reduces pollution. Public transport reduces pollution because one bus can carry as many passengers as many cars, so fewer exhaust gases are produced in the city centre.
Homework is bad for children. Large amounts of homework can harm young children, because they need time to rest and play. In some schools, pupils of nine receive more than an hour each night, and many become tired and lose interest in school.
Everyone uses the internet. Most young people in cities use the internet every day, and many older people now use it to shop and to contact family.
Governments should make laws. Governments should make laws that limit the sale of cigarettes to young people, because smoking that begins in the teenage years is hard to stop.
Pollution is a big problem. Pollution from traffic is a serious problem in large cities, because it causes breathing difficulties, particularly for children and the elderly.
Young people do not read. Many young people read fewer books than previous generations did, because they spend more of their free time on screens.
Tourism is good for the economy. Tourism creates jobs in hotels, restaurants and transport, so it can bring income to regions that have few other industries.
Fast food is unhealthy. Fast food is often high in fat, sugar and salt, so eating it every day can lead to weight gain and heart problems.

C. Testing an argument

A developed idea can still be weak if the logic is poor. Use two tests.

The "So what?" test. Read your reason and ask So what? If the answer is not in your paragraph, add it.

The sceptic test. Imagine a reader who disagrees with you. Would they accept your reason? If not, what would they say? Answer it.

Five common logical errors

Error Example Fix
False cause Students who use computers get lower marks, so computers cause poor results. Some students who spend long hours on computers may study less, which can lower their marks.
Generalising from one case My uncle worked from home and was lonely, so remote work is bad. Some people who work from home feel isolated, because they have less contact with colleagues.
Circular reasoning Exams are bad because they are not good. Exams can be harmful because they create stress and may not test practical skills.
Irrelevant reason Cars are bad because they are expensive. (The question was about pollution.) Cars are harmful to the environment because they produce exhaust gases.
False either/or Either we ban cars, or the cities will die. Cities can reduce pollution by limiting cars in the centre and improving public transport.

D. Choosing and using examples

A good example is relevant, specific enough, true and short. A weak example breaks one of these rules.

Weak example Why it is weak Better example
For example, my friend lives in a big house. Not related to the idea For example, a family living in a small flat may have no space for a child to study.
For example, 85% of people use phones. Invented statistic. The examiner cannot trust it, and you cannot defend it For example, many teenagers check their phones within minutes of waking up.
For example, when I was seven I had a dog called Max who ate my shoes and then... Too long and too personal. It does not prove the point When I was at school, my classmates who read every night had larger vocabularies.
For example, in some places this happens. Too vague For example, in cities with good metro systems, air quality is usually better in the centre.

One example is enough. A body paragraph with three examples and no explanation is a list, not an argument. Choose the strongest example and explain why it proves the point.

E. Rescuing a weak paragraph

Take this paragraph from a student essay on the question Should museums be free? (37 words).

Weak: Museums should be free because it is good for people. Many people like museums. Free museums are good. Everyone can go and it is not expensive. Also it is good for tourists. So museums should be free.

Step 1: diagnose.

Sentence Problem
Museums should be free because it is good for people. Too general: why is it good? Also a grammar error (it is should be free entry is)
Many people like museums. Off the focus and unexplained
Free museums are good. Repeats the first sentence
Everyone can go and it is not expensive. Two weak ideas, no example
Also it is good for tourists. A new idea with no support
So museums should be free. Repeats the topic sentence with no development

Step 2: repair (62 words). Keep one main idea, explain it, give an example, and show the result.

Museums should be free because free entry gives more people the chance to learn. Families on low incomes often cannot afford tickets for several people, so their children may never see real objects from history. In cities where museums are free, for example, school groups can visit several times a year. As a result, more young people develop an interest in culture.

Step 3: polish (83 words). Improve the topic sentence, the linking and the vocabulary, and add a limit.

The main reason why museums should be free is that free entry gives more people the chance to learn. Families on low incomes often cannot afford tickets for several people, so their children may never see real objects from history. In cities where museums are free, for instance, school groups can visit several times a year, and many pupils develop a lasting interest in culture as a result. Although the government loses ticket income, donations and taxes can cover most of the cost.

What changed: one idea instead of four; a reason (cannot afford tickets), an example (school groups), and a result (lasting interest); a counter-argument (Although the government loses ticket income...) that is answered. The language is more precise (free entry, real objects, lasting interest, cover the cost).

F. Try it yourself

Exercise 1: name the weakness (use the seven types from part A).

  1. Education is very important in life.
  2. Public libraries are useful. Libraries that the public can use are very helpful.
  3. All teenagers are addicted to their phones.
  4. (Question: Should cars be banned from city centres?) Cities have many beautiful old buildings.
  5. Many people lose their jobs because of automation. This is a problem.
  6. Tall buildings are bad because they are tall.
Show the answer key
  1. Too general (or unexplained): why is it important, and for whom?
  2. Repeated: the second sentence says the same thing in different words.
  3. Over-claimed: all is too strong.
  4. Off the focus: the sentence does not say how buildings relate to banning cars.
  5. Unsupported: it needs an example and an explanation of why it is a problem.
  6. Illogical (circular): it gives no reason.

Exercise 2: develop each idea into three or four sentences (reason, explanation, example, result).

  1. Learning a foreign language is useful.
  2. Parks are important in cities.
  3. Online learning has become popular.
  4. Rising prices affect families.
Show the answer key

Answers vary. Models:

  1. Learning a foreign language is useful because it opens up more job opportunities. Many employers need staff who can communicate with customers or partners abroad, so bilingual applicants are often preferred. A graduate who speaks good English, for instance, can apply to international companies. As a result, he has a wider choice of careers and may earn a higher salary.
  2. Parks are important in cities because they give residents a place to relax and exercise. Without green spaces, people who live in crowded flats may have nowhere to walk or play. In many cities, for example, families gather in parks at weekends. As a result, people feel less stressed and the community becomes stronger.
  3. Online learning has become popular because it is flexible and cheaper than attending a college. Students can watch lessons at any time and study from home, so they save money on travel and housing. A parent with young children, for example, can complete a course in the evenings. As a result, more adults are able to gain qualifications.
  4. Rising prices put pressure on families, particularly those with low incomes. When the cost of food and housing increases faster than wages, parents may have to cut spending on other things. A family may stop buying fresh fruit, for instance, and choose cheaper, less healthy food. As a result, rising prices can affect children's health as well as the family budget.

Exercise 3: find the logical error (false cause, one-case generalisation, circular reasoning, irrelevant reason, false either/or).

  1. My neighbour lost his job after buying a computer, so computers cause unemployment.
  2. Reading is good because it is a good habit.
  3. Either we stop all tourism, or the country's culture will be destroyed.
  4. Public transport should be free because buses are often yellow.
  5. I once got a bad meal at a restaurant, so all restaurants are dirty.
Show the answer key

1. False cause. 2. Circular reasoning. 3. False either/or. 4. Irrelevant reason. 5. Generalising from one case.

Exercise 4: rescue this paragraph (27 words). Use the three steps in part E. The question is: Should children be allowed to use mobile phones at school?

Children should not use phones at school. Phones are bad. Children play games. It is not good for lessons. Teachers are angry. So phones should be banned.

Show the answer key

One possible repair:

Children should not be allowed to use mobile phones at school because phones distract pupils from their lessons. A pupil who checks messages or plays games during a lesson, for example, misses the explanation and may fall behind. Teachers also lose time asking pupils to put their phones away. As a result, the whole class learns less. Although phones can be useful in emergencies, schools can allow pupils to leave them in a locker and use them at break time.
