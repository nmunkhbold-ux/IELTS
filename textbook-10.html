10.1 Trends: verbs for change

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
rise Go up. Intransitive: it never has an object. Past: rose, risen The figure rose from 12% to 20%. The figure was risen. / They rose the price. (use increased with an object)
increase Go up. Can be a verb or a noun, and can take an object Sales increased by 15%. Increase to when you mean by (by = the amount; to = the end point)
grow Go up steadily, usually over a long period The population grew steadily. Using it for a short, sudden change
climb Rise gradually or over a long distance Attendance climbed to 9,000. Using it for a falling figure
jump, surge, soar Rise very quickly and by a lot Exports soared to $9 billion in 2015. Using them for small changes
fall Go down. Intransitive. Past: fell, fallen Prices fell from $8 to $5. falled; fell down
decrease Go down. Verb or noun The number of students decreased by 200. The amount of students (use the number of with countable nouns)
decline Go down steadily, often over a long time Coal use declined throughout the period. Using it for a sudden fall
drop Fall, often a clear and noticeable fall Unemployment dropped to 4%. Drop down is redundant
plunge, plummet, slump Fall very sharply Profits plummeted in 2009. Using them for a gentle fall
dip Fall slightly for a short time The figure dipped to 9% before recovering. Using it for a long, large fall
recover, rebound Rise again after a fall Sales recovered to 40,000 in 2012. Using it when there was no earlier fall
fluctuate Go up and down repeatedly Prices fluctuated between $2 and $4. Using it for one rise and one fall
remain stable, stay constant Not change The rate remained stable at 6%. Remained stable from 6% (use at)
level off, plateau Stop changing after a rise or a fall The figure levelled off at 30%. Using it for a figure that never changed

Verbs and the nouns that match them.

Verb phrase Noun phrase
Sales rose sharply. There was a sharp rise in sales.
Prices fell gradually. There was a gradual fall in prices.
Numbers fluctuated. There were fluctuations in numbers.
The figure peaked at 40%. The figure reached a peak of 40%.
Output levelled off. There was a levelling off in output.
Sales recovered. There was a recovery in sales.

A common mistake is a rise of when you mean in: use a rise in sales (what changed) and a rise of 20% (how much).

10.2 Peaks, low points and extremes

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
peak at Reach the highest point Visitor numbers peaked at 102,000 in August. Peaked in 102,000 (use at for the figure, in for the date)
reach a peak of The same, with a noun The rate reached a peak of 9% in 2010. Reached to a peak
reach a low of Reach the lowest point Prices reached a low of $3 in 2016. A lowest point of
bottom out Stop falling and reach the lowest point Sales bottomed out at 3,000 in March. Using it for a flat line
the highest, the lowest For rates, levels and prices The lowest rate was 4%. The most high
the largest, the greatest For numbers and amounts The largest number of visitors came in August. The most big
the first, the last Start and end of the period The last figure was 28%. The final one when the noun is needed

10.3 Degree and speed

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
sharp, sharply A big, fast change There was a sharp fall in prices. Using the adjective as an adverb: fell sharp
dramatic, dramatically A very big change Sales rose dramatically. Using it for a change of one or two points
rapid, rapidly Fast The population grew rapidly. Using it for a big but slow change
significant, considerable, substantial A big change, in formal language A considerable increase in sales. Considerable as an adverb (considerably is the adverb)
steady, steadily At a constant rate The figure rose steadily. Using it for an up-and-down pattern
gradual, gradually Slowly and continuously A gradual decline. Using it for a short period
moderate, moderately A medium change A moderate rise. Using it as a synonym for small
slight, slightly, marginal, marginally A small change The figure dipped slightly. Slightly for a large change

Approximations and quantifiers.

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
approximately, roughly, around, about Close to a figure Approximately 40% of students Using them before an exact figure you can read
just over, just under A little more or less Just under half Just than
nearly, almost A little below Nearly 90% Using them for a figure above the number
a quarter, a third, a fifth, three-quarters Simple fractions A quarter of households A fourth (US usage; a quarter is standard in IELTS)
one in five The same as 20% One in five students One of five
the majority, a minority More than half, less than half The majority of respondents agreed. The majority of people was (use were)

10.4 Comparisons

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
higher than, lower than Compare two figures Rent in A was higher than in B. More higher than
more … than, less … than Compare with adjectives and nouns Women were more likely to swim than men. More likely to swim that men
as … as Equal comparison A was as expensive as B. As expensive than
twice as … as, three times as … as A ratio Gas cost twice as much as coal. Twice higher than (unclear: use twice as high as)
the same as, similar to Equal or alike The figure for A was similar to that for B. Similar with, same with
compared with, in comparison with Set two things side by side Compared with 2000, the figure for 2020 was higher. Compared than
the highest, the most popular, the least popular Superlatives Tea was the most popular drink. Forgetting the
rank, ranked second, came third Position in an order Tea ranked first, followed by coffee. Ranked in second
topped the list, led, trailed Be first, or behind Coffee led the way; juice trailed. Topped the list in
followed by The next in an order Walking, followed by cycling Followed with
overtake, surpass, exceed Pass another Gas overtook coal in 2010. Overtake than

10.5 Proportions and percentages

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
account for Form a proportion of a whole Coal accounted for 45% of output. Accounted of
make up Form a proportion (less formal) Paper made up 18% of the waste. Using it for amounts that are not parts of a whole
the proportion, the percentage, the share of A proportion of a total The share of coal fell. The percent of (use percentage)
percentage points The difference between two percentages It rose by five percentage points. Rose by 5% when the figures are already percentages
the remainder, the rest What is left The remainder came from gas. The rest part
doubled, tripled, quadrupled, halved Exact multiples Social media use quadrupled. Doubled up, halved down
fourfold, fivefold Multiplied by four or five A fivefold increase Fivefolds
respectively Pair items in order 40% and 35% respectively Using it when the order is not clear

10.6 Contrast, concession and linking

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
whereas Contrast inside a sentence Tablets doubled, whereas laptops fell. Writing it at the start of a one-clause sentence
while Contrast, or two things at the same time While sales rose, profits fell. Using it when whereas would avoid confusion with the time meaning
in contrast, by contrast Contrast with the previous sentence By contrast, sport hardly changed. In the contrary
however Contrast, with commas Sales rose. However, profits fell. Joining two sentences with however and a comma only
although, even though Concession, with a clause Although sales fell, profits rose. Although … but … in the same sentence
despite, in spite of Concession, with a noun or -ing Despite a fall in July, sales rose. Despite of
overall Marks the overview Overall, prices rose. In conclusion for an overview
therefore, consequently A result that follows from the data Four stages therefore prepare the paste. Using it when the cause is not shown

10.7 Time and measurement

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
from … to … Start and end points Sales rose from 5 to 9 million. From 5 until 9
between … and … A period or range Between 2000 and 2010 Between 2000 to 2010
over the period, throughout the period The whole time span Over the period, prices doubled. In the period
at the beginning of, at the end of the period The start or end At the end of the period, rent was $1,800. In the begin
by + date No later than that date By 2010, the figure had reached 20%. The plain past with by for a completed change (the past perfect is more natural)
until, up to Continue to a point Stable until 2005 Until to
since, for Present perfect time words Sales have grown since 2000. Since with the past simple
in the following decade, over the next five years A later period In the following decade, sales fell. In the next years
the figure for 2010 The data for one year The figure for 2010 was 20%. The figure of 2010

10.8 Maps and plans: location, direction, construction and demolition

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
in the north-west of Inside the area, in that part A farm stood in the north-west of the village. At the north-west of
to the north of Outside, in that direction A hotel stands to the north of the park. In the north of when it is outside
next to, beside, adjacent to Very close A car park lies next to the shop. Next of
opposite, between … and … Facing, or in the middle The library is opposite the school. In front of when the buildings face each other
along, at the end of Following a line, or at its end A path runs along the river. Along of the river
on the site of Where something used to be A supermarket was built on the site of the farm. On the place of
built, constructed Make a new building A bypass was constructed. Using constructed for small things
demolished, knocked down, cleared Remove a building or land cover The shop was demolished. Destroyed (it suggests accident or war)
replaced by, converted into Something changes use The office was converted into a meeting room. Replaced to (use replaced by)
extended, expanded, enlarged Make bigger The school was extended. Extended for a new separate building
renovated, relocated Repaired or modernised; moved elsewhere The hall was renovated. Renovated for a new building

10.9 Processes and cycles

Word or phrase Meaning and when to use it Example Common mistake
first, initially, in the first stage The start In the first stage, olives are picked. Firstly, secondly, thirdly for every stage
next, then, after that, subsequently The following step Next, the paste is pressed. Then after that together
once, after, before, while Link two steps in one sentence They are washed before being crushed. Before to crush
meanwhile, at the same time Two things at once Meanwhile, the oil is filtered. Using it when the steps are in order
finally, in the final stage, eventually The last step Finally, the oil is bottled. At last for a process
turned into, converted into, develops into Change of form The eggs hatch into tadpoles. Change to for a change of form
to + verb, so that Purpose Washed to remove dirt For remove dirt
the cycle repeats, the cycle begins again A cycle closes Then the cycle begins again. The end in a cycle
is / are + past participle The passive voice The oil is filtered. Is filter, is filtering
