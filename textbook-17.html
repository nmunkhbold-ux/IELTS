17.1 The one-page Task 1 strategy

Step What to do Time
1. Identify Read the task and name the visual (Module 1.3). Decide: static or dynamic, or a process, map or plan 30 seconds
2. Look Use the five looks: labels, time, extremes, patterns, groups. Mark the visual with a pencil 2 to 3 minutes
3. Plan Choose the two or three overview features. Group the details into two body paragraphs 2 minutes
4. Write Introduction (paraphrase), overview, body 1, body 2. Use your own words and the right tense 12 to 13 minutes
5. Check Figures, units, tenses, verb endings, at least 150 words 2 to 3 minutes

Always: paraphrase the task; write a separate overview; group instead of listing; compare; check every figure.

Never: copy the task; give opinions or reasons; write in conclusion; describe every number; guess a figure.

17.2 The universal 20-minute plan

Minutes Do Result
0–3 Five looks A marked-up visual
3–5 Group the features and choose the overview points A two-line plan
5–8 Write the introduction and the overview About 70 to 90 words
8–17 Write the two body paragraphs About 100 to 130 words
17–20 Check figures, tenses, plurals and word count The final answer

For a task with two visuals, take about four minutes for the first step and aim for 180 to 200 words.

17.3 The checklist to use before you submit

  • The introduction paraphrases the task, with the numbers and names unchanged.
  • There is a separate overview with the two or three main features and almost no figures.
  • The body paragraphs are grouped by idea, not by item.
  • Every figure has been checked against the visual, with its unit.
  • There is at least one ratio, ranking or gap, and one exception or contrast.
  • The tense is right for the dates, with no changes inside a sentence.
  • Subjects and verbs agree, especially with the number of and the percentage of.
  • At least five different verbs or phrases for change or comparison are used.
  • There are no opinions, reasons or conclusion.
  • The answer has at least 150 words.

17.6 Grammar reference sheet

Structure Form Example
Past simple verb + -ed (or irregular) Sales rose in 2010.
Present simple verb (+ s) The oil is filtered.
Present perfect have/has + past participle Sales have risen since 2000.
Future will / is expected to + verb Prices are expected to level off.
Passive be + past participle The shop was demolished.
Comparative adjective + -er / more + adjective + than Rent was higher than in Stonebridge.
Superlative the + adjective + -est / the most + adjective Tea was the most popular drink.
Relative clause noun + which / who / where + clause … 28% in 2020, which made it the leading city.
Participle clause -ing / -ed phrase Eastfield peaked at 27% before falling to 21%.
Concession although + clause / despite + noun Despite a fall in July, sales rose.
Contrast clause, whereas clause Tablets doubled, whereas laptops fell.
Agreement the number of … is, a number of … are The number of visitors was 102,000.

17.7 Useful sentence structures

These are builders with a slot for your own content. Choose the one that fits what you see, and never copy a whole answer.

Purpose Pattern Example
Introduce a chart The [chart type] [compares / illustrates] [what] in [where] [when]. The bar chart compares weekly study time among students in six countries.
Overview of a trend Overall, [item] [verb of change], whereas [item] [verb of change]. Overall, cycling rose in Northbury, whereas it declined in Southport.
Overview of a ranking [Item] was the [superlative], while [item] was the [superlative]. Business was the most popular course, while Music was the least popular.
Describe a change [Item] [verb] from [A] to [B] [period]. Sales rose from 5 to 9 million between 2000 and 2010.
Describe a peak [Item] peaked at [figure] in [time] before [-ing]. Visitors peaked at 102,000 in August before falling.
Compare two figures [A] was [ratio / comparative] [B] ([A] against [B]). Coal was nearly twice as high as gas (45% against 25%).
Group two items [A] and [B] [verb] [figure] and [figure] respectively. Metal and plastic accounted for 80 and 70 tonnes respectively.
A crossing [A] overtook [B] between [year] and [year]. Northbury overtook Southport between 2005 and 2010.
A process step [Thing] is [past participle] [purpose / next step]. The paste is pressed so that the oil separates.
A map change The [old feature] was [change verb] [new feature]. The farm was replaced by a housing estate.
A plan (future) According to the plan, [feature] will be [change verb]. According to the plan, the car park will be turned into a public square.
A loop …, so the cycle [starts / begins] again. … a pump sends it back to the roof panel, so the cycle starts again.
A decision If [condition], [result]. Otherwise, [other result]. If the item is faulty, a full refund is paid. Otherwise, …

17.8 The final mock test

The mock test has four tasks: a line graph, a table, a process and a map. Each takes 20 minutes and needs at least 150 words. You can sit all four in one session (80 minutes) or take one at a time. Write your answers by hand or on a keyboard, and do not look at the key features until you have finished.

Task 1: line graph

The line graph below shows the number of passengers (in millions) using three airports between 2000 and 2020.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words. (About 20 minutes)

Task 2: table

The table below shows the percentage of adults in one country who read different types of material at least once a week in 2010 and 2024.

17.9 Marking guide for the mock test

Compare your answers with the key features below. A strong answer includes all of the features in its row, in its own words, with accurate figures.

Task Key features A strong overview might say
1. Airports Airport B rose almost fourfold (8 to 30 million) and had the highest figure in 2020; Airport C fell steadily and halved (20 to 10); Airport A rose to a peak of 27 in 2015 and then fell to 18; A and B both overtook C between 2005 and 2010; B overtook A between 2015 and 2020; the ranking in 2020 was the reverse of the ranking in 2000 Overall, passenger numbers rose at Airports A and B but fell steadily at Airport C. Airport B, the quietest in 2000, had become the busiest by 2020, while Airport C went from the busiest to the quietest.
2. Reading Printed material fell (newspapers by 35 points, magazines by 18, books by 11); online reading rose (social media posts by 40 points, websites and blogs by 27); newspapers were the most widely read in 2010 (63%) and social media posts the most widely read in 2024 (88%); magazines had the lowest figure in 2024 (22%) Overall, reading of printed material fell while online reading rose sharply. Newspapers, the most widely read material in 2010, fell by more than half, whereas social media posts became the most widely read in 2024.
3. Bricks Six stages; the start (clay dug from a quarry) and the end (delivery); the clay is crushed, mixed and shaped (stages 1 to 3); the bricks are dried and baked at a very high temperature in a kiln (4 and 5); they are cooled and delivered by lorry (6) The process consists of six stages, beginning with the digging of clay and ending with the delivery of the finished bricks. The clay is first crushed, mixed and shaped, and the bricks are then dried and baked.
4. Resort Part of the forest was replaced by villas; the hotel was extended; a café was added on the beach; the jetty was replaced by a harbour; the beach and the sea were unchanged The resort became much more developed: part of the forest was replaced by villas, the hotel was extended, and a café and a harbour were added. The beach remained unchanged.

Scoring. For each task, count the true statements in the Band 7 self-assessment checklist in section 16.6 (17 statements). This gives a teaching estimate only.

True statements Teaching estimate
15 to 17 Around Band 7 or above
11 to 14 Band 6 to 6.5
7 to 10 Band 5 to 5.5
Fewer than 7 Below Band 5

17.10 After the mock test

  1. Check each answer against the key features and mark what you missed.
  2. Record your mistakes in the error log (Module 14.12).
  3. Choose the weakest criterion and follow the matching plan in Module 16.4 and 16.7.
  4. Rewrite your weakest answer, then compare the two versions.
  5. Sit one new task a week under test conditions, and track your checklist score.
