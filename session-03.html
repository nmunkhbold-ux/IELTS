Part B. Main ideas and topic sentences

Learn to see the map of a passage.

3

The map of a passage

Every academic passage has three levels. Learn them, and every question becomes easier to locate.

1
Main idea: the big message of the whole passage. Usually in the first or last paragraph, often matching the title.
2
Topic sentences: the main idea of each paragraph. Usually the first sentence, but not always.
3
Supporting details: facts, numbers, names, examples and reasons that explain the topic sentence.
IELTS uses the levels differently: matching headings and summary questions test levels 1 and 2. True/False/Not Given, sentence completion and short answers test level 3.
4

Find the topic sentence

The topic sentence gives the main idea of a paragraph. The other sentences give details and examples. It is usually the first sentence, but not always. Click the topic sentence in each paragraph.

Tip: if the first sentence is a question, a story or a surprising fact, the topic sentence is often the second one. Sometimes it comes last, as a conclusion.

Part C. Skimming and scanning

Read fast for the main idea; jump to the details you need.

8

Skimming or scanning?

Skimming

Reading quickly for the main idea. Read the title, the first sentence of each paragraph and words that stand out.

Like reading newspaper headlines.

Scanning

Searching quickly for a specific detail: a name, a number, a date. Your eyes jump; you do not read.

Like finding a friend’s name in your phone contacts.

Which one do you use?

The Changing Face of Education

AEducation today is vastly different from what it was just a few decades ago. One of the most significant changes is the rise of technology in the classroom. Many schools have integrated computers, tablets, and online learning platforms into their teaching methods. This shift has allowed students to access a wealth of information instantly and learn at their own pace. Furthermore, online courses have made education more accessible to people around the world, regardless of their location.
BAnother important development is the growing emphasis on skills over memorization. Educators now focus more on critical thinking, creativity, and problem-solving rather than rote learning. This approach prepares students better for the challenges of the modern workforce.
CDespite these advances, traditional teaching methods still play a vital role. Face-to-face interactions, group projects, and classroom discussions foster social skills and teamwork. Many institutions are combining old and new methods to create a balanced curriculum that meets the needs of diverse learners.
DFinally, lifelong learning is becoming essential. Rapid technological and social changes mean people must continually update their skills. Many adults now return to education later in life to stay competitive in their careers. Overall, the education system is evolving to become more flexible, inclusive, and student-centered.

Green Valley University: A Hub of Innovation

AGreen Valley University, established in 1980, is one of the leading educational institutions in the country. It currently serves over 15,000 students from various backgrounds, offering more than 120 different academic programs across multiple faculties. The university campus includes modern facilities such as advanced laboratories, a large library, and several sports centers.

Part D. Summaries

Say the main ideas in your own words.

12

Writing a summary

A summary is a short version of a text in your own words. It gives the main ideas and leaves out small details and examples. Skimming and topic sentences make this easy: the topic sentences are the main ideas.

1
Read and find the main idea
Ask: what is this text mostly about?
2
Note one key point per paragraph
Use the topic sentences. Skip examples, numbers and names.
3
Write in your own words
Start with the main idea, then add the key points with linking words.
4
Check
Short? No opinions? No copied sentences?

Useful language

A model summary of Passage 1

About 75 words. Compare it with the passage: one point per paragraph.The text explains how education has changed in recent decades. Firstly, technology such as computers and online courses allows students to learn at their own pace and from anywhere. In addition, teachers now focus more on skills like critical thinking than on memorising facts. However, traditional methods such as group work are still important, so many schools combine old and new approaches. Finally, people now need to keep learning throughout their lives.
Many schools have integrated computers, tablets, and online learning platforms into their teaching methods. (copied)
Technology such as computers and online courses is now common in schools. (own words)
I think online learning is the best way to study. (opinion)
Online courses make education available to more people. (the writer’s idea)

    Multiple-choice practice: the Olympic Games

    Skim the passage first (two minutes), then answer. Read the explanation after each answer.

    The Olympic Games: From Olympia to the World

    AThe Olympic Games began in ancient Greece, traditionally in 776 BC, at Olympia in the west of the country. Athletes from rival Greek city-states competed in running, wrestling and chariot racing, and a sacred truce was declared so that competitors and spectators could travel to the games safely, even in times of war.
    BThe ancient games ended in the fourth century AD, and the idea was only revived some 1,500 years later by a French educator, Pierre de Coubertin. He believed that international sport could encourage understanding between nations. The first modern Olympics took place in Athens in 1896. They were small by today’s standards: around a dozen nations took part, and every competitor was a man. Women competed for the first time four years later, at the Paris Games of 1900.
    CThe Games grew steadily. In 1924, a separate Winter Olympics was held for the first time, in Chamonix, France, with events such as skiing and ice hockey. Like the summer Games, the winter event began modestly but attracted more countries with each edition.
    DAfter the Second World War, the Olympics became a stage for political rivalry. During the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union treated the medal table as a measure of national strength, and both led boycotts: many Western countries stayed away from Moscow in 1980, and the Soviet Union and its allies did not attend Los Angeles in 1984. At the same time, many newly independent nations in Africa and Asia used the Games to present themselves to the world for the first time.
    EToday the Olympics are the largest sporting event on earth. The 2016 Games in Rio de Janeiro welcomed more than 11,000 athletes from over 200 teams. Hosting has also spread beyond Europe and North America: Beijing in 2008 and Rio in 2016 were seen as signs of the growing confidence of emerging economies.
    FYet this success has a price. Hosting now requires huge investment in stadiums, transport and security, and several cities have withdrawn from the bidding process after local opposition. Oslo, for example, abandoned its bid for the 2022 Winter Games. In my view, unless the Games become simpler and cheaper to organise, fewer cities will be willing to host them, and the Olympic movement will lose much of its appeal.

    The Long Sleep

    AFor many animals, winter is a dangerous season, not because of the cold itself but because food becomes scarce. Some species solve this problem by hibernating: they enter a state of deep inactivity and wait until spring brings food again. Ground squirrels, woodchucks and many kinds of bat are well-known examples.
    BHibernation is very different from ordinary sleep. A hibernating animal’s body temperature falls sharply, sometimes to just a few degrees above freezing, and its heart rate and breathing slow dramatically. The change can be so extreme that the animal appears to be dead.
    CTo survive these months, hibernators prepare in autumn. They eat far more than usual and build up stores of body fat, which supply energy through the winter. Some also hide food in their burrows to eat during short periods of waking.
    DBears are the animals most people associate with hibernation, yet scientists disagree about whether they are true hibernators. A bear’s body temperature drops by only a few degrees, much less than a squirrel’s. On the other hand, some bears can go for months without eating, drinking or going to the toilet, something even true hibernators rarely manage.
    EWhat tells an animal that it is time to hibernate? Shorter days, falling temperatures and a shrinking food supply all seem to play a part, but the exact biological trigger is still not fully understood. In places where winters are mild, some animals hibernate only briefly, or not at all.
