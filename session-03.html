Part A. Learn the skills

1

Skimming or scanning?

Skimming

Reading quickly for the main idea: the title, headings, first sentences and words that stand out.

Like reading newspaper headlines.

Scanning

Searching quickly for a specific detail: a name, a number, a date. Your eyes jump; you do not read.

Like finding a friend’s name in your contacts.

Which one do you use?

2

Find the topic sentence

The topic sentence gives the main idea of a paragraph; the other sentences are details and examples. It is usually the first sentence, but not always. Click the topic sentence.

Clues: a topic sentence is general (it could be a heading). Details contain examples, numbers and names. If the first sentence is a question or a story, look at the second. Words like “Clearly” or “In short” can mark a topic sentence at the end.
3

Topic sentence to heading

A heading matches the meaning of the topic sentence, often in different words. One heading is extra.

4

The map of a passage

1
Main idea: the message of the whole passage.
2
Topic sentences: the message of each paragraph.
3
Supporting details: facts, numbers, examples and reasons.
A summary = the main idea + one topic sentence per paragraph, in your own words. Leave out the details.
