Module 10: Mixed and less common questions

Some questions do not fit one of the five standard types. They combine two types, ask three questions, or use unusual wording. The method for all of them is the same: count the main parts, give each part its own paragraph, and keep your position clear. This module teaches that method.

A. Understanding the question

Eight patterns you may meet

# Pattern and example wording Main parts Paragraph map
1 View + solution. Some people think A. Others think B. Which view do you agree with? What can be done? 1. Which view 2. What can be done Body 1: your view with reasons. Body 2: solutions
2 Opinion + solution. To what extent do you agree? What can be done about it? 1. Position 2. Solutions Body 1: position and reasons. Body 2: solutions
3 Three questions. What are the causes? What problems does it create? What can governments do? 1. Causes 2. Problems 3. Solutions Three short body paragraphs (five paragraphs in all)
4 Future. What will be the main changes in the next twenty years? Will they be positive or negative? 1. Changes 2. Judgement Body 1: two changes with reasons. Body 2: your judgement with reasons
5 Choice. Is it better for children to... or to...? Why? 1. Which is better 2. Reasons An opinion essay that compares both options
6 Responsibility. Who should be responsible for...? What can they do? 1. Who 2. What they can do Body 1: who and why. Body 2: actions
7 Ways. In what ways does... affect people? Is this a good thing? 1. Ways (effects) 2. Judgement Body 1: two ways. Body 2: judgement
8 Extent + second question. How far do you agree? Why do people think this? 1. Position 2. Reasons people hold the view Body 1: your position. Body 2: the reasons behind the view

Wording you may not expect

Wording Treat it as
What is your opinion? / Should...? / How far do you agree? An opinion essay (Module 5)
Which is more important,... or...? An opinion essay with a comparison. Compare both, then choose
In what ways...? Two or three ways, each explained
Evaluate... / Consider... A discussion of both sides, with a judgement
What can be done? / How can this be solved? The solutions part of Module 7

The rule is always the same: the more parts there are, the shorter each paragraph must be. With three parts you cannot write 100 words on each. Aim for 60 to 75 words per body paragraph, and make every sentence count.

B. Planning the answer

Use the parts map. It takes two minutes.

  1. Number the parts. Underline every question or instruction word and write 1, 2, 3 above them.
  2. Give each part a paragraph. Two parts means two body paragraphs. Three parts means three body paragraphs.
  3. Decide your position first if any part asks for one (which view, extent, positive or negative).
  4. Budget your words.
Two parts Three parts
Introduction 40–50 35–45
Each body paragraph 85–100 60–75
Conclusion 30–40 30–40
Total About 270–300 About 270–300
  1. Check that every part has an example. One example per paragraph is enough.

Parts maps for three questions

Question Parts map
Obesity is increasing in many countries. What are the causes? What are the effects on society? How can the problem be reduced? 1 causes (two: diet, inactivity) → 2 effects (health costs, lost work days) → 3 solutions (food labels, school sport). Five paragraphs
To what extent do you agree that museums should be free? What can governments do to pay for this? 1 position (agree, with reasons) → 2 solutions (taxes, donations). Four paragraphs
What will be the main changes in the way we work? Will they be mainly positive or negative? 1 changes (remote work, automation) → 2 judgement (mainly positive, with a limit). Four paragraphs

C. Writing structure

The five-paragraph template (three parts)

Paragraph Content Frames
Introduction Topic, then a list of what the essay will do This essay will explain..., describe... and suggest...
Body 1: Part 1 Main point, explanation, example The main cause of... is...
Body 2: Part 2 Link from Part 1, then the main point This growth creates serious problems. / As a result of this,...
Body 3: Part 3 Link from Part 2, then solutions Governments can reduce these problems in two main ways. / To solve this,...
Conclusion One sentence for each part, no new ideas In conclusion, people... mainly because..., but this... Careful planning would...

Links between paragraphs. In a five-paragraph essay, the reader moves quickly from topic to topic. Help them with a short link at the start of paragraphs 3 and 4 (Rapid growth creates serious problems. / Governments can reduce these problems in two main ways.). These topic sentences also tell the examiner exactly which part you are answering.

Common mistakes with mixed questions

Mistake Fix
Answering only the first and last parts Number the parts. Tick each one in your plan
Writing 100 words per paragraph in a five-paragraph essay Shorten each body paragraph to 60–75 words
Merging two parts into one paragraph One part, one paragraph
No link between parts Open each body paragraph with a sentence that connects to the last
An introduction that lists the parts in the wrong order List them in the order of the essay

D. Band 7+ model essay

In many countries, the number of people who live in cities is growing rapidly. What are the causes of this trend? What problems does it create? What can governments do?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Model answer · 275 words

Cities in many countries are growing faster than ever before, and millions of people move to them every year. This essay will explain why people make this choice, describe the problems that result, and suggest what governments can do.

The main cause of urban growth is the search for work. Factories, offices and services are concentrated in cities, whereas villages offer few jobs, so young people often leave rural areas to earn a better income. A farmer's son, for example, may move to the capital because he cannot find a stable income at home. In addition, cities offer better schools, hospitals and entertainment, which attract families as well as workers.

Rapid growth creates serious problems. Housing becomes expensive, and many newcomers have to live in crowded or unsafe areas. Roads and public transport cannot cope with the extra demand, which leads to traffic jams and air pollution. Schools and hospitals may also become so crowded that waiting times increase. At the same time, villages lose their young people, so farms and local services decline.

Governments can reduce these problems in two main ways. First, they should plan ahead by building affordable housing, cycle routes and public transport before cities become overcrowded. Second, they could encourage development outside the capital by supporting businesses, universities and hospitals in smaller towns. If people could find good jobs near their homes, fewer of them would feel the need to leave.

In conclusion, people move to cities mainly for work and services, but this movement puts pressure on housing, transport and rural areas. Careful planning and investment in smaller towns would make cities more pleasant and keep rural communities alive.

E. The model essay explained

Paragraph by paragraph

Paragraph What it does Why it works
1. Introduction (39 words) Gives the background and lists the three parts in order: explain, describe, suggest The examiner can see that all three parts will be answered
2. Body 1: causes (71 words) Main cause (work), explanation (jobs in cities, few in villages), example (a farmer's son), second cause (services) Two causes, one with an example
3. Body 2: problems (64 words) A topic sentence that links to paragraph 2 (Rapid growth creates...), then housing, transport, public services and rural decline Four problems in short sentences, each with a clear effect
4. Body 3: solutions (62 words) A signal sentence (in two main ways), then two matched solutions The solutions answer the problems (housing, transport; rural decline)
5. Conclusion (39 words) One sentence for causes and problems, one for solutions No new ideas

Do the solutions match the problems? Yes. Affordable housing, cycle routes and public transport answer the problems of housing and transport. Support for smaller towns answers rural decline, and it also reduces the causes (the search for work).

The four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the essay What would push it further
Task Response All three parts are answered, each in its own paragraph. Ideas are extended and supported, although the paragraphs are short A second example in paragraph 3 or 4
Coherence and Cohesion Five paragraphs, each with one purpose. Link sentences open paragraphs 3 and 4. Linking words: whereas, in addition, at the same time, First... Second... A wider variety of linking words
Lexical Resource urban growth, stable income, rural areas, cope with the extra demand, affordable housing, overcrowded, rural communities More synonyms for problems and cities
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Relative clauses (which attract, which leads to). Result clauses (so crowded that). Conditional (If people could find..., fewer of them would...). Passive (are concentrated). Modal verbs (may become, could encourage, should plan) More participle clauses and complex noun phrases

F. Try it yourself

Exercise 1. Make a parts map for each question: number the parts and say how many paragraphs you will write.

  1. More young people are leaving rural areas for cities. What are the causes? What can be done to encourage them to stay?
  2. Some people think that it is the government's job to protect the environment. Others think that individuals are responsible. Which view do you agree with? What can be done?
  3. Obesity is increasing in many countries. What are the causes? What are the effects on society? How can the problem be reduced?
  4. Some people believe that the disappearance of traditional languages is a great loss. To what extent do you agree? What can be done to keep them alive?
  5. What will be the main changes in the way we work over the next twenty years? Will they be mainly positive or negative?
Show the answer key
  1. Two parts: (1) causes, (2) solutions. Four paragraphs.
  2. Two parts: (1) which view, (2) what can be done. Four paragraphs. The second part may include actions for both the government and individuals.
  3. Three parts: (1) causes, (2) effects, (3) solutions. Five paragraphs.
  4. Two parts: (1) position (extent), (2) solutions. Four paragraphs.
  5. Two parts: (1) main changes, (2) judgement (positive or negative). Four paragraphs.

Exercise 2: write the essay.

Some people believe that governments should provide free healthcare for everyone, while others think that people should pay for their own treatment. Which view do you agree with? What can be done to make healthcare affordable?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

  1. Analyse and map the parts (3 minutes).
  2. Plan (5 minutes).
  3. Write (27 minutes) and check (5 minutes).
  4. Compare with the sample plan.
Show the sample plan

Your plan may differ and still be good.

  • Parts. (1) Which view do you agree with? (2) What can be done to make healthcare affordable? Four paragraphs.
  • Position. Free healthcare for everyone is the better system.
  • Body 1 (view). Illness can strike anyone, and treatment can be very expensive (example: an operation that costs more than a family earns in a year). Healthy people work and pay taxes, so everyone benefits. Counter: it costs the government a lot. Answer: preventing illness early costs less than treating it late.
  • Body 2 (solutions). Taxes on harmful products such as cigarettes and sugary drinks can raise money. Spending on prevention (vaccinations, health education) lowers future costs. Bulk purchasing of medicines can reduce prices.
  • Conclusion. Free healthcare is fairer and, with prevention and careful spending, can be affordable.

More mixed questions for practice

  1. Some people think that children learn more from their parents than from teachers. Others think teachers are more important. Which view do you agree with? What can parents and teachers do to help children succeed?
  2. In many countries, people are working longer hours. What are the causes? What effects does this have on families?
  3. Many people believe that governments should spend more on public services and less on defence. To what extent do you agree? What can governments do to find the money?
  4. Some people say that international tourism harms local cultures. Do you agree? What can be done to protect them?
  5. Is it better for young people to live with their parents until they marry, or to leave home when they start work? Why?
  6. What will be the main effects of artificial intelligence on jobs over the next twenty years? Will they be mainly positive or negative?
