Part A: Data visuals

A1. Line graph

Data. The line graph shows the average price of a litre of milk in two towns between 2015 and 2023 (in dollars).

Year Town A Town B
2015 1.10 1.40
2017 1.20 1.38
2019 1.35 1.30
2021 1.60 1.30
2023 1.85 1.52
  1. Read the data. (a) Between which two years did the two prices cross? (b) By how much, and by what percentage, did Town A's price rise between 2015 and 2023? (c) What was the lowest price in Town B?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The line graph shows changes in the price of a litre of milk in two towns between 2015 and 2023.
  3. Overview. Write two sentences that describe the main trends.
  4. Gaps. The price in Town A ___ steadily from $1.10 in 2015 ___ $1.85 in 2023. In Town B, the price ___ to a low of $1.30 in 2019, ___ at that level in 2021, and then jumped to $1.52.
  5. Correct. (a) The price in Town A rises from $1.10 in 2015 to $1.85 in 2023. (b) Town A's price was three times more than Town B's in 2023.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) Between 2017 and 2019 (Town A was lower in 2017 and higher in 2019). (b) $0.75, about 68% ($0.75 ÷ $1.10). (c) $1.30, in 2019 and 2021.
  2. The line graph illustrates how the cost of a litre of milk changed in two towns over an eight-year period.
  3. Overall, milk became more expensive in both towns, but the rise was much steeper in Town A. Town A went from the cheaper of the two towns in 2015 to the more expensive from 2019 onwards.
  4. rose (or increased) … to; fell (or dropped) … remained (or stayed). To gives the end point, and remained describes no change.
  5. (a) rose: 2015 and 2023 are past dates, so use the past simple. (b) Town A's price was about 22% higher than Town B's in 2023 (1.85 ÷ 1.52 = 1.22), so three times is wrong.

A2. Bar chart

Data. The bar chart shows the number of books borrowed from a library (in thousands) in 2015 and 2024.

Type 2015 2024
Fiction 42 30
Non-fiction 25 28
Children's 18 33
E-books 4 21
  1. Read the data. (a) Which type rose most, and which fell? (b) What was the total in each year? (c) How many times larger was e-book borrowing in 2024?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The bar chart shows the number of books, in thousands, that were borrowed from a library in 2015 and 2024, by type.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. Loans of e-books ___ more than fivefold, ___ 4,000 to 21,000. Fiction ___ the most popular category in 2015 but ___ to 30,000 by 2024.
  5. Correct. (a) Fiction decreased by 12 thousand books to 30. (b) E-books increased five times more than 2015.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) E-books rose most (+17,000); fiction was the only type that fell (−12,000). (b) 89,000 in 2015 and 112,000 in 2024. (c) 5.25 times, so more than five times.
  2. The bar chart compares how many books of four types people borrowed from a library in 2015 and 2024.
  3. Overall, borrowing shifted from fiction towards children's books and e-books. Total loans rose, e-books and children's titles grew fastest, and fiction was the only category to decline.
  4. rose (or increased) … from; was … fell. From … to gives the start and end.
  5. (a) Fiction decreased by 12,000 books to 30,000: give both figures their units. (b) E-books increased more than fivefold compared with 2015: times more than is wrong.

A3. Pie chart

Data. The pie charts show how students travelled to campus (%).

Mode 2005 2025
Car 40 22
Bus 30 28
Walking 20 25
Bicycle 10 25
  1. Read the data. (a) Check that each column adds to 100. (b) By how many percentage points did car use fall? (c) Which mode changed least?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The pie charts show how students travelled to campus in 2005 and 2025.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. Cars ___ for 40% of journeys in 2005, ___ than any other mode. The share of cyclists ___ from 10% to 25%. Bus travel remained fairly ___, falling only ___ two percentage points.
  5. Correct. (a) The percent of students who cycle increased to 15%. (b) The car use was fell sharply.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) 40 + 30 + 20 + 10 = 100 and 22 + 28 + 25 + 25 = 100. (b) 18 percentage points. (c) Bus travel (2 points).
  2. The two pie charts compare the ways in which students got to campus in 2005 and 2025.
  3. Overall, car use fell sharply while cycling and walking became more common. The car, the most popular choice in 2005, had become the least used by 2025, and bus travel changed least.
  4. accounted … more; rose (or grew); stable … by.
  5. (a) The percentage of students who cycled rose by 15 percentage points, to 25%: use percentage, the past tense, and percentage points. (b) Car use fell sharply: use one verb, in the past simple.

A4. Table

Data. The table shows the average number of hours of television watched each week.

Age group 2000 2020
18–29 22 12
30–49 24 19
50–64 28 26
65+ 33 34
  1. Read the data. (a) Which group had the largest fall, and what was it as a percentage? (b) Which group did not fall? (c) What was the gap between the youngest and oldest groups in each year?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The table shows the average number of hours of television watched each week by four age groups in 2000 and 2020.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. Viewing by 18- to 29-year-olds ___ sharply from 22 hours ___ 12 hours. The over-65s were the only group ___ viewing ___ slightly.
  5. Correct. (a) In 2020, the 18–29 group watched the less hours. (b) The over-65s watched 34 hours, that was the highest.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) The 18–29 group: down 10 hours, about 45%. (b) The 65+ group (up one hour). (c) 11 hours in 2000 and 22 hours in 2020.
  2. The table compares weekly television viewing among four age groups in 2000 and 2020.
  3. Overall, television viewing fell in every age group except the oldest, and the fall was biggest among the youngest. As a result, the gap between the youngest and oldest groups doubled, from 11 hours to 22 hours.
  4. fell (or dropped) … to; whose … rose (or increased).
  5. (a) the fewest hours: hours is countable. (b) The over-65s watched 34 hours, which was the highest: use which after a comma.

A5. Mixed charts

Data. A bar chart shows quarterly ice-cream sales in 2024, and a line graph shows the average temperature in the same quarters.

Quarter Sales (thousand units) Temperature (°C)
1 20 5
2 55 15
3 90 24
4 30 9
  1. Read the data. (a) In which quarter did both peak? (b) How many times larger were sales in the third quarter than in the first?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The bar chart shows quarterly ice-cream sales in 2024, and the line graph shows the average temperature in the same quarters.
  3. Overview. Write the overview, linking the two charts.
  4. Gaps. Sales ___ a peak of 90,000 units in the third quarter, ___ the temperature reached 24°C. Both measures then ___ in the fourth quarter.
  5. Correct. Ice-cream sales in the third quarter were 90 and the temperature was 24 degrees.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) The third quarter. (b) 4.5 times (90 ÷ 20).
  2. The bar chart gives ice-cream sales by quarter in 2024, while the line graph compares the average temperature for the same quarters.
  3. Overall, sales and temperature followed the same pattern: both rose from the first quarter to a peak in the third and fell in the fourth.
  4. reached (or peaked at) … when (or while); fell (or declined).
  5. Ice-cream sales in the third quarter were 90,000 units and the temperature was 24°C: each figure needs its own unit.

A6. Static chart

Data. A bar chart shows the number of students enrolled on six courses at one college in 2024.

Course Students
Business 480
Nursing 350
Engineering 300
Art 210
Law 180
Music 90
  1. Read the data. (a) What is the difference between the highest and lowest? (b) How many times larger is the highest than the lowest? (c) How many courses had 300 or more?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The chart shows the number of students enrolled on six courses at one college in 2024.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. Business ___ the list with 480 students, ___ more than five times as many as Music. Law, ___ 180 students, ranked fifth.
  5. Correct. (a) Business were more popular than the others courses. (b) Music had less students than Art.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) 390 students. (b) 5.3 times (480 ÷ 90). (c) Three.
  2. The bar chart compares enrolment on six courses at a college in 2024.
  3. Overall, Business was by far the most popular course and Music attracted the fewest students. The three biggest courses each had 300 or more students.
  4. topped (or led) … which was; with.
  5. (a) Business was more popular than the other courses: Business is singular and other has no s. (b) Music had fewer students than Art: students is countable.

A7. Dynamic chart

Data. A line graph shows the number of subscribers (millions) to two streaming services.

Year Service X Service Y
2018 5 12
2019 9 14
2020 16 17
2021 22 18
2022 25 17
  1. Read the data. (a) When did X overtake Y? (b) How many times larger were X's subscribers in 2022 than in 2018? (c) What was Y's peak?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The line graph shows the number of subscribers to two streaming services between 2018 and 2022.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. Service X ___ fivefold, ___ 5 million to 25 million. Service Y ___ a peak of 18 million in 2021 before falling back slightly.
  5. Correct. (a) Service X increase from 5 million to 25 million. (b) Service Y peaked in 18 million in 2021.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) Between 2020 and 2021. (b) Five times. (c) 18 million in 2021.
  2. The line graph illustrates how the number of people who subscribed to two streaming services changed over a five-year period.
  3. Overall, Service X grew much faster than Service Y and overtook it in 2021, while Service Y rose only slightly and then fell back a little.
  4. grew (or rose) … from; reached (or peaked at).
  5. (a) Service X increased: use the past simple. (b) peaked at 18 million: at goes with the figure.

A8. Comparisons and rankings

Data. A bar chart compares average annual salaries (in thousands of dollars) for men and women in four jobs.

Job Men Women
Teacher 48 47
Engineer 72 64
Nurse 52 50
Manager 90 70
  1. Read the data. (a) What is the gap in each job? (b) Rank the jobs for men and for women. (c) Where is the gap largest?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The chart compares the average annual salaries of men and women in four jobs.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. Male managers earned ___ more than female managers: $90,000 ___ $70,000. The gap for teachers was ___ small, at only $1,000.
  5. Correct. (a) Men earned more than women in all of the jobs, the biggest gap was manager. (b) Engineer men earned 72 thousand.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) Teacher $1,000; engineer $8,000; nurse $2,000; manager $20,000. (b) The order is the same for both: manager, engineer, nurse, teacher. (c) For managers ($20,000).
  2. The chart compares how much men and women earn on average in four jobs.
  3. Overall, men earned more than women in every job, and the ranking of the jobs was the same for both sexes. The gap was largest for managers and almost non-existent for teachers.
  4. $20,000 (or considerably) … against (or compared with); extremely (or very).
  5. (a) Men earned more than women in every job, and the biggest gap was among managers: a comma cannot join two sentences. (b) Male engineers earned $72,000: use the adjective male and a full figure.

A9. Diagram with numbers and percentages

Data. A diagram shows how a university divided its annual budget of $200 million. Teaching received $90 million, research $50 million, facilities $40 million and administration $20 million. Of the teaching money, $72 million went on salaries and $18 million on equipment.

  1. Read the data. (a) Check that both splits add up. (b) What percentage of the whole budget did each of the four main parts receive? (c) What percentage of the teaching money went on salaries?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The diagram shows how a university spent its annual budget of $200 million.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. Teaching ___ for $90 million, ___ 45% of the budget. Of this, $72 million ___ spent on salaries.
  5. Correct. (a) Teaching got 45 percent from the budget. (b) Salaries were 36% of the teaching money.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) 90 + 50 + 40 + 20 = 200 and 72 + 18 = 90. (b) Teaching 45%, research 25%, facilities 20%, administration 10%. (c) 80% (72 ÷ 90).
  2. The diagram illustrates how a university divided its yearly budget of $200 million.
  3. Overall, teaching took the largest share, nearly half of the budget, and most of that money went on salaries. Research was second and administration received the least.
  4. accounted … representing (or which was); was.
  5. (a) Teaching received 45% of the budget: got ... from is wrong, and percent needs a number. (b) Salaries were 36% of the whole budget, or 80% of the teaching money: say which total the percentage refers to.

Part B: Diagrams, maps, plans and unusual visuals

For these types the data is described in words. If you are teaching, draw each one on the board as a diagram or map, or ask students to draw it from the description.

B1. Manufacturing process

Data. How glass bottles are recycled. (1) Used bottles are collected from recycling banks. (2) They are sorted by colour. (3) They are crushed into small pieces called cullet. (4) The cullet is melted in a furnace. (5) The melted glass is moulded into new bottles. (6) The new bottles are cooled and packed.

  1. Read the data. (a) How many stages are there? (b) Which stage changes the glass from solid to liquid? (c) Which stages prepare the glass for melting?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The diagram shows how glass bottles are recycled.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. First, used bottles ___ from recycling banks. They ___ then sorted ___ colour. The cullet ___ melted in a furnace.
  5. Correct. (a) First, the bottles collect from recycling banks. (b) After they sorting, they crushed.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) Six. (b) Stage 4 (the cullet is melted). (c) Stages 1 to 3 (collecting, sorting and crushing).
  2. The diagram illustrates the stages in the recycling of glass bottles.
  3. Overall, the process consists of six stages, beginning with the collection of used bottles and ending with the packing of new ones. The glass is sorted and crushed before it is melted and moulded.
  4. are collected … are … by … is.
  5. (a) First, the bottles are collected: a process uses the passive. (b) After being sorted, they are crushed: use after + being + past participle, and a passive main clause.

B2. Natural process

Data. How a pearl forms in an oyster. (1) A grain of sand enters the oyster's shell. (2) The oyster covers the sand with a smooth substance. (3) More layers of this substance build up over several years. (4) A pearl forms.

  1. Read the data. (a) How many stages are there? (b) Which stage is the turning point? (c) Which words give the time scale?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The diagram shows how a pearl is formed inside an oyster.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. A grain of sand ___ the oyster's shell. The oyster ___ the sand with a smooth substance, and more layers ___ up over several years.
  5. Correct. (a) The sand was covered by the oyster with a substance, that is smooth. (b) Layers of the substance are built up by themselves.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) Four. (b) Stage 2, when the oyster begins to cover the sand. (c) Over several years.
  2. The diagram illustrates the natural process by which a pearl develops inside an oyster.
  3. Overall, the process has four stages and involves no human activity: it begins when a grain of sand enters the oyster and ends with the formation of a pearl. The key change is the covering of the sand with layers of a smooth substance.
  4. enters … covers … build.
  5. (a) The oyster covers the sand with a smooth substance: nature is the doer, so use the active, and a defining adjective, not a clause. (b) Layers of the substance build up: build up is a natural change and takes the active.

B3. Life cycle

Data. The life cycle of a butterfly. (1) Adult butterflies lay eggs on leaves. (2) Caterpillars hatch and eat leaves. (3) Each caterpillar forms a pupa (a hard case) and changes inside it. (4) An adult butterfly comes out of the pupa. The adult then lays eggs, and the cycle starts again.

  1. Read the data. (a) How many stages are there? (b) Which stage is the biggest change of form? (c) What closes the cycle?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The diagram shows the life cycle of a butterfly.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. The cycle ___ when an adult butterfly lays eggs on leaves. The eggs ___ into caterpillars, which ___ leaves. Finally, an adult butterfly ___ out of the pupa and the cycle ___ again.
  5. Correct. (a) The caterpillar is hatch from the egg. (b) The cycle is ended when the butterfly lays eggs.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) Four. (b) Stage 3, inside the pupa. (c) The adult butterfly lays eggs.
  2. The diagram illustrates the stages in the life of a butterfly.
  3. Overall, the cycle has four stages and is continuous: adult butterflies lay eggs, and the adults that develop from them repeat the process. The biggest change takes place inside the pupa, where the caterpillar turns into a butterfly.
  4. begins (or starts) … hatch … eat … comes … begins.
  5. (a) The caterpillar hatches from the egg: hatch is intransitive and has no passive. (b) The cycle begins again when the butterfly lays eggs: a cycle does not end.

B4. Flow chart

Data. What happens when a student returns a library book. The student returns a book. Decision: Is the book late? No: no fine. Yes: the librarian counts the days late, and the student pays $1 for each day late.

  1. Read the data. (a) How many decisions and how many outcomes are there? (b) Which is the shortest path? (c) What decides the size of the fine?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The flow chart shows what happens when a student returns a library book.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. If the book ___ not late, the student ___ nothing. ___, the librarian counts the days late, and the student ___ $1 for each day.
  5. Correct. (a) If the book was late, the librarian count the days late. (b) Student pay one dollar by day.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) One decision and two outcomes. (b) No: the book is on time and there is no fine. (c) The number of days the book is late.
  2. The flow chart outlines the procedure that follows when a student returns a book to the library.
  3. Overall, the procedure has one decision and two possible outcomes: a student whose book is on time pays nothing, while a student whose book is late pays a fine that depends on the number of days.
  4. is … pays … Otherwise … pays.
  5. (a) If the book is late, the librarian counts the days: use the present simple for a procedure, and the third-person s. (b) The student pays one dollar for each day late: for each and the student.

B5. Map: past and present

Data. A school campus in 2000 and 2025. In 2000: the library stood in the north, a sports field in the east, a car park in the south and the main building in the centre. In 2025: the library was extended, the sports field was replaced by a sports hall, the car park was enlarged, a café was built next to the main building, and the main building was unchanged.

  1. Read the data. (a) Which feature did not change? (b) Which feature was added? (c) Which feature was replaced?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The maps show a school campus in 2000 and 2025.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. The sports field in the east ___ replaced ___ a sports hall. The library in the north ___ extended, and a café ___ built next to the main building.
  5. Correct. (a) The sports field was replace to a sports hall. (b) A café has built in 2025.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) The main building. (b) The café. (c) The sports field (replaced by a sports hall).
  2. The two maps illustrate how a school campus changed between 2000 and 2025.
  3. Overall, the campus became larger and more modern: the library and the car park were extended, the sports field was replaced by a sports hall and a café was added. The main building remained unchanged.
  4. was … by … was … was.
  5. (a) The sports field was replaced by a sports hall: replaced by, with the past participle. (b) A café was built in 2025: a finished past date takes the past simple passive.

B6. Map: present and proposed development

Data. A park now and the plan for it. Now: a pond in the centre, a playground in the north, open grass in the south and a car park in the west. Plan: the playground will be enlarged, sports courts will replace the open grass, a cycle path will be built around the pond, and the car park will be removed. The pond will remain.

  1. Read the data. (a) Which feature stays? (b) Which will be removed? (c) Which two features will be added?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The maps show a park now and a plan for its development.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. The open grass in the south ___ be replaced ___ sports courts. A cycle path ___ be built around the pond.
  5. Correct. (a) The car park will remove. (b) The playground is going to make bigger.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) The pond. (b) The car park. (c) The sports courts and the cycle path.
  2. The maps show the present layout of a park and the proposed changes to it.
  3. Overall, the park will become more active: the playground will be enlarged, sports courts and a cycle path will be added, and the car park will be removed. The pond in the centre will remain.
  4. will … by … will.
  5. (a) The car park will be removed: will be + past participle. (b) The playground will be enlarged: going to is informal, and make bigger is replaced by enlarged.

B7. Floor plan

Data. A flat before and after renovation. Before: living room (north), separate kitchen (north-east), bedroom 1 (south-west), bedroom 2 (south-east), bathroom (centre). After: the wall between the kitchen and living room was removed, so they form one open-plan area; bedroom 2 was converted into a study; bedroom 1 and the bathroom were unchanged.

  1. Read the data. (a) Which rooms did not change? (b) Which wall was removed? (c) Which room changed its use?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The floor plans show a flat before and after it was renovated.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. The wall between the kitchen and the living room ___ removed, so the two rooms ___ combined. The second bedroom ___ converted ___ a study.
  5. Correct. (a) The kitchen and living room has been combined. (b) The study was converted of a bedroom.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) Bedroom 1 and the bathroom. (b) The wall between the kitchen and living room. (c) Bedroom 2 (now a study).
  2. The plans show how the layout of a flat changed during its renovation.
  3. Overall, the flat was changed to create more living space: the kitchen and living room were combined into one open-plan area and one bedroom became a study. The other bedroom and the bathroom stayed the same.
  4. was … were … was … into.
  5. (a) The kitchen and living room were combined: a plural subject and the past simple passive for a before-and-after plan. (b) The bedroom was converted into a study: the original room is the subject and into introduces the new use.

B8. Unusual visual: scatter graph

Data. Hours studied and exam score for eight students (hours, score): (2, 45), (4, 52), (5, 58), (6, 61), (8, 70), (9, 40), (10, 78), (12, 84).

  1. Read the data. (a) Which student does not fit the pattern? (b) What is the overall relationship? (c) What is the range of scores?
  2. Introduction. Paraphrase: The scatter graph shows the number of hours that eight students studied and their exam scores.
  3. Overview. Write the overview.
  4. Gaps. The ___ the number of hours studied, the ___ the score. One student is an ___: he or she studied for nine hours but scored only 40.
  5. Correct. (a) The scatter graph shows a trend over time. (b) All students who studied more scored more.
Show the answer key
  1. (a) The student with 9 hours and a score of 40. (b) A positive relationship: more hours of study went with higher scores. (c) From 40 to 84 (or 45 to 84 without the outlier).
  2. The scatter graph compares the hours that eight students spent studying with the marks they achieved in an exam.
  3. Overall, students who studied for more hours tended to achieve higher scores. The only exception was one student who studied for nine hours but scored just 40.
  4. higher … higher … outlier.
  5. (a) The scatter graph shows a relationship between two measures: it does not show change over time. (b) Most students who studied more scored more: the outlier shows that all is false.

Part C: Skills drills and full writing tasks

C1. Introduction writing

Paraphrase each task statement in one sentence. Keep numbers, years and names.

  1. The bar chart shows the number of cars sold in four countries in 2022.
  2. The line graph shows changes in the amount of rainfall in two cities between 1990 and 2020.
  3. The pie chart shows the percentage of household income spent on different items in one country.
  4. The table gives information about the number of international students at five universities in 2010 and 2020.
  5. The diagram shows how tea is produced.
  6. The maps show a village in 1980 and 2020.
Show the answer key
  1. The bar chart compares car sales in four countries in 2022.
  2. The line graph illustrates how rainfall in two cities changed over thirty years.
  3. The pie chart illustrates how households in one country divide their income between different types of spending.
  4. The table compares international student numbers at five universities in 2010 and 2020.
  5. The diagram illustrates the stages in the production of tea.
  6. The two maps illustrate the changes that took place in a village between 1980 and 2020.

C2. Overview writing

Write a two-sentence overview for each data set. Use almost no figures.

Data set 1. Internet users (% of the population).

Year Country P Country Q Country R
2005 30 10 60
2010 50 25 70
2015 70 50 75
2020 85 80 78

Data set 2. Average monthly rent ($) by size of flat.

Flat City A City B
One bedroom 900 1,200
Two bedrooms 1,300 1,700
Three bedrooms 1,800 2,400

Data set 3. How workers spent their time (%).

Activity 2000 2020
Working 45 40
Commuting 10 5
Leisure 30 40
Other 15 15
Show the answer key
  1. Overall, internet use rose in all three countries, but fastest in Country Q, which started lowest and had almost caught up with Country P by 2020. Country R began highest but grew slowly, so the three countries were much closer by the end.
  2. Overall, rents were higher in City B for every size of flat. The gap widened as the flats got bigger, from $300 for one bedroom to $600 for three.
  3. Overall, workers spent less time working and commuting and more time on leisure in 2020. The time spent on other activities stayed the same.

A good overview picks the biggest change and the exception or the contrast, as these do.

C3. Data comparison

Data. Smartphone market share in 2024 (%): Brand X 38, Brand Y 27, Brand Z 19, Others 16.

Complete the sentences with the correct comparison.

  1. Brand X had ___ the share of Brand Z.
  2. Brand Y's share was ___ percentage points lower than Brand X's.
  3. Brand Z and the other brands had ___ shares: 19% and 16%.
  4. The three named brands together accounted for ___ of the market.
  5. Write one sentence comparing Brand X with Others.
Show the answer key
  1. twice (38 ÷ 19 = 2).
  2. 11 (38 − 27).
  3. similar (a difference of only three points).
  4. 84% (38 + 27 + 19).
  5. For example: Brand X's share was more than twice as large as that of the other brands (38% against 16%). (38 ÷ 16 = 2.4.)

C4. Vocabulary gap-fill

Word box: rose, rising, levelled off, contrast, fell, accounted, share, number, higher

Sales of electric cars (1) ___ steadily between 2015 and 2020, (2) ___ from 20,000 to 90,000. After 2020, the figure (3) ___ at around 90,000. By (4) ___, sales of petrol cars (5) ___ sharply, from 300,000 to 150,000. As a result, electric cars (6) ___ for a growing (7) ___ of total sales, although the (8) ___ of petrol cars sold was still (9) ___ than the number of electric cars in 2025.

Show the answer key

(1) rose, (2) rising, (3) levelled off, (4) contrast, (5) fell, (6) accounted, (7) share, (8) number, (9) higher.

Why. Rose is the past tense that goes with a past date. Rising from … to … is a participle clause that adds the start and end. Levelled off describes no change after a rise. By contrast introduces the opposite. Accounted for goes with a share, and the number of goes with a countable noun.

C6. Grammar correction

Correct the error in each sentence.

  1. The number of tourists were rising.
  2. Prices have increased in 2010.
  3. Sales rose to 20 million by 2010 from 12 million.
  4. The figure for cars is more higher than the figure for buses.
  5. Despite of the fall, sales recovered.
  6. The oil is filtering and then bottled.
  7. The tourists number increased.
  8. Although sales fell, but profits rose.
  9. There was a rise of sales.
  10. Men were more likely than women to using gyms.
Show the answer key
  1. The number of tourists was rising. The number is singular.
  2. Prices increased in 2010. A finished past date takes the past simple.
  3. Sales rose from 12 million to 20 million by 2010. Put the start before the end.
  4. The figure for cars is higher than the figure for buses. Do not use more with -er.
  5. Despite the fall, sales recovered. Despite has no of.
  6. The oil is filtered and then bottled. The passive needs the past participle.
  7. The number of tourists increased. A countable noun needs the number of.
  8. Although sales fell, profits rose. Use although or but, not both.
  9. There was a rise in sales. Use in for what changed.
  10. Men were more likely than women to use gyms. To is followed by the base form here.

C7. Paragraph organisation

Put these six sentences into the order of a Task 1 answer: introduction, overview, body paragraph 1, body paragraph 2. They describe the milk-price data in exercise A1.

  1. Town B's price, by contrast, fell slightly to $1.30 in 2019 and stayed at that level until 2021.
  2. Overall, milk became more expensive in both towns, but the rise was much steeper in Town A.
  3. The line graph illustrates how the cost of a litre of milk changed in two towns over an eight-year period.
  4. It then jumped to $1.52 in 2023, which was still $0.33 below Town A's price.
  5. Town A's price then rose steadily, overtaking Town B's between 2017 and 2019 and reaching $1.85 in 2023.
  6. In 2015, milk was cheaper in Town A, at $1.10, than in Town B, at $1.40.
Show the answer key

3, 2, 6, 5, 1, 4.

Why. Sentence 3 is the introduction (it paraphrases the task). Sentence 2 is the overview (the main trend, no detail). Sentences 6 and 5 form body paragraph 1 (Town A, in time order: 2015, then later). Sentences 1 and 4 form body paragraph 2 (Town B, in time order, linked by by contrast and it then). Then and by contrast show the order inside the paragraphs.
