Module 5: Opinion essays

Opinion essays are the most common Task 2 type. They test the skill examiners value most: a clear position, stated at the start and still clear at the end, with ideas that are extended and supported.

A. Understanding the question

The statement gives a view, usually beginning Some people think..., It is better to... or Governments should.... The instruction then asks for your opinion.

Instruction What it asks
To what extent do you agree or disagree? How far you agree. You may agree completely, disagree completely, or agree in part
Do you agree or disagree? Take a side (or a clearly explained middle position)
Do you think this is a good idea? Your opinion of the idea
What is your opinion? Your view on the issue

There is one main part: your position. But two things can make it harder:

  1. The statement may contain two claims. Children spend too much time on devices and not enough time playing outside has two claims. Your essay must deal with both.
  2. "To what extent" does not mean "discuss both sides". It means "how far do you agree?" You answer with a clear position and support it. You may mention the other side briefly, but you must not leave the reader unsure what you believe.

Positions on the scale

Position Thesis wording Body plan
Completely agree I strongly agree that... Two strong reasons for the statement
Mostly agree I largely agree that..., although... Two reasons for, with one short limit
Partly agree I agree up to a point, but... Body 1: the part you accept. Body 2: the part you reject, or a condition
Mostly or completely disagree I do not believe that... / I disagree with this view because... Two strong reasons against

B. Planning the answer

Three plans work for opinion essays.

Plan When to use it Body 1 Body 2
1. One side You have two strong reasons and nothing important against Reason 1 Reason 2
2. One side with a counter-argument You feel strongly but know the other side's best point Reason 1 Reason 2, plus a short counter-argument that you answer
3. Partly agree The statement is partly right and partly wrong What you accept, with a reason What you reject (or a condition), with a reason

Plan 2 is the strongest for Band 7. It shows the examiner that you have considered the other side, and you still keep a clear position.

Choosing your side. Write two quick lists, one for and one against. Then choose the side for which you can write two clear explanations with examples in English you know. The examiner marks your reasoning, not your opinion.

A completed plan (Plan 2)

Some people believe that managing personal finances should be taught as a compulsory subject in schools. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Notes
Question T: school subjects. F: money management as a compulsory subject. I: opinion, 1 part. L: compulsory, every school
Position Strongly agree
Introduction Young people leave school without money skills. Position: strongly agree, because skills are essential and rarely taught at home
Body 1 Young people face money decisions early (phones, online payments, loans). Few understand interest or debt. Example: a student with a loan. Result: safer choices
Body 2 School is the only equal place to learn. Many parents lack knowledge or time. Poorer children learn the least. Counter: timetable is full. Answer: a short weekly lesson can replace weaker material
Conclusion Agree: prepares young people, gives equal opportunity

C. Writing structure

Paragraph Sentences Frames
Introduction 1. Background in your own words 2. The view in the question 3. Your position Many... Some people believe that... / I strongly agree with this view, because...
Body 1 Topic sentence, explanation, example, result The main reason for supporting this idea is that... / For instance,... / As a result,...
Body 2 Topic sentence, explanation, example, result, then a counter-argument and your answer (Plan 2) Another reason is that... / Some people argue that... However,...
Conclusion Position + reasons In conclusion, I believe that..., because...

Phrases for stating a position

Position Phrases
Strong agreement I strongly agree that... / I firmly believe that... / There is no doubt in my mind that...
Partial agreement I agree to some extent, but... / I largely agree, although... / I accept this view only in certain circumstances.
Disagreement I disagree with this view. / I do not accept that... / I find this argument unconvincing, because...

Phrases for a counter-argument

  • It is true that..., but...
  • Some people argue that... However, this overlooks the fact that...
  • Admittedly,... Nevertheless,...

Common mistakes in opinion essays

Mistake Fix
Giving two sides equally and no opinion Decide first. State the position in the introduction and conclusion
Changing the position in the conclusion Check that the conclusion matches the introduction
Writing In my opinion in every paragraph State your opinion once in the introduction and once in the conclusion. In the body, argue with reasons
Ignoring the second claim in the statement Underline every claim in the statement and deal with each
Listing four ideas with no examples Choose two ideas and develop them fully
Using I think and I believe to hide a weak argument Support each idea with an explanation and an example

D. Band 7+ model essay

Some people believe that managing personal finances should be taught as a compulsory subject in every school. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Model answer · 283 words

Many young people leave school without understanding how money works. Some people believe that managing personal finances should be taught as a compulsory subject in every school. I strongly agree with this view, because financial skills are essential for adult life and are rarely taught at home.

The main reason for supporting this idea is that young people face serious money decisions much earlier than in the past. Teenagers now use mobile phone contracts, online payments and student loans, yet few of them understand interest or debt. A student who borrows money without reading the conditions, for instance, may spend years repaying far more than he expected. If schools taught these basic ideas, young adults would be able to make safer choices and avoid problems that can damage their lives for a long time.

Another reason is that school is the only place where every child can learn these skills equally. It is true that parents can teach their children about money, but many families do not have the knowledge or the time to do so. As a result, children from poorer homes, who have the most to lose from bad financial decisions, often learn the least. A compulsory lesson would give all pupils the same foundation, whatever their background. Some people argue that the school timetable is already too full. However, a short weekly lesson on budgeting and saving could replace less useful material, so it would not overload pupils.

In conclusion, I believe that money management should be a compulsory subject, because it prepares young people for real financial decisions and gives every pupil the same opportunity to learn. Schools would then produce adults who are more confident and more independent.

E. The model essay explained

Paragraph by paragraph

Paragraph What it does Why it works
1. Introduction (47 words) Paraphrases the topic (managing personal finances from money management), states the view, gives a clear position and two reasons in brief The position (strongly agree) is clear from the start, and the reasons tell the examiner what is coming
2. Body 1 (87 words) Idea: young people face money decisions early. Explanation: they use phones, payments and loans, but do not understand interest. Example: a student who borrows. Result: safer choices Four steps (IEER). The example is specific and realistic, with no invented statistic
3. Body 2 (107 words) Idea: school is the equal place to learn. Explanation: many parents lack time or knowledge. Result: poorer children learn the least. Counter-argument: a full timetable. Answer: a short lesson can replace weaker material The counter-argument is short and answered, so the position stays clear
4. Conclusion (42 words) Restates the position in new words and summarises both reasons No new ideas. The position matches the introduction

The four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the essay What would push it further
Task Response One main part, answered fully. The position is clear in paragraph 1 and paragraph 4. Both main ideas are extended and supported. The counter-argument is handled More specific support, for example naming one skill (comparing loan offers)
Coherence and Cohesion Four paragraphs, each with one idea and a topic sentence (The main reason..., Another reason...). Linking is varied: for instance, as a result, however, yet, so. Pronouns (these skills, they) refer clearly Vary the topic-sentence pattern (the two body paragraphs both start with a reason)
Lexical Resource Collocations: financial skills, compulsory subject, make safer choices, budgeting and saving, overload pupils, repaying. Topic words are precise A few more less common items, such as financial literacy or prevent debt
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Conditional (If schools taught..., young adults would be able to...). Relative clauses (who have the most to lose). Passive (should be taught). Modals (may spend, could replace). Comparatives (much earlier than, far more than). Concession (It is true that..., but...) More error-free complex sentences of different shapes (Module 12)

F. Try it yourself

Many people believe that shops should not open on public holidays so that employees can spend time with their families. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Show the sample plan

Your plan may differ and still be good. Check that yours has the same parts.

  • Analysis. T: shops and holidays. F: whether shops should close on public holidays for employees' family time. I: opinion, 1 part. L: should not open, so that employees...
  • Position. Mostly agree: most shops should close, but essential services should stay open.
  • Body 1. Employees need time with their families, and public holidays are the one day when everyone is free together. Example: a shop worker with school-age children who normally works weekends. Result: stronger family relationships and less stress.
  • Body 2. Closing is fair, because shops lose little (customers shop on other days), but some services such as pharmacies and public transport must stay open. Counter-argument: customers and owners lose income. Answer: customers adjust, and workers in essential services can be given extra pay and days off.
  • Conclusion. Mostly agree: closing most shops helps families, while essential services should continue.

More opinion questions for practice

  1. Some people think that it is better to teach children in single-sex schools. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  2. The best way to reduce traffic in cities is to make driving more expensive. Do you agree or disagree?
  3. Some people believe that university students should be required to study a foreign language. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  4. Reading books is a better use of leisure time than watching television. Do you agree or disagree?
  5. Governments should spend money on the arts only after meeting basic needs such as health and education. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  6. Children should not be allowed to use mobile phones at school. Do you agree or disagree?
