Module 11: Topic vocabulary

Lexical Resource is one quarter of your score. The official guidance says examiners look at the range of your vocabulary, how precise and appropriate it is, how well you use collocations, and how often spelling and word-formation errors appear. At Band 7 you need enough range for some flexibility and precision, including some less common words and a sense of style. This module gives you the words, and the habits, to do that.

A. How to learn and use vocabulary

1. Learn phrases, not single words. A collocation is a group of words that often go together. Raise awareness is natural. Increase awareness is possible, but rise awareness is wrong. When you learn a word, write down the verb or adjective that goes with it.

2. Learn the whole word family. Many errors in Task 2 are word-form errors, such as the economy growth instead of economic growth, or an educate system instead of an education system. The table in part D shows how.

3. Use a formal register. Task 2 is academic or semi-formal. Replace informal words as shown below.

Informal Academic or semi-formal
kids children, young people
a lot of many, a large number of, a great deal of, considerable
get obtain, receive, gain, become
good beneficial, positive, valuable, effective
bad harmful, negative, damaging, serious
big significant, considerable, major, substantial
thing factor, aspect, issue, feature, development
show demonstrate, indicate, illustrate, reveal
think believe, argue, consider, maintain
stuff goods, materials, items, equipment
really important essential, crucial, vital
and so on (write the full list, or use such as)

Also avoid contractions (write do not, not don't) and direct questions to the reader.

4. Avoid repetition, but not by changing key words. Use pronouns (they, this), synonyms (young people, teenagers) and different sentence structures. Do not change a key term so much that the meaning is lost. Public transport should stay public transport, not common vehicles.

5. Use only the words you can control. One precise collocation is worth more than three rare words used wrongly. Learn ten words at a time and use each in a sentence you write yourself.

B. Ten topic banks

Each table gives key phrases, what they mean, and an example sentence. Do not memorise the sentences. Use them to see how the phrase works, then write your own.

1. Education

Phrase Meaning Example
compulsory education schooling that the law requires Compulsory education in many countries ends at sixteen.
academic performance how well students do in their studies Large classes can harm academic performance.
vocational training training for a particular job or trade Vocational training prepares students for skilled work.
tuition fees money paid for teaching at university High tuition fees discourage students from poorer families.
gain qualifications obtain certificates or degrees Adults often gain qualifications in order to change careers.
lifelong learning learning that continues through adult life Lifelong learning helps workers to keep up with change.
the curriculum the subjects taught in a school The curriculum should include practical skills.
critical thinking the ability to analyse and judge ideas Good teachers develop critical thinking in their pupils.
drop out of leave a course before finishing it Students who drop out of university may find it hard to get a job.
distance learning studying away from the school, usually online Distance learning allows people in remote areas to study.

2. Technology

Phrase Meaning Example
technological advances new developments in technology Technological advances have transformed the way people communicate.
rely on depend on Many people rely on their phones for daily information.
automation the use of machines to do work Automation has reduced the number of jobs in factories.
artificial intelligence computer systems that perform tasks needing human thought Artificial intelligence may replace some routine office work.
digital skills the ability to use computers and the internet Most employers now expect basic digital skills.
privacy concerns worries about personal information Privacy concerns grow as companies collect more data.
screen time time spent using screens Excessive screen time can disturb children's sleep.
the digital divide the gap between people with and without access to technology The digital divide leaves some rural communities behind.
keep up with stay at the same level as It is difficult for older workers to keep up with new software.
become addicted to be unable to stop using something Some teenagers become addicted to online games.

3. Environment

Phrase Meaning Example
climate change long-term change in weather patterns Governments must act now to tackle climate change.
greenhouse gases gases that trap heat in the atmosphere Burning fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases.
renewable energy energy from sources that do not run out Wind and solar power are important sources of renewable energy.
fossil fuels coal, oil and gas Dependence on fossil fuels causes air pollution.
carbon footprint the amount of carbon dioxide caused by a person or activity Frequent flying increases an individual's carbon footprint.
deforestation the clearing of forests Deforestation destroys the habitats of many animals.
endangered species animals or plants at risk of disappearing Many endangered species survive only in protected areas.
raise awareness make people understand an issue Campaigns can raise awareness of the problem of plastic waste.
sustainable able to continue without causing lasting harm Sustainable farming protects the soil for future generations.
reduce waste produce less rubbish Households can reduce waste by recycling glass and paper.

4. Health

Phrase Meaning Example
a balanced diet a diet with the right amount of each kind of food A balanced diet lowers the risk of many diseases.
a sedentary lifestyle a way of life with little physical activity A sedentary lifestyle is a major cause of obesity.
obesity the condition of being seriously overweight Obesity is increasing among children in many countries.
life expectancy the average number of years a person lives Better healthcare has raised life expectancy.
mental health the health of the mind and emotions Long working hours can damage mental health.
a chronic disease an illness that lasts for a long time Diabetes is a chronic disease that needs lifelong treatment.
preventive healthcare measures that stop illness before it starts Preventive healthcare is cheaper than treating serious illness.
access to healthcare the ability to use medical services People in remote areas often have limited access to healthcare.
take regular exercise exercise often Adults should take regular exercise to stay fit.
processed food food that has been changed by industry before sale Processed food is often high in sugar and salt.

5. Work

Phrase Meaning Example
job security confidence that a job will continue Many young workers lack job security.
a work-life balance a healthy division of time between work and private life Flexible hours help employees to achieve a better work-life balance.
career prospects chances of advancing in a career Graduates who speak two languages have better career prospects.
the workforce all the people who work in a country or company The workforce is ageing in many countries.
the unemployment rate the percentage of people without jobs The unemployment rate rose during the economic crisis.
remote working working away from the office, often from home Remote working reduces travel time and costs.
gain work experience learn by doing a job Students can gain work experience through summer internships.
job satisfaction the feeling of being happy in one's work Job satisfaction tends to be higher in jobs with some independence.
meet deadlines finish work on time Pressure to meet deadlines is a common cause of stress.
be made redundant lose one's job because it is no longer needed Hundreds of employees were made redundant when the factory closed.

6. Crime

Phrase Meaning Example
commit a crime do something illegal Young people who commit crimes often come from poor areas.
the crime rate the number of crimes in a place The crime rate fell after more police patrolled the streets.
a prison sentence time in prison given as punishment A long prison sentence does not always change behaviour.
deter make someone decide not to do something Harsh punishments may deter some people from committing crimes.
reoffend commit another crime after being punished Offenders who learn a trade in prison are less likely to reoffend.
rehabilitation helping offenders to return to normal life Rehabilitation programmes can reduce crime in the long term.
community service unpaid work done for society as punishment Community service is a sensible punishment for minor offences.
law enforcement the work of the police and courts in applying the law Effective law enforcement needs public trust.
petty crime minor crime such as small thefts Petty crime increases in areas with high unemployment.
tackle the root causes deal with the basic reasons for a problem To reduce crime, governments must tackle the root causes, such as poverty.

7. Family and society

Phrase Meaning Example
an extended family a family that includes grandparents and other relatives In an extended family, grandparents often help to look after children.
the generation gap differences in attitudes between older and younger people The generation gap can cause misunderstanding between parents and teenagers.
an ageing population a population with a rising proportion of older people An ageing population puts pressure on pensions and healthcare.
social isolation a lack of contact with other people Social isolation is a serious problem for many elderly people.
bring up children care for and educate children as they grow Parents bring up their children according to their own values.
a sense of community a feeling of belonging to a local group Local festivals strengthen the sense of community.
cultural heritage the traditions and objects passed down from the past Museums help to preserve a country's cultural heritage.
peer pressure pressure to behave like people of the same age Peer pressure can lead teenagers to take risks.
the gap between rich and poor the difference in wealth between groups The gap between rich and poor is widening in many cities.
a support network a group of people who give help A strong support network helps people to cope with difficulty.

8. Transport and cities

Phrase Meaning Example
traffic congestion too many vehicles on the roads Traffic congestion wastes hours of workers' time every week.
public transport buses, trains and trams available to everyone Cheap and frequent public transport persuades people to leave their cars at home.
commuters people who travel regularly to work Commuters in large cities may spend two hours a day travelling.
the rush hour the busiest travel times of the day Buses are overcrowded during the rush hour.
urban planning the design and organisation of towns and cities Good urban planning keeps homes, work and services close together.
infrastructure basic systems such as roads, rail and water supply Rapid growth puts pressure on infrastructure.
affordable housing homes that people on average incomes can pay for Affordable housing is scarce in many capital cities.
overcrowding having too many people in a place Overcrowding increases the spread of disease.
cycle lanes roads or paths reserved for bicycles Cycle lanes make cycling safer and encourage people to cycle.
air quality how clean or polluted the air is Air quality improves when fewer cars enter the city centre.

9. Media and advertising

Phrase Meaning Example
the mass media newspapers, television, radio and online news The mass media can influence public opinion.
a target audience the group an advertisement or programme is aimed at Many advertisements for sweets have children as their target audience.
an advertising campaign a planned series of advertisements The advertising campaign raised sales by a quarter.
consumerism the belief that buying goods brings happiness Consumerism encourages people to buy things they do not need.
misinformation false information, often spread by mistake Misinformation spreads quickly on social media.
freedom of the press the right of the media to report without control Freedom of the press is essential in a democracy.
influence public opinion change what many people think Television can influence public opinion during elections.
be exposed to experience or see something often Children are exposed to hundreds of advertisements every day.
impulse buying buying something suddenly, without planning Special offers encourage impulse buying.
celebrity endorsement a famous person recommending a product Celebrity endorsement makes some products more attractive.

10. Government and money

Phrase Meaning Example
public spending money spent by the government Public spending on health has increased steadily.
allocate funds decide how to use money The government must allocate funds to schools and hospitals.
tax revenue money the government receives from taxes Tax revenue pays for roads and public services.
invest in spend money to produce future benefits Governments should invest in renewable energy.
subsidise give money to make something cheaper Some governments subsidise public transport.
economic growth an increase in the value of goods and services produced Economic growth depends partly on an educated workforce.
the cost of living the price of ordinary goods such as food and housing The rising cost of living hits low-income families hardest.
poverty the condition of lacking basic needs Poverty limits children's chances at school.
a priority something treated as more important than others Healthcare should be a priority in public spending.
the welfare system state support for people in need The welfare system helps people who lose their jobs.

C. Collocations that work in any essay

These verb and noun combinations are useful in almost every Task 2 essay.

Verb Common partners
tackle / address / solve a problem, an issue, unemployment, a crisis
raise / increase / boost awareness, income, productivity, living standards
reduce / cut / lower costs, pollution, crime, stress, the risk of
improve / enhance quality of life, performance, skills, health
provide / offer opportunities, support, services, access to
face / encounter difficulties, challenges, a risk, competition
make / reach a decision, a conclusion, progress
have / play an impact on, an effect on, a role in, a part in
encourage / discourage people to do something, investment, healthy habits
bring about / lead to / result in change, improvements, a decline, problems
place / put pressure on, a burden on, a strain on
take / carry out action, measures, steps, research
meet / satisfy demand, needs, requirements
gain / acquire knowledge, experience, skills, qualifications

D. Word families and word forms

Choose the correct form for the position in the sentence: noun, verb, adjective or adverb.

Verb Noun Adjective Adverb Common error
educate education, educator educational, educated educationally an educate system → an education system
economise economy, economics economic, economical economically the economy growth → economic growth
pollute pollution, pollutant polluted the pollute air → the polluted air
employ employment, employer, employee employed, unemployed unemployment people → unemployed people
decide decision decisive decisively to make a decide → to make a decision
develop development, developer developing, developed a developed country (rich) and a developing country (poorer)
succeed success successful successfully successed → succeeded
advertise advertisement, advertising advertise is a verb; the advertise is wrong

Spelling check. The most common spelling errors in student essays are: government, environment, necessary, definitely, separate, a lot (two words), receive, beginning, knowledge, unemployment, argument, different, advantage, especially. Check these in your own writing.

E. Try it yourself

Exercise 1: choose the correct collocation.

  1. Governments must (make / do / take) action to reduce air pollution.
  2. Long working hours can (put / give / make) pressure on families.
  3. Campaigns help to (rise / raise / arise) awareness of healthy eating.
  4. New roads will not (solve / win / pass) the problem of traffic congestion.
  5. Large classes can (hurt / harm / damage) academic performance. (Which is the best collocation here? More than one may be possible.)
  6. Young people who (finish / drop / leave) out of school often find it hard to get a job.
  7. Free museums provide people with (opportunity / opportunities) to learn about their culture.
  8. Employees who feel valued usually have greater job (satisfy / satisfying / satisfaction).
Show the answer key
  1. take
  2. put
  3. raise
  4. solve
  5. harm or damage (both are correct; hurt is less natural in an academic essay)
  6. drop
  7. opportunities
  8. satisfaction

Exercise 2: write the correct form of the word in brackets.

  1. The (economy) ____ growth of the country has been rapid. (use the adjective)
  2. Many (employ) ____ now work from home. (use the noun, plural)
  3. Air (pollute) ____ is a serious problem in big cities.
  4. A good (educate) ____ gives young people more choices.
  5. Parents make important (decide) ____ about their children's schools.
  6. The new policy was (success) ____ in reducing crime. (use the adjective)
  7. (Employ) ____ is higher in cities than in rural areas.
  8. Cheap flights have made travel much more (afford) ____. (use the adjective)
Show the answer key

1. economic. 2. employees. 3. pollution. 4. education. 5. decisions. 6. successful. 7. Employment. 8. affordable.

Exercise 3: replace the informal word with an academic one.

  1. Kids spend a lot of time on their phones.
  2. This is a really big problem in many cities.
  3. Television shows how people live in other countries.
  4. I think that exercise is good for everyone.
  5. Parents get stuff for their children that they do not need.
Show the answer key

Answers vary. Models:

  1. Children spend a great deal of time on their phones.
  2. This is a serious (or significant) problem in many cities.
  3. Television illustrates (or demonstrates) how people live in other countries.
  4. I believe that exercise is beneficial to everyone.
  5. Parents buy goods (or items) for their children that they do not need.

Exercise 4: your own word bank. Choose the topic you find hardest from part B. Write five sentences of your own using five phrases from the table, each on a different idea. Check them against the topic: are the words used in the right way? Add the sentences to a notebook and test yourself a week later.
