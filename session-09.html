Speaking Part 3: the discussion

Part 3 is a 4–5 minute conversation about bigger ideas connected to your Part 2 topic: society, change, the future. You don’t need perfect ideas. You need a clear answer, a reason and an example, in natural spoken English.

Part A. From Part 1 to Part 3

1

Personal → general

Part 1 asks about you. Part 3 asks about people and society.

Part 1What do you like to do with your friends?
Part 3Why do children make friends more easily than adults?
Part 1Would you like to be rich one day?
Part 3Some say happiness cannot be bought with money. Do you agree?
Part 1What is your favourite type of holiday?
Part 3Can travelling abroad change the way people think?
Aim for 3–5 sentences per answer: longer than Part 1, but not a speech. The examiner wants a conversation, so it’s fine to say “It depends…” or “I’m not sure, but…”.

Part D. The answer formula

7

Answer → Reason → Example → Balance

1
Answer the question directly.
2
Reason: say why. because, mainly since, the main reason is…
3
Example: make it real. For instance, in my city…
4
Balance (optional): show the other side. That said, … / On the other hand, …

Question: Do you think people are less healthy today than in the past? Label each sentence of this answer.

8

Four common mistakes

“Yes, I think so.” (and nothing more)
Add a reason and an example: 3–5 sentences.
Panicking at a hard question and going silent.
“That’s a tricky one… I suppose…” Then give a simple, general answer.
Hearing one word and answering a different question.
Listen to the whole question, especially words like why, compared with, in the future.
Giving a memorised, essay-like speech.
Speak naturally, as in a conversation: contractions, “I’d say”, “to be honest”.
