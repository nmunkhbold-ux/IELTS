Your turn: a new chart

Task sentenceThe pie chart below shows the native languages spoken by students at Cambridge College in year 2007.
English 45%Hindi 30% Chinese 13%Urdu 10%French 2%

A. Write one introduction sentence. The checklist updates as you type.

    The pie chart illustrates the proportion of students at Cambridge College in 2007 who spoke each of five native languages.

    The pie chart gives a breakdown of the first languages of students at Cambridge College in 2007.

    The chart shows what percentage of Cambridge College students in 2007 spoke English, Hindi, Chinese, Urdu or French as their mother tongue.

    B. Now the overview

    Choose the right words. Remember: an overview gives the big picture, with no detailed numbers.

    Overall, was the most common native language, spoken by of students, only a very small spoke French.

    Homework

    Write the full report for the bar chart in Step 3: introduction, overview and two detail paragraphs. Aim for 160–180 words in 20 minutes.

    • Use a different paraphrase from the one you made in Step 1.
    • Use at least five words from Step 5 and underline them.
    • Check every number against the chart before you hand it in.
    Model report (open after you have written yours)

    The bar chart compares the number of hours that school pupils in the United States, Australia and Canada spend each day on sleeping, studying and relaxing.

    Overall, Australian pupils devote far more time to studying than those in the other two countries, while pupils in the United States and Canada divide their day much more evenly between the three activities.

    In the United States and Canada, daily routines are very similar. American pupils sleep for 8.5 hours, study for 8 hours and relax for 7.5 hours. Canadian pupils spend almost exactly the same amount of time on each activity: 8 hours sleeping, 8.5 hours studying and, again, 7.5 hours relaxing.

    Australia, by contrast, stands out. Pupils there study for 13 hours a day, roughly 5 hours more than in the other two countries. As a result, they sleep the least, at 7 hours, and have only 4 hours to relax, which is around half the relaxation time of American and Canadian pupils.

    About 170 words.
