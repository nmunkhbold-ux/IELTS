Skimming and scanning

SkimmingScanning
PurposeThe main idea or general meaningA specific detail
What you readTitle, headings, first sentences, words that stand outOnly the words you search for: names, dates, numbers
Everyday exampleReading newspaper headlinesFinding a friend’s number in your contacts
IELTS questionsMatching headings, main-idea multiple choiceShort answers, sentence completion, matching information
Before scanning, predict the type of answer: a year, a number, a time, a name. Your eyes find it much faster.
4

The map of a passage

1
Main idea: the core message of the whole passage.The Eiffel Tower began as a controversial project but became a celebrated engineering achievement.
2
Topic sentence: the main idea of one paragraph. Usually the first sentence; sometimes the second (after a hook) or the last (as a conclusion).The speed of construction was remarkable.
3
Supporting details: facts, numbers, examples and reasons.About 2.5 million rivets were used, and the tower took just over two years to build.

Clues to a topic sentence: general words (main, key, another reason), ideas that the rest of the paragraph explains. Examples and numbers come after it.

5

Writing a summary

A summary gives the main ideas of a text in your own words. It is much shorter than the original, leaves out small details and examples, and contains no opinions.

1
Read and ask: what is this text mostly about?
2
Note one key point per paragraph, using the topic sentences.
3
Write: main idea first, then the key points with linking words.
4
Check: short, own words, no opinions.
FunctionUseful language
Main ideaThe text is about… / The article explains…
First pointFirstly, … / The text begins by…
Extra pointIn addition, … / Also, … / Moreover, …
ContrastHowever, … / On the other hand, …
Last pointFinally, … / Overall, …

True / False / Not Given: the method

TRUE: the passage says the same thing in different words.
FALSE: the passage says the opposite.
NOT GIVENThe passage says nothing about it. You cannot decide true or false.
1
Underline keywords, especially names, numbers and dates.
2
Find the place in the passage. Answers follow the passage order.
3
Compare the small words: only, all, never, always, first, most, some.
4
Decide: same meaning, opposite meaning, or missing.
Yes / No / Not Given works the same way, but it tests the writer’s opinion, not facts.
