Writing Task 2: the essay, step by step

Task 2 is worth twice as much as Task 1. A Band 7 essay is not about difficult words: it answers the exact question, has a clear plan, and develops two strong ideas with explanations and examples. Today you will practise each step, then write a full essay.

Part A. Understanding Task 2

1

The task at a glance

Five steps to a Band 7 essay

1
Understand the question: topic, keywords and instruction words.
2
Plan for 5–10 minutes: ideas, reasons and examples.
3
Introduction: paraphrase, position, main ideas.
4
Body paragraphs: point, explanation, example.
5
Conclusion: restate the position and summarise. No new ideas.
2

Recognise the essay type

Agree or disagreeGive your opinion and explain why.

Advantages and disadvantagesDiscuss the positive and negative sides.
Causes and solutionsExplain why a problem happens and how to solve it.
Causes and effectsExplain why something happens and its results.

Two-part questionAnswer two different questions, for example “Why?” and “Is it good?”.

Part B. Analyse the question

3

Topic, keywords and instruction

QuestionThe crime rate among teenagers has increased dramatically in many countries. Discuss some possible reasons for this increase and suggest solutions.
1
Topic: teenage crime, not “crime”. Writing about crime in general would only partly answer the question.
2
Keywords: crime rate, teenagers, increased dramatically, many countries.
3
Instruction: reasons for the increase and solutions. Two parts: answer both.
4

Practice: choose the precise topic

The right topic is not too general and not too narrow. Read the explanation after each answer.

Part C. Brainstorm and plan

5

From question to plan

1
Turn the instruction into focus questions. Why has teenage crime increased? What can be done?
2
Brainstorm two ideas for each. Reasons: family breakdown, nothing to do. Solutions: government support, parental responsibility.
3
Extend each idea by asking: Why does this happen? What is the result? Can I give an example?
4
Organise the ideas into an introduction, two body paragraphs and a conclusion.

Part D. The introduction

7

Three parts of a good introduction

1
Paraphrase the question in your own words.
2
Give your position clearly, if the question asks for it.
3
Outline your main ideas in a few words.
QuestionThere is a good deal of evidence that increasing car use is contributing to global warming and having other undesirable effects on people’s health and well-being. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
Nowadays, cars are very popular in modern life. Many people drive cars every day. Cars are good but also bad for the environment and health. This essay will talk about cars.
It is often claimed that the growing number of cars on the roads is damaging both the environment and people’s health. I agree with this view, because vehicle emissions are a major cause of global warming, and heavy car use encourages an inactive lifestyle and exposes people to polluted air.

The weak introduction is too general, repeats words, gives no position and no main ideas. Which part of the strong introduction is which?

You do not need an interesting opening. Avoid starting with “Nowadays…”, “In modern life…” or “In today’s society…”: they lead to general sentences that do not answer the question.

9

Label the paragraph

Question: Increasing the price of petrol is the best way to solve growing traffic and pollution problems. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Label each sentence of this body paragraph.

Check that your example really supports your point. An example about food prices, for instance, does not prove that petrol prices reduce traffic.

Part F. The conclusion

10

Rules for conclusions

1
Restate your position in new words.
2
Summarise your main arguments in one or two sentences.
3
No new ideas. A recommendation or prediction is optional, and only if it follows from your arguments.

Which conclusion starters are good for an IELTS essay?

Paraphrase, but don’t force it. Repeating an accurate word is better than using an incorrect synonym.

12

Common mistakes

Writing generally: “In today’s society, technology is very important.”
Start with the exact issue in the question.
No clear opinion, or changing your opinion halfway.
State it in the introduction and keep it to the end.
A list of five ideas with one sentence each.
Two ideas, each explained and supported with an example.
Informal words: “kids”, “a lot of stuff”, “gonna”.
Academic style: “children”, “many things”, “going to”.
Long, impressive words you are not sure about.
Words you can use accurately. Clarity beats complexity.
Running out of time with no conclusion.
Always finish with at least a one-sentence conclusion.
