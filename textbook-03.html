Module 3: Bar charts

A bar chart compares quantities by the length of its bars, so a Band 7 report picks out the largest, smallest and most changed bars and compares them, instead of listing each bar in turn.

A. Understanding the question

What it shows. Each bar's length (or height) equals a quantity. Bars can show categories at one moment, or the same categories at two or more dates.

How to recognise it. Rectangular bars on a scale, either standing up (vertical, or column) or lying across (horizontal). There may be one bar per category, a group of bars per category, or bars divided into stacked segments.

What to look for. The unit and where the scale starts; the categories; what each colour or pattern means (usually a year or a group); the highest and lowest bars; the biggest differences between bars in a group; and, if there are two dates, which bars changed most and which hardly changed.

What the task asks. Select and report the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Common wordings.

Wording What it means for your answer
“The bar chart compares … in 2010 and 2025.” Grouped bars, two dates: compare each category and describe change
“The chart shows the number of … in six countries.” One date: rank and compare the countries
“The bar chart shows … by age group and gender.” Two grouping variables: compare groups first, then gender within them
“The chart gives information about the percentage of … in 1990 and 2010.” Percentages: use percentage points for differences
“The bar chart shows … for different types of … (stacked bars).” Compare the totals first, then the largest segments

Vertical and horizontal bars. The reading method is the same; the use is different.

Vertical (column) Horizontal
Typical use Change between dates; a few categories Ranking many categories at one date; long labels
How to read it Compare heights Compare lengths; the order from top to bottom is a ranking
The overview says Which categories rose or fell most, and any change in the top category Which category is largest and smallest, and how the leaders differ from the rest
Typical language rose, fell, halved, overtook topped the list, followed by, the least popular

B. How to analyse a bar chart

  1. Read the axis. Note the unit and check where the scale starts. Bar lengths are only honest if the scale starts at zero.
  2. Identify the series. What do the colours or patterns mean? Here the light bars are 2010 and the dark bars are 2025.
  3. Rank each series. List the order from highest to lowest in the first year, then again in the second. A change in the order is a feature for your overview.
  4. Measure each change. Work out the difference or the ratio in pencil. Ratios give you better language than two separate figures.
  5. Spot the exception. The category that hardly changed is as useful to a comparison as the one that changed most.
  6. Group. Put categories that moved the same way together (the two that fell; the two that rose).
  7. Select. Choose the highest and lowest values, the biggest changes and the exception. With a bigger chart, leave the middle bars out.

C. Writing structure for a bar chart

Paragraph What to write Why
Introduction Paraphrase the task: what is measured, for whom, and the two dates or categories Shows you understand the visual; no figures
Overview The main contrast (which categories rose, which fell) and the biggest change in ranking Gives the big picture before the details
Body 1 The starting position (the first date's ranking) and the categories that fell Sets the baseline and groups the declines
Body 2 The categories that rose, and the exception that hardly changed Completes the comparison and keeps one idea per paragraph

With two dates there are two sensible plans: by date (the first year, then the second) or by direction of change (those that fell, then those that rose). The model answer uses a mixture: Body 1 gives the ranking in the first year and the declines, and Body 2 gives the increases and the exception.

D. Band 7+ model answer

The bar chart below shows the average number of hours per week that teenagers spent on five leisure activities in 2010 and 2025.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words. (About 20 minutes)

Model answer · 174 words

The bar chart compares the average number of hours that teenagers spent each week on five leisure activities in 2010 and 2025.

Overall, teenagers spent much more time on social media and video games in 2025, whereas television and reading both declined sharply. Social media, the least popular activity in 2010, had become the most time-consuming by 2025.

In 2010, watching television was the leading activity, at 14 hours a week, followed by sport and exercise (8 hours) and reading (6 hours). Social media and video games attracted only 4 and 5 hours respectively. By 2025, however, the time devoted to television had halved to 7 hours, and reading also fell by half, to just 3 hours.

In contrast, social media use quadrupled from 4 to 16 hours, overtaking every other activity, and the time spent playing video games rose from 5 to 9 hours. Sport and exercise was the most stable activity, dipping only slightly from 8 to 7 hours. Taken together, the five activities rose from 37 to 42 hours a week.

E. The model answer explained

Paragraph 1: introduction

Sentence Why it works
“The bar chart compares the average number of hours that teenagers spent each week on five leisure activities in 2010 and 2025.” Compares replaces shows and each week replaces per week. Not every word needs a synonym: hours, teenagers and the dates stay, because there is no better alternative and changing them would change the meaning. One sentence covers the unit, the group, the topic and the two dates.

Paragraph 2: overview

Sentence Why it works
“Overall, teenagers spent much more time on social media and video games in 2025, whereas television and reading both declined sharply.” It groups the four main bars by direction (two up, two down) with no figures, and whereas shows the contrast. Sport is left out of the overview because it hardly changed.
“Social media, the least popular activity in 2010, had become the most time-consuming by 2025.” The biggest change in ranking, from last place to first. The past perfect (had become) shows that the change was complete before the end of the period.

Paragraph 3: first body paragraph (2010 ranking and the declines)

Sentence Why it works
“In 2010, watching television was the leading activity, at 14 hours a week, followed by sport and exercise (8 hours) and reading (6 hours).” It ranks the top three in one sentence with followed by. The two smallest bars are saved for the next sentence, so no figure is repeated.
“Social media and video games attracted only 4 and 5 hours respectively.” Respectively matches two items to two figures in the same order, which saves a whole sentence.
“By 2025, however, the time devoted to television had halved to 7 hours, and reading also fell by half, to just 3 hours.” However signals the change of date. Halved and fell by half express the same size of change in two ways, so the vocabulary does not repeat. To just 3 hours adds a small judgement of size.

Paragraph 4: second body paragraph (the increases and the exception)

Sentence Why it works
“In contrast, social media use quadrupled from 4 to 16 hours, overtaking every other activity, and the time spent playing video games rose from 5 to 9 hours.” In contrast links back to the declines. Quadrupled gives the ratio. Overtaking every other activity is a participle clause that states the result of the change.
“Sport and exercise was the most stable activity, dipping only slightly from 8 to 7 hours.” The exception, introduced with a superlative (the most stable). Dipping only slightly matches the size of a one-hour change on a scale of 20.
“Taken together, the five activities rose from 37 to 42 hours a week.” A calculated total adds a fresh, accurate comparison (14+8+6+5+4 = 37; 7+16+9+3+7 = 42) without opinion. It is optional: it earns credit only because it is correct and relevant.

Selecting data. This chart has only ten bars, so almost every figure is used. Even so, the figures are grouped and compared and never listed one by one. With a chart of 20 bars you would choose the extremes, the biggest changes and the exception.

How the answer meets the four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the answer
Task Achievement A clear overview with the main contrast and the change in ranking; all key features covered, including the exception; figures accurate; no opinions or reasons.
Coherence and Cohesion One idea per paragraph; linking with however, in contrast, whereas and taken together; respectively and followed by keep the ranking compact; the time devoted to television is not repeated word for word.
Lexical Resource attracted, devoted, quadrupled, halved, overtaking, dipping, the most time-consuming; precise ratios replace repeated increase and decrease.
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Past simple, with the past perfect (had become, had halved) for completed change; participle clauses (overtaking, dipping); superlatives; a reduced clause (taken together); complex sentences with whereas.
