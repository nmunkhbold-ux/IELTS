Module 2: Reading the question

More students lose marks in the first three minutes than in the last five. They write a good essay about the wrong thing, or they answer one question when the prompt asks two. This module teaches a four-step method, TFIL, that finds every part of the question before you write a word.

A. The anatomy of a Task 2 question

Every Task 2 question has the same four parts.

Part What it is Example
Topic The general subject Museums
Statement A point of view, an argument or a problem Some people think that museums should be free to enter for everyone
Instruction The question you must answer To what extent do you agree or disagree?
Closing lines The same in every test Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

The instruction decides what kind of essay you write. There are five main question types, and many questions mix two of them.

Type Typical instruction Main parts you must answer Module
Opinion To what extent do you agree or disagree? / Do you agree or disagree? 1. Your position, and how far you agree 5
Discussion Discuss both views and give your own opinion. 1. View A 2. View B 3. Your opinion 6
Problem and solution What problems does this cause? What can be done? (or: What are the causes? What are the solutions?) 1. Problems (or causes) 2. Solutions 7
Advantages and disadvantages What are the advantages and disadvantages? / Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? 1. Advantages 2. Disadvantages (3. Your judgement, if "outweigh") 8
Two-part (direct) questions Why is this? Is this a positive or negative development? 1. Answer to question 1 2. Answer to question 2 9
Mixed A combination, such as: Which view do you agree with, and what can be done? Count the questions 10

Why "main parts" matter. The official guidance says that to reach Band 6 or higher you must address all the main parts of the prompt, and that leaving out a main part limits you to Band 5. Counting the parts correctly is the cheapest way to protect your Task Response score.

B. The TFIL method

Use these four steps on every question. They take two or three minutes.

  1. T: Topic. What is the general subject? Write it in two or three words.
  2. F: Focus. What exactly is the prompt asking about this topic? Look for the narrow idea inside the topic. The focus is what your essay must stay on.
  3. I: Instruction. What kind of essay is this (opinion, discussion, problem and solution, advantages and disadvantages, two-part)? How many main parts are there?
  4. L: Limits. Which small words limit the answer? Circle them. Words such as all, only, most, should, better and main change what you may argue.

Worked example

Some people believe that the best way to reduce crime is to give longer prison sentences. Others think that education and job training are more effective. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Step Answer
T Crime
F Which method reduces crime best: longer prison sentences, or education and job training? (Not: the causes of crime, or the history of prisons)
I Discussion. Three main parts: view A (prison), view B (education and training), your opinion
L best way, reduce crime, more effective. The question is about reducing crime, so every idea must be about reducing crime

The focus is where most students go wrong. If you write about "why crime happens", you have chosen your own topic, and your Task Response will suffer even if your English is excellent.

Limit words and what they do

Word Why it matters What you must do
all / everyone A strong claim. It is hard to defend without exceptions Agree fully, or argue that exceptions exist
only Excludes alternatives Say whether other things matter too
should Asks what is right, not what is true Argue about what ought to happen, not what does happen
the best / better / more important A comparison Compare both things. Do not just praise one
too much / not enough Two judgements in one phrase Deal with both, or say clearly which one you accept
nowadays / increasingly A trend over time Use present tenses and trend language
in your country Limits the evidence Use examples from a country you know
young people / children / workers A specific group Keep your ideas about that group

D. Five traps and how to avoid them

Trap What happens How to avoid it
1. Answering the topic, not the question The essay is about the general subject and misses the focus Write the focus in one sentence before you plan. Check that every main idea answers it
2. Missing the second question Only one part is fully answered, and Task Response stays at Band 5 Count the question marks and instruction verbs. Write the number of parts at the top of your plan
3. Ignoring limit words You argue against something the prompt did not say Circle every limit word. Say whether you accept it
4. Not giving your own position In a discussion or outline you describe both sides but never say what you believe Write your position in one sentence before you start. It must appear in the introduction and the conclusion
5. Misreading "to what extent" You think you must fully agree or fully disagree You may agree partly. But explain exactly which part you accept and why

E. Paraphrasing the topic for your introduction

Your introduction should restate the topic in your own words. Copying the question gives the examiner no evidence of your vocabulary range. These four techniques cover most cases.

Technique Original Paraphrase
Synonyms Young people spend too much time online. Teenagers and young adults spend an excessive amount of time on the internet.
Change the word class Technology makes life easier. Advances in technology have simplified daily life.
Change the structure Governments should spend more on public transport. More public money ought to be invested in public transport by governments.
Change the order Many people believe that university education is too expensive. University education is too expensive, according to many people.

Six more pairs for practice:

Original Paraphrase
More people are choosing to study abroad. An increasing number of students decide to take their degrees in other countries.
Children learn better when lessons are fun. Pupils achieve better results when classes are enjoyable.
Pollution is a serious problem in big cities. Air and noise pollution seriously affect people who live in large urban areas.
Some people believe that zoos should be closed. There is an opinion that zoos should no longer exist.
Many workers feel stressed because of long hours. A large number of employees suffer from stress as a result of lengthy working days.
Public libraries are losing visitors. Fewer people are using libraries that are open to the public.

Rules for paraphrasing

  • Keep the meaning exactly the same. Teenagers are not the same as children.
  • Do not paraphrase a word that has no good synonym (museum, internet). Change the words around it instead.
  • Do not change a key limit word. If the prompt says all, do not write many.
  • Never copy more than three or four words in a row from the question.

F. Try it yourself

Exercise 1. Analyse each question with TFIL. For each, write the Topic, the Focus, the Type, the number of main parts and the most likely trap.

  1. Some people think that all young people should do a period of unpaid community service. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  2. Some people prefer to live in the countryside, while others prefer cities. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
  3. Fast food is becoming more popular in many countries. Why is this? Is it a good or bad development?
  4. Some people say that it is more important to protect natural places than to build new housing. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  5. Many people leave their home town to live and work in another city. What are the causes of this, and what effects does it have on the people who stay?
  6. Zoos keep wild animals in small spaces. Some people think zoos should be closed. Others think zoos are useful. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
Show the answer key
  1. Topic: community service. Focus: whether all young people should do unpaid service. Type: opinion, 1 part (how far you agree). Trap: writing about the value of volunteering and ignoring all and unpaid.
  2. Topic: where to live. Focus: countryside versus city. Type: discussion, 3 parts. Trap: describing both places but never giving your own opinion.
  3. Topic: fast food. Focus: reasons for its popularity, and whether the trend is good or bad. Type: two-part, 2 parts. Trap: giving reasons but not judging the trend.
  4. Topic: environment and housing. Focus: which is more important, protection of nature or new housing. Type: opinion with a comparison, 1 part. Trap: discussing only one side. You must compare.
  5. Topic: moving to another city. Focus: causes, and the effects on the people who stay (not on those who leave). Type: causes and effects, 2 parts. Trap: writing about the effects on the people who move.
  6. Topic: zoos. Focus: whether zoos should be closed or are useful. Type: discussion, 3 parts. Trap: writing about animal rights in general.

Exercise 2. Paraphrase each sentence for an introduction. Use a different technique each time.

  1. Many people think that technology makes life easier.
  2. Cities are becoming more crowded every year.
  3. Employers should offer more training to their staff.
  4. Some students find exams very stressful.
  5. Governments should do more to protect the environment.
Show the answer key

Your answers will differ. These are acceptable models.

  1. A large number of people believe that modern devices simplify everyday life.
  2. The populations of urban areas are growing larger each year.
  3. Companies ought to provide their employees with more opportunities to develop their skills.
  4. For some pupils, taking examinations causes a great deal of stress.
  5. Public authorities have a responsibility to take stronger action to safeguard the natural environment.
