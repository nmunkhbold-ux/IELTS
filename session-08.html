Speaking Part 2: the two-minute talk

In Part 2 you get a topic card, one minute to plan, and then you talk on your own for up to two minutes. It sounds long, but with a quick plan, a strong first sentence and a few ways to add detail, two minutes goes surprisingly fast.

Part A. What happens in Part 2

1

The long turn

A topic card

Describe an exciting experience you’ve had.You should say:
  • when it was
  • where you were
  • what it was
and explain what made this experience especially exciting.
The bullet points are a guide, not a test. Covering all of them gives you a plan, but you can add other ideas. The last line (“and explain…”) is usually the best place to say the most.

Part C. Plan in one minute

4

Notes, not sentences

Write one or two keywords for each bullet point. That is enough to remind you what to say next.

“Last summer I went to Bali with my friends and it was very relaxing because…” (too slow to write)
What: beach · Where: Bali · When: last summer · Why: relaxing, friends, sunset

Shape of a good talk

Opening
10–15 s
Bullet points, with details
about 20–30 s each
Ending
10–15 s
5

Spot the problem

Describe a place you would like to visit.You should say:
  • where it is
  • what you can do there
  • how you learned about it
and explain why you want to visit it.
A student’s answerOne place I really like to go to is the beach. There is a beach near my city called Golden Beach. It’s about 30 minutes away by car. When I go there, I usually walk along the shore, swim and sometimes just relax… I often go there with my family or friends, especially at weekends…
Read an improved answer

I’d like to tell you about a place I’ve wanted to visit for years: Lake Khövsgöl in the north of Mongolia. I first heard about it from my uncle, who went camping there and showed me photos of the clearest water I’d ever seen.

From what I’ve read, there’s a lot to do there. You can go horse riding along the shore, hike in the forests or take a boat out on the lake. In winter the whole lake freezes, and there’s even an ice festival with horse-sleigh races, which I’d love to see.

The main reason I want to go is that I grew up in the city, so I’ve never really spent time in wild nature. I think a few days by the lake, with no traffic and no phone signal, would be the perfect break. So I’m planning to go with my friends next summer, after my exams.

8

Nerves and mistakes

Long silence, or “Sorry, I forgot everything.”
“Let me think for a second…” then continue with the next bullet point.
Ignoring a mistake, or starting the whole talk again.
A quick self-correction: “…a park, sorry, I mean a beach.”
Saying nothing about a topic you don’t know well.
Talk about something close to it, or something you have heard about. It does not have to be true.
Stopping after one minute.
Add your feelings, a short story, or what you would do next time, until the examiner stops you.
