Module 5: Static and dynamic charts, comparisons and rankings, diagrams with numbers

This module teaches four skills that cut across every chart type: describing one moment (static), describing change (dynamic), comparing and ranking groups, and describing a diagram that carries numbers.

  • 5.1 Static charts: one point in time
  • 5.2 Dynamic charts: describing change over time
  • 5.3 Comparisons and rankings
  • 5.4 Diagrams with numbers and percentages

5.1 Static charts: one point in time

A. Understanding the question

What it shows. Figures for several items at a single moment: one year, one month, one survey. Nothing changes over time, so the task is about size, rank and difference.

How to recognise it. One date in the task or title (or none), and a bar chart, pie chart or table with a single set of figures. The task often says compares, in 2024, in a survey or the average.

What to look for. The unit; the highest and lowest items; the size of the gaps between items; groups of similar values; and the middle of the range.

What the task asks. Select and report the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Because nothing moves, all of your marks come from ranking and comparing accurately.

Common wordings.

Wording What it means for your answer
“The chart shows the average monthly rent in seven cities in 2024.” A ranking task: order the cities and compare the extremes
“The bar chart compares the number of visitors to eight attractions last year.” Last year is a past date: past simple
“The chart gives information about the percentage of students who chose each subject.” Shares: use percentage points for differences
“The graph shows the results of a survey of 500 people.” Say respondents or people surveyed; the survey is past

Tense. Use the past simple when the date is in the past, including “last year” or “2024”. Use the present simple when there is no date or the chart describes a general fact.

Unsorted bars. Exam charts often list items in no particular order, as the chart in this section does. Sorting them in your head is the first step of the analysis.

B. How to analyse a static chart

  1. Note the unit and the date.
  2. Rank every item from highest to lowest and write the order on the paper.
  3. Measure the gaps between neighbours. A large gap marks a natural boundary between groups.
  4. Form two or three groups (high, middle, low) at the big gaps.
  5. Choose the comparisons. Leader against second place (a difference), highest against lowest (a ratio), and the middle against the extremes.
  6. Select the extremes, the group boundaries and the biggest gap. You do not need every figure.

A worked gap analysis for this chart:

Rank City Gap to the city below
1 Harbourton $430
2 Eastmere $160
3 Northgate $160
4 Millbrook $120
5 Ashford Bay $110
6 Westvale $230
7 Stonebridge Lowest

Two gaps stand out: $430 below the leader and $230 above the lowest. They isolate the most and least expensive cities, and the cities between them form a smooth sequence. The average of the seven cities is $1,160, and only the top three are above it. A ratio gives the best single comparison: Harbourton costs almost three times as much as Stonebridge.

Include the extremes, the group boundaries and the largest gap. Leave out most of the middle figures, and any reason for the differences (“because it is a port”).

C. Writing structure for a static chart

Paragraph What to write Why
Introduction Paraphrase the task: what is measured, where, and when Shows you understand the chart
Overview The spread from lowest to highest, the highest and lowest items, and the main grouping The big picture of a ranking
Body 1 The top group in rank order, with a ratio or difference against the lowest Compares instead of listing
Body 2 The remaining items, grouped by size, ending with the lowest Completes the ranking

Group by size, not by the order of the chart. The unsorted order of the bars has no meaning.

D. Band 7+ model answer

The bar chart below shows the average monthly rent for a one-bedroom flat in seven cities in 2024.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words. (About 20 minutes)

Model answer · 160 words

The bar chart compares the average monthly cost of renting a one-bedroom flat in seven cities in 2024.

Overall, rents varied widely between the cities, from $640 to $1,850 a month, and only the three most expensive cities charged more than $1,200. Harbourton was by far the most expensive place to rent, whereas Stonebridge was the cheapest.

Harbourton topped the ranking at $1,850, which was almost three times the figure for Stonebridge, the lowest at $640. Eastmere came second at $1,420, some $430 below Harbourton, and Northgate was third at $1,260, a further $160 lower, so these three cities formed a clearly more expensive group.

The remaining four cities were all cheaper than the average of $1,160 across the seven cities. Rents in the next three ranged from $1,100 in Millbrook to $870 in Westvale, with Ashford Bay in between at $980, while Stonebridge, at $640, was the only city where a one-bedroom flat cost less than $800 a month.

E. The model answer explained

Paragraph 1: introduction

Sentence Why it works
“The bar chart compares the average monthly cost of renting a one-bedroom flat in seven cities in 2024.” Compares replaces shows, and the cost of renting turns the noun rent into a different phrase. One-bedroom flat stays because it is a key detail that cannot be changed without changing the meaning.

Paragraph 2: overview

Sentence Why it works
“Overall, rents varied widely between the cities, from $640 to $1,850 a month, and only the three most expensive cities charged more than $1,200.” For a ranking, the range (lowest to highest) is itself the main feature, so two figures are acceptable here. The second half names a grouping: three expensive cities and four cheaper ones.
“Harbourton was by far the most expensive place to rent, whereas Stonebridge was the cheapest.” The extremes in plain words. By far signals the large gap ($430) between first and second place.

Paragraph 3: the top group

Sentence Why it works
“Harbourton topped the ranking at $1,850, which was almost three times the figure for Stonebridge, the lowest at $640.” Topped the ranking is ranking language. The relative clause turns two figures into a ratio (1,850 ÷ 640 = 2.9), and the figure for avoids repeating rent.
“Eastmere came second at $1,420, some $430 below Harbourton, and Northgate was third at $1,260, a further $160 lower, so these three cities formed a clearly more expensive group.” Came second, was third give the order; some $430 below and a further $160 lower chain the differences. The last clause states the grouping, which is what the examiner needs to see.

Paragraph 4: the remaining cities

Sentence Why it works
“The remaining four cities were all cheaper than the average of $1,160 across the seven cities.” A calculated average (8,120 ÷ 7 = 1,160) gives a fresh point of comparison. It is correct, relevant and optional: only add a calculated figure if you are certain of it.
“Rents in the next three ranged from $1,100 in Millbrook to $870 in Westvale, with Ashford Bay in between at $980, while Stonebridge, at $640, was the only city where a one-bedroom flat cost less than $800 a month.” Ranged from … to …, with … in between compresses three figures into one phrase. The only city where picks out the lowest as an exception and closes the ranking.

How the answer meets the four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the answer
Task Achievement A clear overview naming the range, the extremes and the grouping; the leaders, the lowest and the boundary figures are selected; figures and calculations accurate; no reasons or opinions.
Coherence and Cohesion Paragraphs by size, not by chart order; so, whereas, while connect ideas; the remaining four cities links the last paragraph to the third.
Lexical Resource topped the ranking, came second, ranged from, a further $160 lower, by far, the cheapest; no repeated rent is high.
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Past simple throughout for a 2024 date; relative clauses (which was almost three times); comparatives and superlatives (the most expensive, cheaper, the lowest); a where clause; accurate use of a further.

5.2 Dynamic charts: describing change over time

A. Understanding the question

What it shows. How quantities change between two or more dates. Any chart can be dynamic: a line graph, grouped bars, a table with several years, or, as here, stacked bars. In a stacked bar each bar is a whole (here 100% of households) and its coloured segments are the parts, so you can follow every part from one bar to the next.

How to recognise it. Two or more dates in the task, on the axis or in the key. In a stacked chart, bars split into coloured segments with a key.

What to look for. For each part, its start, its end and its direction; the largest and smallest part at each date; turning points; parts that stay stable; and any change in ranking. Segments in the middle of a stack do not start at zero, so read their size from the labels or by subtraction, not from where they end.

What the task asks. Select and report the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Describe change: do not just list figures for each date.

Common wordings.

Wording What it means for your answer
“The chart shows the proportion of households by type in 1990, 2000, 2010 and 2020.” Follow each type across the four bars
“The bar chart illustrates changes in … between 1980 and 2020.” Past simple; compare the first and last dates, then the turning points
“The chart shows how … changed over a thirty-year period.” Restate the period in your own words

Language. Past simple for completed past change. The past perfect (had risen, had become) shows that a change was complete before a later date.

B. How to analyse a dynamic chart

  1. Read the key and the unit. Which segment is which? What does each bar total?
  2. Follow each part across the bars. Write its start, its end and its direction in a few words (rose steadily, peaked then fell).
  3. Measure the change in percentage points and as a ratio.
  4. Combine parts that belong together. If two parts form a meaningful whole, add them. This is a high-band skill.
  5. Find the biggest change and the one that reverses.
  6. Group the parts that rose together and the parts that fell together.

A worked analysis of this chart:

Housing type Overall change (percentage points) Pattern
Owned outright +9 Rose in every decade
Mortgage −11 Rose to a peak in 2000, then fell
Private renting +16 Rose in every decade; more than doubled
Social housing −14 Fell in every decade; halved
Owners combined (outright + mortgage) −2 Stayed between 60% and 66%

The biggest single change is private renting (+16 points). The most interesting finding is the combined line: ownership as a whole hardly moved, although its two parts moved in opposite directions. That earns a place in the overview.

Include the biggest changes, the turning point, the stable total and any change in ranking. Leave out every middle figure, and reasons such as “because house prices rose”.

C. Writing structure for a dynamic chart

Paragraph What to write Why
Introduction Paraphrase the task: the whole, the types and the period Shows you understand the chart
Overview The main direction of change in the parts that matter, plus any stable total Gives the big picture of change
Body 1 The parts with the biggest changes, with start, end and any crossing Puts the most important change first
Body 2 The remaining parts, with any turning point Completes the picture; keeps one idea per paragraph

Group by size of change, not by the order of the key. The segment order in a stack is arbitrary.

D. Band 7+ model answer

The stacked bar chart below shows the percentage of households in one country by type of housing in 1990, 2000, 2010 and 2020.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words. (About 20 minutes)

Model answer · 155 words

The stacked bar chart shows how the proportion of households living in four types of housing changed in one country between 1990 and 2020.

Overall, private renting became much more common, while social housing declined steadily. The share of households that owned their homes, with or without a mortgage, stayed within a narrow range of 60% to 66% throughout the period.

Private renting showed the most striking change, more than doubling from 10% of households in 1990 to 26% in 2020. In contrast, social housing fell in every decade, halving from 28% to 14%. Private renting overtook social housing between 2000 and 2010.

Among owners, the picture was mixed. The proportion of homes owned outright climbed steadily from 24% to 33%, whereas households with a mortgage peaked at 40% in 2000 before falling to 27% by 2020. As a result, outright owners outnumbered mortgage holders in 2020 for the first time in the period shown.

E. The model answer explained

Paragraph 1: introduction

Sentence Why it works
“The stacked bar chart shows how the proportion of households living in four types of housing changed in one country between 1990 and 2020.” The task said the percentage of households … by type of housing; the answer says the proportion of households living in four types of housing. Shows how … changed signals a dynamic chart, and between 1990 and 2020 gives the period without listing the four dates.

Paragraph 2: overview

Sentence Why it works
“Overall, private renting became much more common, while social housing declined steadily.” The two biggest changes, in opposite directions, with no figures. Steadily is accurate: social housing fell in every decade.
“The share of households that owned their homes, with or without a mortgage, stayed within a narrow range of 60% to 66% throughout the period.” The combined total is the insight a Band 7 answer adds: two segments that moved in opposite directions add up to a stable whole (62, 66, 63, 60). Here the range is the key feature, so two figures are acceptable in an overview.

Paragraph 3: the biggest changes

Sentence Why it works
“Private renting showed the most striking change, more than doubling from 10% of households in 1990 to 26% in 2020.” A superlative names it as the biggest change (+16 points), and more than doubling gives the ratio (2.6 times) before the figures.
“In contrast, social housing fell in every decade, halving from 28% to 14%.” In every decade tells the reader about all four bars without listing the middle ones. Halving is exact (28 ÷ 2 = 14).
“Private renting overtook social housing between 2000 and 2010.” A crossing: in 2000, social housing was higher (22% against 12%); in 2010, private renting was (20% against 17%). Between is accurate because the bars are ten years apart.

Paragraph 4: owners

Sentence Why it works
“Among owners, the picture was mixed.” A short topic sentence introducing the second group.
“The proportion of homes owned outright climbed steadily from 24% to 33%, whereas households with a mortgage peaked at 40% in 2000 before falling to 27% by 2020.” Two opposite patterns in one whereas sentence. Peaked … before falling gives the turning point without listing the middle years.
“As a result, outright owners outnumbered mortgage holders in 2020 for the first time in the period shown.” A change in ranking is a key feature. For the first time in the period shown is accurate: in 2010, mortgage holders (34%) were still ahead of outright owners (29%).

How the answer meets the four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the answer
Task Achievement An overview with the two biggest changes and the stable total; the turning point, the crossing and the change in ranking are all selected; every figure and ratio is accurate; no reasons or opinions.
Coherence and Cohesion Paragraphs by size of change (renting and social housing, then owners); in contrast, whereas, as a result, among owners; the period as a reference to the dates.
Lexical Resource striking, more than doubling, halving, overtook, peaked, outnumbered; precise ratios instead of repeated increase.
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Past simple throughout; participle clauses (more than doubling, halving); whereas and before falling; a reduced clause (with or without a mortgage); accurate comparatives and a superlative.

5.3 Comparisons and rankings

A. Understanding the question

What it shows. Two or more groups (men and women, three countries, four age groups) measured on the same categories. Each group has its own ranking, and the groups can be compared with each other.

How to recognise it. Grouped bars (two or more bars per category) or a table with the groups in columns, and a key naming the groups. The task often says compares, men and women, in three countries or by age group.

What to look for. The top and bottom item in each group; where the groups agree (the same item in the same position); the biggest difference between groups; the categories where one group is clearly higher; and the categories where the groups are almost identical.

What the task asks. Select and report the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. This is the type of task where comparison is the whole point, so every paragraph needs comparing words.

Common wordings.

Wording What it means for your answer
“The chart shows the percentage of men and women who took part in six activities.” Rank each sex, then compare the sexes activity by activity
“The bar chart compares three countries in terms of …” Compare countries; group the ones that behave alike
“The table shows the average salary in five jobs, for men and women.” Compare the gap in each job; say which gap is largest
“The chart shows the results for four age groups.” Describe how the pattern changes from the youngest to the oldest

Three kinds of comparison. (1) Ranking: is the order the same or different in each group? (2) Gap: how big is the difference, in points or as a ratio? (3) Direction: which group is higher in each category?

B. How to analyse a comparison chart

  1. Rank each group separately. Write two lists. Ask: where are they the same, and where do they differ?
  2. Measure the gap for each category (the difference in points, or a ratio).
  3. Mark the direction. Which group is higher in each category?
  4. Sort the categories into three kinds: the groups are similar; group A is higher; group B is higher.
  5. Select: the shared top choice, the largest gap, the similar categories, and the exceptions.

A worked ranking for this chart:

Rank Men Women
1 Walking Walking
2 Gym Gym
3 Football Swimming
4 Cycling Running
5 Running Cycling
6 Swimming Football

The top two places are identical, which is a major feature. The biggest change in order is football: third for men and last for women.

A worked gap analysis:

Activity Gap (men minus women, percentage points) Higher group How to say it
Walking −13 Women more likely to walk; still the first choice for both
Gym +3 Men (slightly) almost identical
Football +28 Men by far the largest gap
Cycling +14 Men nearly twice as popular among men
Running +6 Men slightly more popular among men
Swimming −11 Women more popular among women

The three kinds of category are now clear: similar (gym, running), men higher (football, cycling) and women higher (walking, swimming). Those groups give you the shape of the overview.

Comparison language.

Purpose Language
Higher or lower more likely than, less common among, higher among women than men
Size of gap nearly twice as popular as, roughly half as many, a difference of 28 percentage points
Similarity almost identical, much the same, a difference of only three percentage points
Contrast whereas, while, by contrast, compared with, against
Ranking ranked second, the most popular, came last

C. Writing structure for comparison charts

Paragraph What to write Why
Introduction Paraphrase the task: the groups, the categories and the date Shows you understand the chart
Overview What the groups share (the top choice) and the main difference between them The two things the examiner looks for in a comparison
Body 1 The shared top places and similar categories, then the largest gap Starts with agreement, then the biggest contrast
Body 2 The remaining categories, grouped by which group is higher Completes the comparison with proportion

Organise by the kind of difference, not by group. Do not describe all the men's figures and then all the women's.

D. Band 7+ model answer

The bar chart below shows the percentage of men and women in one country who took part in six sports and fitness activities in 2024.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words. (About 20 minutes)

Model answer · 185 words

The bar chart compares the percentage of men and women who took part in six types of physical activity in one country in 2024.

Overall, walking was the most popular activity for both sexes, but the two groups differed markedly in their other choices. Men were much more likely to play football or cycle, whereas women were more likely to swim, and the two groups were similar in their use of gyms.

Walking attracted 68% of women and 55% of men, making it the clear first choice in both groups. Gym use ranked second for both, at 36% of men and 33% of women, a difference of only three percentage points. By contrast, football showed the largest gap: 34% of men played it, compared with only 6% of women, a difference of 28 percentage points.

The remaining activities showed further differences. Cycling was nearly twice as popular among men (31%) as among women (17%), and running was slightly more popular among men (28% against 22%). Swimming was the only activity other than walking in which participation was higher among women, at 29% compared with 18%.

E. The model answer explained

Paragraph 1: introduction

Sentence Why it works
“The bar chart compares the percentage of men and women who took part in six types of physical activity in one country in 2024.” Compares replaces shows, and types of physical activity replaces sports and fitness activities. The groups, the number of categories, the place and the date are all present, and there are no figures.

Paragraph 2: overview

Sentence Why it works
“Overall, walking was the most popular activity for both sexes, but the two groups differed markedly in their other choices.” The first half names what the groups share; the second half says the main thing the chart is about, a difference. A comparison overview needs both.
“Men were much more likely to play football or cycle, whereas women were more likely to swim, and the two groups were similar in their use of gyms.” The three kinds of category from the analysis (men higher, women higher, similar), summarised with no numbers. Whereas places the contrast inside one sentence.

Paragraph 3: agreement and the largest gap

Sentence Why it works
“Walking attracted 68% of women and 55% of men, making it the clear first choice in both groups.” Two figures in one sentence, one for each group; the participle clause making it states what the figures mean.
“Gym use ranked second for both, at 36% of men and 33% of women, a difference of only three percentage points.” The shared second place is reported once, and the closing noun phrase measures the gap. Only signals that the difference is small.
“By contrast, football showed the largest gap: 34% of men played it, compared with only 6% of women, a difference of 28 percentage points.” By contrast turns from similarity to the biggest difference. The colon introduces the evidence; compared with only sets the figures side by side; the final phrase gives the gap.

Paragraph 4: other differences

Sentence Why it works
“The remaining activities showed further differences.” A topic sentence that links to the previous paragraph.
“Cycling was nearly twice as popular among men (31%) as among women (17%), and running was slightly more popular among men (28% against 22%).” Nearly twice as popular as gives a ratio (31 ÷ 17 = 1.8); slightly more popular matches the gap of six points; against is a compact alternative to compared with.
“Swimming was the only activity other than walking in which participation was higher among women, at 29% compared with 18%.” The exception is stated with the only activity other than, which is accurate: women were higher only in walking and swimming.

How the answer meets the four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the answer
Task Achievement A clear overview of the shared and different features; the shared top places, the largest gap, the similarities and the exception are all selected; every figure and ratio is accurate.
Coherence and Cohesion Organised by kind of difference, not by group; by contrast, whereas, compared with, against, the remaining activities; each paragraph has one purpose.
Lexical Resource took part, attracted, ranked second, markedly, nearly twice as popular, participation; comparison language is varied.
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Past simple throughout; participle clauses (making it); comparatives (more likely, more popular); whereas; a relative clause (in which participation was higher); correct ratio structures (as popular among … as among …).

5.4 Diagrams with numbers and percentages

A. Understanding the question

What it shows. A flow of quantities: a total at the start, and boxes and arrows showing how it is divided and where each part goes. The usual subjects are waste, energy, money, water or time. It is different from a process diagram (steps in order, no figures) and from a pie chart (one whole, no flow).

How to recognise it. Boxes joined by arrows, with amounts or percentages attached, and a task that says shows how … is used, is divided, is distributed or what happened to ….

What to look for. The starting total and its unit; each split; the size of each branch; which branches are broken down further; whether the branches add up to the total; and the largest and smallest parts.

What the task asks. Select and report the main flows and figures, and make comparisons where relevant. You are not describing every box: you are describing how a total is divided.

Common wordings.

Wording What it means for your answer
“The diagram shows how household waste was dealt with in one city.” Describe the main split, then the detailed branch
“The diagram illustrates the distribution of a university's budget.” Say what each part accounted for
“The flow chart shows the amount of energy used in a home.” Compare the biggest and smallest uses
“The diagram shows what happened to 1,000 students after graduation.” Fractions and ratios of the 1,000

Useful language. was divided into, accounted for, made up, went to, was sent to, the remainder, of the total, the largest share. Use the passive for what happens to things: was recycled, were burned.

B. How to analyse a diagram with numbers

  1. Find the starting total and its unit.
  2. Follow each arrow and write in a few words what goes where.
  3. Check the numbers add up. A quick sum prevents misreading. In this diagram, the first split is 420 + 380 + 200 = 1,000, and the detailed branch is 180 + 90 + 80 + 70 = 420. If the numbers do not add up, you have misread an arrow.
  4. Convert amounts to shares if the diagram gives only amounts, and rank the branches.
  5. Find the largest and smallest at each stage, and the stage with the most detail.
  6. Select the main split and the one branch the diagram breaks down, and compare within them.

Percentages in a diagram. A percentage may be a share of the starting total or a share of its own branch. Read the note under the diagram. Here the percentages are shares of all the waste collected, so paper is 18% of the total, not 18% of the recycled material. Repeating that distinction in your answer shows the examiner that you read the diagram accurately.

Include the starting total, the main split with its figures, the largest part and any detailed branch. Leave out reasons (“because people care about the environment”) and any box you cannot link to the rest.

C. Writing structure for a diagram with numbers

Paragraph What to write Why
Introduction Say what the diagram shows, with the starting total, the place and the year Sets the scale for every figure
Overview The biggest branch and the key fact about the detail The big picture of the flow
Body 1 The first split with amounts and percentages, compared Describes the main flow
Body 2 The detailed branch, with the largest part compared against the others Shows the second level

Group by level of the diagram (the first split, then the detail) and not by each box.

D. Band 7+ model answer

The diagram below shows what happened to the household waste collected in one city in 2024.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words. (About 20 minutes)

Model answer · 155 words

The diagram shows what happened to 1,000 tonnes of household waste collected in one city in 2024, and how much of it was recycled.

Overall, recycling was the most common way of dealing with the waste, accounting for just over two-fifths of the total, while landfill and incineration together handled the rest. Paper made up the largest part of the recycled material.

Of the 1,000 tonnes collected, 420 tonnes (42%) were recycled, 380 tonnes (38%) went to landfill, and the remaining 200 tonnes (20%) were burned to produce energy. Recycling therefore handled more than twice as much waste as incineration.

The recycled material consisted of four types. Paper was the largest category, at 180 tonnes, which was twice the amount of glass (90 tonnes) and represented 18% of all the waste collected. Metal and plastic accounted for 80 and 70 tonnes respectively, so even together (150 tonnes) they amounted to less than the paper alone.

E. The model answer explained

Paragraph 1: introduction

Sentence Why it works
“The diagram shows what happened to 1,000 tonnes of household waste collected in one city in 2024, and how much of it was recycled.” It gives the starting total and unit, the subject, the place and the year. The second clause previews the detailed branch, so the reader knows the diagram has two levels.

Paragraph 2: overview

Sentence Why it works
“Overall, recycling was the most common way of dealing with the waste, accounting for just over two-fifths of the total, while landfill and incineration together handled the rest.” The largest branch, expressed as a fraction (42% is just over two-fifths) so that no exact figure is needed. Adding the other two branches (38% + 20% = 58%) gives the whole picture in one clause.
“Paper made up the largest part of the recycled material.” The key fact about the detailed branch, in one sentence.

Paragraph 3: the first split

Sentence Why it works
“Of the 1,000 tonnes collected, 420 tonnes (42%) were recycled, 380 tonnes (38%) went to landfill, and the remaining 200 tonnes (20%) were burned to produce energy.” All three branches in one sentence, with both amount and percentage in brackets. The sentence paraphrases the labels on the diagram (incinerated for energy becomes burned to produce energy; landfill becomes went to landfill), and it uses the passive correctly because the actor is irrelevant.
“Recycling therefore handled more than twice as much waste as incineration.” A ratio that compares two branches (420 ÷ 200 = 2.1). Therefore is accurate here because it states an arithmetical consequence of the figures just given.

Paragraph 4: the detailed branch

Sentence Why it works
“The recycled material consisted of four types.” A topic sentence that introduces the second level.
“Paper was the largest category, at 180 tonnes, which was twice the amount of glass (90 tonnes) and represented 18% of all the waste collected.” A ratio (180 ÷ 90 = 2) and the correct reading of the percentage: 18% of all the waste, as the note under the diagram says.
“Metal and plastic accounted for 80 and 70 tonnes respectively, so even together (150 tonnes) they amounted to less than the paper alone.” The two smaller parts are grouped, and their sum (80 + 70 = 150) is compared with the largest part. Respectively pairs two names with two figures.

How the answer meets the four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the answer
Task Achievement A clear overview of the main flow and the detail; all three branches and the largest sub-branch are reported; every figure is accurate and the sums check; percentages are read correctly.
Coherence and Cohesion Organised by level (the first split, then the recycled branch), not box by box; overall, therefore, so, respectively, of the total; a topic sentence for each new level.
Lexical Resource accounting for, handled, went to, consisted of, made up, amounted to, burned to produce energy; labels paraphrased.
Grammatical Range and Accuracy The passive (were recycled, were burned); relative clauses (which was twice the amount); participle clauses (accounting for); so even together as a reduction; fractions and ratios used accurately.
