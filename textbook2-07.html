Module 7: Problem and solution essays

A problem and solution essay has two main parts, and the second part is where students fail. They describe problems well, then offer one vague solution at the end (the government should do something). This module teaches you to build solutions that match the problems and are specific enough to convince the reader.

A. Understanding the question

The statement describes a situation that is a problem or is becoming one. The instruction asks you about the problem and what to do.

Version Typical wording Part 1 Part 2
Problems and solutions What problems does this cause? What can be done? Problems Solutions
Causes and solutions What are the causes of this problem? What solutions can you suggest? Causes Solutions
Effects and solutions What are the effects of this? How can they be reduced? Effects Solutions
Responsibility Who should take action? What can be done? Who should act What they should do

There are two main parts, and both must be developed. The official guidance says a main part that is only partly answered keeps Task Response at Band 5. A good balance is about 40% on the problems (or causes) and 60% on the solutions, because the solutions need more explanation.

The link rule. Every solution must answer a problem you have described. If your problem is loneliness, a solution about cheaper housing does not fit.

Read the exact words. What problems does this cause? asks for effects. Writing about why the problem started answers a different question (causes). Module 2 shows how to find this in the focus.

B. Planning the answer

Step 1: find the problems or causes. Use the idea tools from Module 3. Look for two different problems, for instance one for individuals and one for society.

Step 2: for each problem, ask "What would fix it? Who can act?" Write a solution next to every problem.

Who can act Typical actions
Governments Laws, taxes, funding, public campaigns
Schools Lessons, rules, activities
Companies and employers Policies, products, working conditions
Families Rules, example, time together
Individuals Changing habits, joining groups
The media Information, awareness campaigns

Step 3: check each solution.

Question If the answer is no...
Does it match a problem I described? Change it, or add the missing problem
Is it specific (who, what, how)? Add the detail
Is it realistic? Choose a smaller, more practical step
Can I explain why it works in one sentence? Choose a different solution

Weak and strong solutions

Weak Strong
The government should do something about it. Governments should fund after-school clubs in poorer areas, because children with supervised activities spend less time unsupervised on the street.
People must change their attitude. Public campaigns on television and social media could show the effects of the problem and so change habits gradually.
We should stop using cars. Cities could build safe cycle lanes and cheaper bus routes, so that people have a convenient alternative to driving.

Three useful plans

Plan Body 1 Body 2
1. Two problems, two solutions Problem 1 + problem 2, each with a short example Solution 1 + solution 2, each matched to a problem
2. One main problem in depth, two solutions One problem with explanation and example Two solutions that address it
3. Causes and solutions Two causes, each explained Two solutions, each aimed at a cause

A completed plan (Plan 1)

Many people now spend most of their free time on smartphones and other screens. What problems does this cause? What can be done to reduce the problem?

Notes
Question T: screen use. F: problems caused, and solutions. I: problem and solution, 2 parts. L: most of their free time
Introduction Paraphrase. Outline: problems for health and relationships; education and rules can help
Body 1: problems 1. Health: little exercise, poor sleep, tiredness. 2. Relationships: families look at phones during dinner, children communicate less well face to face
Body 2: solutions 1. Schools teach balanced use; parents set rules (answers both problems). 2. Phone companies provide usage tools; employers promote breaks (answers health)
Conclusion Both problems are serious but can be reduced by education and practical rules

C. Writing structure

Paragraph Content Frames
Introduction Topic, and an outline that names both parts This trend causes... but I believe that... can reduce them.
Body 1: problems Problem 1 with explanation and a short example. Problem 2 in the same way The first problem is that... / A second problem is that... / This can lead to...
Body 2: solutions Solution 1 with a reason, solution 2 with a reason, and what would happen if they were used Several measures could help. / Firstly,... Secondly,... / If these steps were combined,... would...
Conclusion Both parts in brief In conclusion,... However,... can help...

Language for each part

Function Phrases
Problems and effects One serious problem is... / This can lead to... / As a result,... / This puts pressure on... / The consequences include...
Causes This is largely due to... / One of the main causes is... / This stems from... / A major factor is...
Solutions One way to tackle this problem is to... / A practical step would be to... / Governments could... / It would be helpful if...
Results of a solution This would make it easier for... / In this way,... / If these steps were taken,... would...

Grammar for solutions. Use modal verbs (should, could, might) and second conditionals (If schools taught..., pupils would...). They sound careful and academic, and they are exactly the structures the descriptors reward at Band 7.

Common mistakes in problem and solution essays

Mistake Fix
Three paragraphs about problems, one line about solutions Aim for 40% problems and 60% solutions
A solution that does not match any problem Check the link rule
The government should in every sentence Vary the actors: schools, companies, families, the media
Solutions that are impossible or extreme (ban all cars) Use realistic steps
Writing causes when the question asks for effects Check the exact words of the question
Not explaining why a solution would work Add because or so that

D. Band 7+ model essay

Many people now spend most of their free time on smartphones and other screens. What problems does this cause? What can be done to reduce the problem?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Model answer · 272 words

Smartphones, tablets and computers now take up a large part of people's free time, particularly among the young. This trend causes problems for both physical health and personal relationships, but I believe that education and practical rules can reduce them.

The first problem is that long hours in front of a screen harm physical health. People who sit for hours tend to take less exercise, and they often sleep badly because the light from screens disturbs their sleep. Over time, these habits can lead to weight gain, tiredness and difficulty concentrating at work or school. A second problem is that screens weaken real relationships. Families who eat dinner while looking at their phones, for example, have fewer conversations, and children may learn to communicate less well face to face.

Several measures could help. Firstly, schools should teach pupils how to use technology in a balanced way, because young people who understand the risks are more likely to limit their own use. Parents can support this by setting clear rules, such as no phones at meals or in bedrooms after a certain time. Secondly, governments and companies could encourage healthier habits. Phone makers, for instance, already offer tools that show how long users spend on each application, and employers could promote breaks and outdoor activities during working hours. If these steps were combined, people would find it easier to enjoy technology without letting it control their lives.

In conclusion, excessive screen use damages both health and relationships. However, education at school and at home, together with sensible rules and tools from technology companies, can help people to use screens in a healthier way.

E. The model essay explained

Paragraph by paragraph

Paragraph What it does Why it works
1. Introduction (40 words) Paraphrases the topic and names both parts: problems (health, relationships) and the kind of solution (education, rules) The outline tells the examiner that both main parts will be answered
2. Body 1: problems (89 words) Problem 1: health (explanation: little exercise, poor sleep, then results). Problem 2: relationships (example: family dinners) Two different problems, each with a reason and an example. This is about 40% of the essay
3. Body 2: solutions (107 words) Solution 1: schools and parents (education and rules). Solution 2: companies and employers (tools and breaks). Ends with what would happen if the steps were combined Each solution has a reason or an example. The actors vary (schools, parents, governments, companies, employers)
4. Conclusion (36 words) Restates both parts in different words No new ideas

Do the solutions match the problems? Yes. The table shows the link.

Problem Solution that answers it
Poor health (little exercise, poor sleep) Employers promote breaks and outdoor activity; phone tools show use; parents set bedtime rules for phones
Weaker relationships Parents ban phones at meals; schools teach balanced use

The four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the essay What would push it further
Task Response Both parts are answered and developed. Problems and solutions are linked. The solutions are specific A short example for a solution, such as a school that already teaches this
Coherence and Cohesion Clear signals: The first problem..., A second problem..., Firstly..., Secondly.... Reference: these habits, these steps, they. Each paragraph has one purpose A wider range of linking words in Body 1
Lexical Resource physical health, disturbs their sleep, weaken real relationships, take up a large part of, encourage healthier habits, limit their own use Some more topic-specific nouns (screen time, digital habits)
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Relative clauses (who understand the risks, that show how long). Conditional (If these steps were combined, people would find it easier...). Modals (could help, should teach, may learn). Passive (were combined). Comparative (more likely, healthier) More complex noun phrases and some participle clauses (Module 12)

F. Try it yourself

In many countries, young people find it difficult to get a job after they graduate. What are the causes of this problem, and what can be done to solve it?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

  1. Analyse (3 minutes). What type of question is it? What are the two main parts? (Hint: causes and solutions, so use Plan 3.)
  2. Plan (5 minutes). List two causes. Write a solution beside each. Check the link rule.
  3. Write (27 minutes) and check (5 minutes).
  4. Compare with the sample plan.
Show the sample plan

Your plan may differ and still be good.

  • Analysis. T: graduate unemployment. F: causes, and solutions. I: causes and solutions, 2 parts. L: young people, after they graduate.
  • Cause 1: a gap between what universities teach and what employers need (example: graduates with theory but no practical or digital skills). Solution 1: universities and employers could create work placements and joint courses.
  • Cause 2: too many graduates apply for too few jobs in popular fields. Solution 2: governments could support small businesses and guide students towards areas with real demand.
  • Conclusion. The causes are a skills gap and a shortage of jobs. Practical training and economic support would address both.

More problem and solution questions for practice

  1. In many cities, rubbish and plastic waste are increasing. What are the causes, and what solutions can you suggest?
  2. More and more people suffer from stress at work. What problems does this cause for individuals and employers? What can be done?
  3. Many children spend little time playing outdoors. What are the effects on children? How can the problem be solved?
  4. In some countries, many people over 65 live alone. What problems does this cause? What can be done?
  5. Fewer young people are choosing to learn traditional crafts. What are the causes, and what could be done to keep these skills alive?
  6. Noise is a serious problem in many large cities. What are its effects on people, and what can be done to reduce it?
