Module 14: Practice exercises with answer keys

This module is a workbook. Parts A to F train one skill at a time, in short exercises you can do in 10 or 15 minutes. Part G gives six full essay tasks, one for each essay type. Do the short exercises first, check your answers, and then do the full tasks under timed conditions.

A. Question types and main parts

For each question, write the type (opinion, discussion, problem and solution, advantages and disadvantages, two-part, mixed) and the number of main parts.

  1. Some people think that sport should be compulsory in schools. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  2. Many people prefer to live in a house with a garden, while others prefer an apartment. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
  3. In many countries, traffic accidents are increasing. What are the causes, and what can be done?
  4. More people now travel abroad on holiday. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this?
  5. Some people say that children who are given too many toys become spoilt. Do you agree or disagree?
  6. More and more people are learning English. Why is this? Is it a positive or negative development?
  7. Do the advantages of online dating outweigh the disadvantages?
  8. Some people think that the government should control what children watch on television. Others think that parents are responsible. Which view do you agree with? What can be done to protect children?
  9. What will be the main changes in education over the next twenty years? Will these changes be mainly positive or negative?
  10. The overuse of antibiotics is a growing problem. What problems does it cause, and what can be done?
Show the answer key
  1. Opinion. 1 part (how far you agree).
  2. Discussion. 3 parts (view A, view B, your opinion).
  3. Problem and solution (causes and solutions). 2 parts.
  4. Advantages and disadvantages. 2 parts.
  5. Opinion. 1 part (a clear position on whether too many toys spoil children).
  6. Two-part. 2 parts (reasons, and a judgement).
  7. Advantages and disadvantages (outweigh). 3 parts (advantages, disadvantages, judgement).
  8. Mixed. 2 parts (which view, what can be done). Plan 1 from Module 10.
  9. Two-part (future and judgement). 2 parts (the main changes, and whether they are positive or negative).
  10. Problem and solution (problems and solutions). 2 parts.

B. Introductions

Write an introduction (two or three sentences, 40 to 55 words) for each question. Paraphrase the topic, state the view, and give your position or an outline.

  1. Some people think that all students should learn to cook at school. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
  2. Many people prefer to live in a house with a garden, while others prefer an apartment. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
  3. Obesity is increasing in many countries. What are the causes of this, and what can be done?
  4. More and more people are learning English. Why is this? Is it a positive or negative development?
Show the answer key

Your wording will differ. Models:

  1. Cooking is a basic life skill, yet many young people leave school without learning it. Some people believe that every student should be taught to cook. I largely agree with this view, because cooking lessons promote healthy eating and independence.
  2. Where to live is an important choice for families, and opinions differ on the best type of home. Some people value the space and privacy of a house with a garden, while others prefer the convenience of an apartment. This essay will discuss both views, but I believe that apartments suit most city residents better.
  3. Rising levels of obesity are a serious concern for many governments. This essay will explain the main causes of the problem, which are poor diet and lack of exercise, and suggest practical steps that could reduce it.
  4. English is now studied by millions of people around the world, and this number continues to grow. There are several reasons for this trend, and I believe that it is mainly a positive development.

C. Topic sentences

Write a topic sentence for a body paragraph about each idea. The sentence should state the main idea, not just the topic.

  1. Free healthcare protects poor families from the cost of illness.
  2. Heavy use of technology reduces face-to-face contact.
  3. Public transport is cheaper than owning a car.
  4. Learning a musical instrument improves children's concentration.
  5. Tourism can damage local cultures.
Show the answer key

Answers vary. Models:

  1. The main benefit of free healthcare is that it protects low-income families from the high cost of illness.
  2. A second problem is that heavy use of technology reduces face-to-face contact.
  3. Public transport is also a much cheaper way to travel than owning a car.
  4. Learning a musical instrument can improve children's concentration in several ways.
  5. One serious disadvantage of tourism is that it can damage local cultures.

D. Conclusions

Write a conclusion (30 to 45 words) for each essay summary. Restate the position and the main reasons. Do not add new ideas.

  1. Question: Should museums be free? Position: yes. Reasons: fairness (low-income families can visit), and more children develop an interest in culture.
  2. Question: Do the advantages of working from home outweigh the disadvantages? Position: yes. Reasons: saves time and money; flexibility. Limit: loneliness.
  3. Question: What can be done about traffic congestion? Position: a combination of measures. Reasons: better public transport; charges for city-centre driving.
Show the answer key

Your wording will differ. Models:

  1. In conclusion, museums should be free, because free entry is fairer to low-income families and encourages more children to develop an interest in culture.
  2. In conclusion, although home working can be lonely, I believe that its advantages outweigh the disadvantages, because it saves time and money and gives employees greater flexibility.
  3. To sum up, traffic congestion can be reduced by a combination of better public transport and charges for driving in city centres, which together make private cars a less attractive choice.

E. Correct the errors

This paragraph has ten errors in grammar, word choice, register and punctuation. Find and correct them.

Nowadays, many peoples prefer to live in cities because there is more jobs. The government should builds more houses, which are affordable. Also, public transport is very important, it reduces the traffic. However, living in a city have some disadvantages. For example the pollution is a big problem, and many kids suffer from breathing problem. In my opinion, the cities will be better place if governments make more green spaces.

Show the answer key
Error Correction
many peoples many people (people is already plural)
there is more jobs there are more jobs
should builds should build (base verb after a modal)
very important, it reduces the traffic (comma splice) very important because it reduces traffic
living in a city have living in a city has (the subject is living, which is singular)
For example the pollution For example, pollution (comma, and no article for a general noun)
a big problem a serious problem (a more formal word)
kids children (informal word)
breathing problem breathing problems (plural)
the cities will be better place cities will be a better place (no article before general cities; add a before better place)

Corrected paragraph. Nowadays, many people prefer to live in cities because there are more jobs. The government should build more houses that are affordable. Also, public transport is very important because it reduces traffic. However, living in a city has some disadvantages. For example, pollution is a serious problem, and many children suffer from breathing problems. In my opinion, cities will be a better place if governments create more green spaces.

F. Put the sentences in order

These six sentences form one body paragraph. Put them in the right order.

  • (a) For this reason, schools should offer short and practical courses on money management.
  • (b) Many young people leave school unable to manage a budget or understand loans.
  • (c) The main reason for teaching financial skills at school is that young adults make serious money decisions early.
  • (d) A student who takes out a loan without understanding the interest, for instance, may repay far more than expected.
  • (e) This lack of knowledge can lead to debt that lasts for many years.
  • (f) Parents cannot always teach these skills, because many do not have the knowledge themselves.
Show the answer key

c, b, d, e, f, a. (c: topic sentence. b: explanation. d: example. e: result. f: extra support. a: link back to the recommendation.)

G. Six full tasks

Do one task a week, in the order given. For each task, follow the 40-minute plan from Module 1: read (3), plan (5), write (27) and check (5). After writing, mark your essay with the checklist from Module 16, and record the result in your progress log from Module 1. Do not read the sample plan until you have finished.

W1: Opinion

Some people think that the best way to reduce crime is to give stronger punishments. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

W2: Discussion

Some people believe that children should learn at home with their parents. Others believe that school is the best place for children to learn. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

W3: Problem and solution

In many countries, farmland is being used to build houses and factories. What problems does this cause? What can be done?

W4: Advantages and disadvantages

More companies now offer a four-day working week. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?

W5: Two-part

Many people buy cheap clothes and throw them away after a few weeks. Why do people do this? Is it a positive or negative trend?

W6: Mixed

Some people think that international aid is the best way to help poorer countries. Others think that trade is more effective. Which view do you agree with? What can richer countries do?

Sample plans. Your plan may differ and still be good. Check that yours has the same parts.

  • W1 (opinion). Position: partly agree. Strong punishments deter some crimes (example: heavy fines for dangerous driving reduce accidents), but they do not deal with causes. Second body: education, job training and community programmes address poverty and unemployment, so fewer people turn to crime (example: youth clubs). Conclusion: punishment has a role, but prevention works better.
  • W2 (discussion). View A (home): individual attention, flexible timetable, safe environment (example: a child with learning difficulties). View B (school): social skills, trained teachers, more subjects and equipment (example: science laboratories). Opinion: school is better for most children, but home learning suits some families. Conclusion: both have benefits, but school gives wider opportunities.
  • W3 (problem and solution). Problems: less food production and higher food prices; loss of wildlife habitats and green space. Solutions: governments could protect good farmland by law and build on unused or damaged land first; cities could build taller, denser housing to save space. Conclusion: planning rules can protect farmland while still providing homes.
  • W4 (outweigh). Judgement: advantages outweigh. Body 1 (disadvantages, the weaker side): longer working days can be tiring; some businesses may find it hard to serve customers on the fifth day. Body 2 (advantages, with weighing): better work-life balance, less stress, lower travel costs, and often higher productivity (example: a worker who spends the extra day with family returns refreshed). Conclusion: the benefits are greater, if businesses plan cover carefully.
  • W5 (two-part). Why: cheap clothes are easy to afford; fashion changes quickly and advertising encourages people to keep buying; clothing is made so cheaply that repairing it seems pointless. Positive or negative? Mainly negative: it creates large amounts of waste and uses many resources (example: heaps of unwanted clothes in landfill); workers may be poorly paid. Limit: low prices help families on low incomes. Conclusion: the habit is understandable but harmful.
  • W6 (mixed). View: a mixture, with trade more effective in the long term. Aid is useful in emergencies (example: after a flood) but can create dependence. Trade creates jobs and builds skills. What can richer countries do? Reduce trade barriers on goods from poorer countries; offer training and technology; spend aid on projects such as schools and clean water that help countries to earn their own income. Conclusion: aid for emergencies, trade for long-term growth.

H. How to use these tasks

Week Task Focus
1 W1 (opinion) A clear position in the introduction and the conclusion
2 W2 (discussion) Fair space for both views, and a clear opinion
3 W3 (problem and solution) Solutions that match the problems
4 W4 (outweigh) Weighing the two sides, not just listing
5 W5 (two-part) A full paragraph for each question
6 W6 (mixed) Counting the parts and mapping the paragraphs

After six weeks, repeat the type that gave you the lowest score on the checklist, using the practice questions at the end of Modules 5 to 10.
