Module 6: Discussion essays

A discussion essay asks you to do three things at once: explain one view, explain another, and say what you think. Students who describe only the two views, or who give an opinion but ignore one view, leave a main part unanswered. This module shows how to cover all three.

A. Understanding the question

The statement gives two views, usually Some people think... Others believe.... The instruction asks you to discuss them.

Instruction What it asks
Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. Explain both views, then give your opinion
Discuss both views. What is your opinion? The same
Which view do you agree with? Explain both briefly, then choose one
Discuss both views. Explain both views. In most tests an opinion is also asked for. If it is not, end with a clear overall judgement, because Band 7 expects a clear position

There are three main parts: view A, view B, and your opinion. If you leave out any one, Task Response will be limited.

What "discuss" means. It does not mean "list". For each view, give the reasons that people hold it, with a short explanation and an example. You are not agreeing with the view; you are explaining it fairly.

Your opinion. You can:

  • agree with view A,
  • agree with view B, or
  • take a clear middle position, such as both are important, but X matters more.

Whichever you choose, it must be clear. A reader should be able to state your opinion in one sentence.

Fair coverage. Both views need real development. If one view has two sentences and the other has eight, the essay looks unbalanced and the first main part is only partly answered.

B. Planning the answer

Two plans work well. Choose by how much time you have and how many ideas you can develop.

Plan Paragraphs Use when
A. Four paragraphs Introduction (both views and your opinion) · Body 1: view A · Body 2: view B, with your opinion · Conclusion Most questions. This is the safest plan for 40 minutes
B. Five paragraphs Introduction · Body 1: view A · Body 2: view B · Body 3: your opinion with its own reasons · Conclusion When you have strong reasons for your opinion and you can write fast. Keep each body paragraph at 70 words or more

Finding reasons for each view. Ask the question Why do people think this? Imagine you are a person who holds the view and explain their reasons. Use the idea tools from Module 3 (who is affected, which area, time).

A completed plan (Plan A)

Some people believe that university students should choose subjects that will help them to find a job. Others think that students should study the subjects they are interested in. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Notes
Question T: choosing a degree. F: job prospects versus interest. I: discussion, 3 parts. L: should
Opinion Interest should be the more important factor
Introduction Paraphrase both views. Outline: both views, opinion for interest
Body 1: view A (jobs) Reason: a degree is an expensive investment. Explanation: fees and living costs; debt if no job. Example: medicine, engineering, accounting. Result: security, especially for poor families
Body 2: view B (interest) + opinion Reason: motivation produces better results. Example: a history student who enjoys reading gets higher grades. Job markets change. Opinion: curiosity and study habits last longer. Add practical skills (languages, computing)
Conclusion Practical degrees offer security, but interest should come first because motivation leads to success

C. Writing structure

Paragraph Sentences Frames
Introduction 1. Topic in your own words 2. View A and view B 3. Outline and opinion Some people believe that..., while others argue that... / This essay will discuss both views, but I believe that...
Body 1 (view A) Topic sentence, explanation, example, result Those who favour... argue that... / Supporters of... point out that... / One argument in favour of... is that...
Body 2 (view B) Topic sentence, explanation, example, result, then your opinion On the other hand,... / Others, however, believe that... / For this reason, I believe that...
Conclusion Both views in brief, then your opinion In conclusion, although..., I believe that..., since...

Verbs for reporting views. Use these when you explain what other people think:

argue, claim, maintain, point out, suggest, hold the view that, take the position that

Say Supporters of this view argue that... and not This is true or This is wrong. In the view paragraphs you are an observer. Save your own voice for the opinion.

Phrases for your opinion. I believe that... / In my view,... / From my perspective,... / Personally, I would argue that.... Use them once or twice, not in every sentence.

Common mistakes in discussion essays

Mistake Fix
Describing both views and giving no opinion Write your opinion in the introduction and the conclusion
One view long and one view short Give each view about the same number of words
Writing In my opinion inside the view paragraphs Use reporting verbs: argue, point out, claim
Writing a personal story instead of reasons Use a short personal example only to support a reason
A conclusion that changes the opinion Match the introduction

D. Band 7+ model essay

Some people believe that university students should choose subjects that will help them to find a job. Others think that students should study the subjects they are interested in.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Model answer · 277 words

Choosing a university subject is one of the most important decisions in a young person's life. Some people believe that students should select courses that lead directly to employment, while others argue that students should follow their own interests. This essay will discuss both views, but I believe that interest should be the more important factor.

Those who favour practical subjects argue that a degree is an expensive investment and should therefore lead to a secure career. Tuition fees and living costs can place a heavy burden on families, and graduates who cannot find work may struggle to repay their debts. Medicine, engineering and accounting, for example, usually offer clear career paths and reliable salaries. For students from poorer families in particular, this security can be extremely valuable, because they cannot afford to take risks with their future.

On the other hand, supporters of interest-based study believe that motivation produces better results. A student who enjoys history, for instance, is likely to read more widely, attend more classes and achieve higher grades than one who chose a course only for its salary. Moreover, job markets change quickly, and skills that are in demand today may be less important in ten years. For this reason, I believe that curiosity and strong study habits are more useful over a lifetime than a choice based on current fashion. Students can also add practical skills, such as languages or computing, to almost any degree, which reduces the risk of unemployment.

In conclusion, although a practical degree offers security, I believe that students should choose the subjects that interest them, since motivation leads to success and useful skills can be added later.

E. The model essay explained

Paragraph by paragraph

Paragraph What it does Why it works
1. Introduction (56 words) Paraphrases the topic, states both views, and gives a clear opinion in the last sentence The examiner knows from the start that all three main parts are coming
2. Body 1: view A (82 words) Reason: a degree is an expensive investment. Explanation: fees and debt. Example: medicine, engineering, accounting. Result: security for poorer families Written in a neutral voice (Those who favour... argue that...). It explains the view fairly without agreeing
3. Body 2: view B and opinion (108 words) Reason: motivation produces better results. Example: a history student. Second reason: changing job markets. Then the writer's opinion (For this reason, I believe...) and a way to reduce the risk View B gets about the same space as view A. The opinion arrives after both views have been explained, so it is supported
4. Conclusion (31 words) Acknowledges the other view (although...) and restates the opinion with the main reason One long sentence, no new ideas

The four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the essay What would push it further
Task Response All three main parts are answered. Both views are developed with reasons and examples. The opinion is clear in paragraph 1, paragraph 3 and paragraph 4 A more specific example for view B (for instance a named subject and what the student does)
Coherence and Cohesion Four paragraphs, each with one purpose. Clear signals: Those who favour..., On the other hand..., For this reason..., In conclusion.... Reference words: this security, one who, they Vary linking inside paragraphs. Two sentences begin with For
Lexical Resource Collocations: expensive investment, secure career, heavy burden, clear career paths, reliable salaries, interest-based study, job markets change quickly One or two more idiomatic items could be added
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Relative clauses (who cannot find work, that lead directly to employment). Comparatives (more widely, higher grades than, more useful... than). Passive and modal verbs (can place, may struggle, can be added). Contrast (although, while, whereas) More inverted or participle structures (Module 12)

F. Try it yourself

Some people think that office workers should work from home whenever they can. Others believe that it is better to work in an office with colleagues.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

  1. Analyse (3 minutes): write T, F, I (number of main parts) and L.
  2. Plan (5 minutes): list two or three reasons for each view. Choose your opinion. Choose Plan A or Plan B.
  3. Write (27 minutes) and check (5 minutes).
  4. Compare your plan with the sample plan.
Show the sample plan

Your plan may differ and still be good.

  • Analysis. T: where to work. F: home working versus office working. I: discussion, 3 parts. L: whenever they can.
  • View A (home). Saves commuting time and money (example: a two-hour daily journey). Gives flexibility for family life. Fewer interruptions, so more focused work.
  • View B (office). Colleagues share ideas faster and learn from one another (example: a new employee asks a quick question). Clear separation between work and home life. Team spirit and company culture.
  • Opinion (a middle position). A mix is best: two or three days in the office for teamwork and the rest at home for focused work.
  • Conclusion. Both ways of working have clear benefits, but a balance gives the advantages of each.

More discussion questions for practice

  1. Some people believe that children should start school at a very early age. Others think that they should begin later. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
  2. Some people think that international sports events, such as the Olympic Games, bring countries together. Others believe they increase competition. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
  3. Some people prefer to read news in newspapers and magazines, while others prefer to get news online. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
  4. Some parents think that children should have their own rooms, while others believe children should share. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
  5. Some people believe that famous people should be allowed a private life. Others think the public has a right to know about them. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
  6. Some people think that the best way to learn about a country is to visit it. Others believe that books and films are better. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
