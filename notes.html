How Task 1 is marked

Four criteria, each worth a quarter of your Task 1 score. Click a card to see what the examiner looks for.

You do not need every number. The examiner wants the key features, chosen well.
2

The four-paragraph structure

1
Introduction: one sentence
Paraphrase the task: what the chart shows, who or where, and when.The bar chart compares how many hours a day school pupils in three countries spend sleeping, studying and relaxing.
2
Overview: one or two sentences
The big picture. No detailed numbers. Start with “Overall,”.Overall, Australian pupils devote far more time to studying than those in the other two countries.
3
Details 1: three or four sentences
One group of similar information, with numbers and comparisons.American and Canadian pupils have very similar routines…
4
Details 2: three or four sentences
The other group, focusing on contrasts.Australia, by contrast, stands out…
Aim for 160–190 words. A report without a clear overview cannot reach the higher bands for Task Achievement.

Overview phrases

Overall, Australian pupils study far more than pupils in the other two countries, while routines in the US and Canada are more balanced.

Describing numbers

WordUse it withExample
the number ofthings you can countthe number of students
the amount oftime and uncountable thingsthe amount of time spent sleeping
the proportion / percentage / share ofpercentagesthe proportion of students who spoke Hindi
percentage pointsthe gap between two percentagesHindi (30%) was 15 percentage points lower than English (45%).
The proportion of time spent sleeping is 8.5 hours.
The amount of time spent sleeping is 8.5 hours.

Also useful: stood at, reached, accounted for, made up, followed by, approximately, roughly, just over, just under.

Fill-in template

A guide to structure, not a sentence to memorise.

The [chart] [compares / illustrates] [what is measured] [who / where] [when].
Overall, [main feature 1], while [main feature 2].
[Group A] [data], [comparison with group B].
By contrast, [the different item] [data], [comparison].

Common mistakes

The bar chart shows a comparison of the daily routines of school pupils…
The bar chart compares how many hours a day school pupils… (paraphrase)
I think Australian pupils study too much.
Australian pupils study for 13 hours a day. (facts only)
Pupils in the US and Canada sleep for 8.5 hours.
American pupils sleep for 8.5 hours, Canadian pupils for 8. (check every number)
In 2007, English is the most common language.
In 2007, English was the most common language. (past data, past tense)
The number increased. The number increased again.
The number rose, and the figure then climbed further. (vary your words)
