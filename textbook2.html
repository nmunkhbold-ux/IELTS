Writing Task 2 textbook

This textbook takes pre-intermediate (B1) students towards Band 7+ in IELTS Academic Writing Task 2, one essay type at a time. It is written so that a student can learn to plan, write and check an essay using the book alone, and so that a teacher can use it as a course. All questions, essays and examples are original.

How this textbook works

Why Task 2 matters most. The whole Writing test lasts one hour. Task 1 needs at least 150 words in about 20 minutes. Task 2 needs at least 250 words in about 40 minutes and is worth two thirds of the Writing mark. Four criteria are used, and they count equally: Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. The essay answers a point of view, an argument or a problem. It is written in full paragraphs (no bullet points or sub-headings), in an academic or semi-formal style, and you support your ideas with relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. You do not need specialist knowledge. (IELTS Writing test preparation resources)

Every essay-type module (Modules 5 to 10) uses the same six parts:

Part What it does
A Understanding the question The wording of this question type and the main parts you must answer
B Planning the answer How to find and choose ideas in five minutes
C Writing structure Paragraph by paragraph, with sentence frames
D Band 7+ model essay A full essay of about 270 to 300 words on an original question
E The model essay explained What each paragraph does and how it meets the four criteria
F Try it yourself A practice question with a planning grid and a sample plan to check against

Modules 1 to 4 teach the foundations that every essay needs. Modules 11 and 12 teach language. Modules 13 to 16 are for practice, correction and the final mock test.

A four-step routine for any essay-type module:

  1. Students read the model essay (part D) once for ideas, then again with a pen, underlining the position, the main ideas and the examples.
  2. Students compare their notes with part E and find one thing they would not have done themselves.
  3. Students do part F in five minutes without looking at the sample plan, then compare.
  4. Students write the essay in 40 minutes, check it with the checklist in Module 16, and rewrite the weakest paragraph.

If you study alone, follow Modules 1 to 4 in order, then one essay-type module a week (Modules 5 to 10), using Modules 11 and 12 for ten minutes at the start of each session. Aim for two timed essays a week, and keep the error log from Module 15. Mark your own essays with the checklists, but ask a teacher to mark at least one essay in every four, because it is hard to see your own recurring errors.

About the band labels. Bands in this book (for example "Band 7+ model essay") are teaching estimates written against the public IELTS descriptors, not official marks. Nobody with examiner experience has reviewed this book. Read the official descriptors yourself: IELTS Writing band descriptors (PDF). The sources used are listed at the end of the book.

For students working alone. Follow the route in the About section: Modules 1 to 4 in order, then one essay-type module a week. Write two timed essays a week, mark them with the checklist in Module 16, and keep the error log from Module 15. Ask a teacher to mark at least one essay in every four.

How to give feedback on an essay. Mark one criterion at a time, not everything at once. Give each essay three comments: one strength (quote it), one target (the single habit that most limits the score), and one rewrite (show how one sentence could be better). Students improve faster when they act on one target a week than when they receive twenty corrections.

What the book does not do. It teaches Task 2 only. For Task 1 (the 150-word report on a graph, chart, table, process or map) use the companion book, IELTS Academic Writing Task 1: Classroom Textbook. Remember that the Writing test is one hour: about 20 minutes for Task 1 and about 40 minutes for Task 2.

About the model essays. The essays in Modules 4 to 10 show a method. They are not templates, and students should never memorise them. An answer that is memorised in full can receive no credit, and phrases from a memorised answer used in the wrong place weaken Lexical Resource.

Review before use. The band labels in this book are teaching estimates written against the public descriptors. The essays and exercises have not been reviewed by anyone with IELTS examiner experience. A teacher with that experience should read the model essays, the rubric in Module 15 and the checklist in Module 16 before the book is used for assessment.
