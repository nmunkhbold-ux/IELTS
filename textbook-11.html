11.1 Present simple and past simple

Explanation. The present simple states general truths and things that are true now. The past simple states completed actions at a past time.

In Task 1. Use the past simple for data with past dates (rose in 2010). Use the present simple for a general truth, for a process (the oil is filtered) and for the chart itself (the chart shows, even when the data is old).

Examples. The figure rose to 28% in 2020. / The river carries sand downhill.

Incorrect Correct Why
In 2010, sales increase. In 2010, sales increased. A past date needs the past simple
The chart showed the percentage of … The chart shows the percentage of … The chart exists now
Sales rose in 2005 and fall in 2006. Sales rose in 2005 and fell in 2006. Keep one tense in a sentence

Band 7+ sentences. The line graph compares the proportion of commuters who cycled to work in three cities. / By 2020, cycling had become far more popular in Northbury.

11.2 Present perfect

Explanation. The present perfect (have/has + past participle) links the past to the present: a change that started earlier and continues now.

In Task 1. Use it when the data runs up to the present or the task says since, over the last decade or today. Use the past simple when the data ends in the past.

Examples. Sales have risen steadily since 2010. / The area has changed considerably over the last thirty years.

Incorrect Correct Why
Sales have risen in 2010. Sales rose in 2010. A finished past date needs the past simple
It has increased since 2000 to 2010. It increased from 2000 to 2010. Since has no end point
The village has been changed in 1995. The village changed in 1995. A past date rules out the present perfect

Band 7+ sentences. Since 2000, the number of cyclists has more than doubled. / The farmland has been replaced by a housing estate.

11.3 Future forms and predictions

Explanation. The future describes events that have not happened: predictions and plans.

In Task 1. Use will, is predicted to, is expected to, is projected to and is forecast to for projections, and will be built for proposals on maps.

Examples. The population is projected to reach 12 million by 2040. / A ferry terminal will be constructed at the end of the pier.

Incorrect Correct Why
The figure will rise in 2015. The figure rose in 2015. If the date is past, use the past simple
Sales are going to increase. Sales are expected to increase. Going to is too informal
The population will probably be … The population is projected to be … A projection in a chart is not a guess

Band 7+ sentences. Prices are expected to level off at around $4 after 2030. / According to the plan, the car park will be turned into a public square.

11.4 Comparatives and superlatives

Explanation. Add -er and -est to short adjectives (higher, highest), use more and most with long adjectives (more popular), and use as … as for equality. Irregular forms: better, worse, further.

In Task 1. Comparatives and superlatives make up most of the comparison language. Add a modifier to show the size of the difference: much, far, slightly, considerably.

Examples. Rents in Harbourton were almost three times as high as in Stonebridge. / Tea was by far the most popular drink.

Incorrect Correct Why
more higher higher Do not use more with -er
the most highest the highest Do not use most with -est
higher that A higher than A Use than, not that
the more popular drink (of three) the most popular drink Use a superlative for three or more

Band 7+ sentences. Women were far more likely to swim than men. / Gas became the largest source, well ahead of coal.

11.5 Complex sentences

Explanation. A complex sentence has a main clause and at least one subordinate clause, joined by although, because, while, whereas, when, if, after, before or so that.

In Task 1. Complex sentences join a trend to its comparison or its result, and let you pack two ideas into one sentence. Use whereas and while for contrast.

Examples. Sales of tablets doubled, whereas sales of laptops fell. / Although the temperature fell in December, visitor numbers rose.

Incorrect Correct Why
Sales rose, profits fell. Sales rose, but profits fell. A comma alone cannot join two sentences
Whereas sales fell. Sales of laptops fell, whereas sales of tablets doubled. A subordinate clause cannot stand alone
Although sales fell, but profits rose. Although sales fell, profits rose. Use although or but, not both

Band 7+ sentences. Eastfield's figure peaked at 27% in 2010 before falling back to 21%, only one percentage point above its starting level. / When the river reaches the flat land, it slows down.

11.6 Relative clauses

Explanation. A relative clause gives more information about a noun. Who is for people, which for things, where for places. A defining clause (no commas) identifies the noun; a non-defining clause (with commas) adds extra information. Which can refer to a whole clause.

In Task 1. Relative clauses attach a comparison or a result to a figure without starting a new sentence.

Examples. Harbourton topped the ranking at $1,850, which was almost three times the figure for Stonebridge. / The cooled fluid leaves the tank, which is when the pump takes over.

Incorrect Correct Why
Sales rose, that was unexpected. Sales rose, which was unexpected. That cannot follow a comma in this use
The people who they cycle … The people who cycle … Do not repeat the subject
The town, where is by the sea … The town, which is by the sea … Where needs a clause that includes a subject

Band 7+ sentences. Northbury's figure more than tripled to reach 28% in 2020, which made it the leading city. / The proportion of commuters who cycled to work fell in two of the three cities.

11.7 Passive voice

Explanation. The passive is be + past participle (is heated, was built, will be demolished). Use it when the doer is unknown, unimportant or obvious.

In Task 1. The passive is the main structure in processes, flow charts and maps: the olives are washed, the farm was replaced, a hotel will be built.

Examples. The paste is pressed so that the oil separates. / The shop was demolished and a supermarket was built on the site.

Incorrect Correct Why
The figure was risen. The figure rose. Rise is intransitive and has no passive
The oil is filtering. The oil is filtered. Use the past participle, not -ing
The wall was removed by the builders. The wall was removed. An unimportant doer is left out

Band 7+ sentences. The olives are washed to remove leaves and dirt before being crushed into a paste. / The school was extended with a new building to its south.

11.8 Participial clauses

Explanation. A participial clause starts with an -ing or -ed form. It shortens a clause and adds information: a result, a simultaneous action or a description.

In Task 1. Use -ing for a result (making it the leading city) or an action alongside the main one (carrying sand and mud). Use a past participle for something done to the noun (known as sediment). Use before or after with -ing to link two stages (before falling, after being crushed).

Examples. The river flows downhill, carrying sand and mud. / Social media use quadrupled, overtaking every other activity.

Incorrect Correct Why
Rising from 5 to 9, the chart shows video games. Rising from 5 to 9, video games became popular. The participle must describe the subject of the main clause
Having rise, the figure … Having risen, the figure … Use the past participle after having
Before to fall … Before falling … Before is followed by -ing

Band 7+ sentences. Eastfield's figure climbed to a peak of 27% in 2010 before falling back to 21%. / Walking attracted 68% of women and 55% of men, making it the clear first choice in both groups.

11.9 Prepositions for time, movement and location

Explanation. Prepositions show when, how much and where. In Task 1 they carry most of the precision.

In Task 1. Time: in 2010, by 2010, from … to …, between … and …, since, until, for, over. Movement: rose by 5% (the amount), rose to 20% (the end point), peaked at 40%, fluctuated between 5% and 10%. Location: in the north-west of, to the north of, beside, along, opposite.

Examples. Sales rose from 5 million to 9 million between 2000 and 2010. / A path runs along the river to the north of the park.

Incorrect Correct Why
rose to 5% (meaning an increase of 5 points) rose by 5 percentage points By gives the amount
peaked in 40% peaked at 40% At goes with a figure
between 2000 to 2010 between 2000 and 2010 Between needs and
in the north of the village (when it is outside) to the north of the village To the north of means outside

Band 7+ sentences. The figure fluctuated between 8% and 12% over the following decade. / The supermarket was built on the site of the farm, with a car park to its east.

11.10 Quantifiers and approximations

Explanation. Quantifiers tell the reader how much or how many. Use many, few, fewer with countable nouns, and much, little, less with uncountable nouns.

In Task 1. The number of goes with countable nouns (people, visitors, students). The amount of goes with uncountable nouns (money, water, traffic). Approximations (approximately, just over, nearly) are safe when you cannot read an exact figure.

Examples. The number of visitors rose to 102,000. / Just under half of the respondents agreed.

Incorrect Correct Why
the amount of people the number of people People is countable
less people fewer people Use fewer with countable nouns
the number of students are rising the number of students is rising The subject is the number, which is singular

Band 7+ sentences. Nearly 90% of 16- to 24-year-olds used social media. / A quarter of households owned their homes outright, while fewer than one in five rented privately.

11.11 Noun phrases

Explanation. A noun phrase packs information around a noun, with words before it (a sharp rise) and after it (in unemployment among young people).

In Task 1. Noun phrases let you replace a whole clause with one phrase and keep sentences compact. They are the main sign of formal writing.

Examples. There was a gradual decline in the proportion of households with internet access. / The proportion of commuters who cycled to work.

Incorrect Correct Why
a rise increase in sales a rise in sales Do not repeat the idea
a increase an increase An before a vowel sound
the percent of people the percentage of people Percent is not a noun for a share

Band 7+ sentences. There was a sharp fall in the number of tourists visiting the region. / A considerable increase in social media use was the most striking change.

11.12 Subject-verb agreement

Explanation. The verb agrees with its true subject: singular with singular, plural with plural, even when words come between them.

In Task 1. Pay attention to the number of, the percentage of, the proportion of, the figures for, each of, and a number of.

Examples. The number of visitors was 102,000. / The figures for 2010 and 2020 were similar.

Incorrect Correct Why
The percentage of students who smoke are falling. The percentage of students who smoke is falling. The subject is the percentage
A number of people was surveyed. A number of people were surveyed. A number of means several, so it is plural
The data was collected in 2024. The data were collected in 2024. Data is plural in formal writing; singular is increasingly accepted, but stay consistent

Band 7+ sentences. The proportion of households that rented privately rose steadily. / Each of the three cities showed a different pattern.

A grammar summary for the Task 1 writer

Task Main structures
Line graph, bar chart, pie chart, table Past simple (or present for no date); comparatives and superlatives; noun phrases; whereas and relative clauses
Process, life cycle, flow chart Present simple passive; -ing and before/after -ing; so that; conditionals (if, when, otherwise)
Map, floor plan Past simple or future passive; prepositions of place; relative clauses
Unusual visuals The method of the nearest standard type
