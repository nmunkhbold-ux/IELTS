Paraphrasing: same meaning, new words

Paraphrasing is the skill behind almost every part of IELTS. Task 1 and Task 2 introductions start with it, Reading questions are paraphrases of the passage, and in Speaking it shows the examiner your range of vocabulary. Today you will learn ten techniques, avoid the common traps, and practise until it feels natural.

Part A. Paraphrasing and plagiarism

1

What is paraphrasing?

Paraphrasing means saying someone else’s idea in your own words while keeping exactly the same meaning. Plagiarism is presenting someone else’s words or ideas as your own. It is treated as cheating in schools, universities and at work.

2

Plagiarism or not?

A student is writing a university essay. Decide whether each action is plagiarism.

To avoid plagiarism at university: paraphrase, put exact words in quotation marks, and always say where the idea came from. In IELTS, you never quote: everything must be in your own words.

Part B. Four steps to a good paraphrase

3

The method

1
Read and understand. You cannot paraphrase what you do not understand.
2
Find the main idea and keywords. Who or what? What happens? When, where, how much? Numbers and names must stay the same.
3
Rewrite using at least two techniques. Changing one word is not enough: change words and structure.
4
Compare with the original. Different words? Same meaning? Nothing added or lost?
