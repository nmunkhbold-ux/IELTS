Part E. A model report

Compare with a model

Open the model report (about 170 words)

The bar chart compares the number of hours that school pupils in the United States, Australia and Canada spend each day on sleeping, studying and relaxing.

Overall, Australian pupils devote considerably more time to studying and far less time to relaxing than their counterparts in the other two countries. Meanwhile, daily routines in the United States and Canada are very similar.

American pupils sleep for 8.5 hours, study for 8 hours and relax for 7.5 hours a day. Canadian pupils follow almost the same pattern: they sleep for half an hour less, at 8 hours, but study for half an hour more, at 8.5 hours, while their relaxation time is identical, at 7.5 hours.

Australia, by contrast, stands out. Pupils there allocate 13 hours a day to studying, roughly five hours more than in the other two countries. As a result, they sleep the least, at 7 hours, and relax for only 4 hours, which is nine hours less than they spend studying.
