Module 12: Linking, cohesion and complex sentences

Two of the four criteria depend on how you connect ideas. Coherence and Cohesion asks whether the reader can follow your argument, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy asks whether you can build complex sentences. Linking words sit at the centre of both, which is why they are taught together here. The official guidance names three kinds of cohesive device, and it names the complex structures that count at Band 7. This module covers all of them.

A. Three kinds of cohesion

Kind What it does Examples
1. Sequencers and other adverbials Show the order and stages of the essay First, secondly, finally, moreover, in addition, in conclusion
2. Linking devices that show relationships Join ideas inside and between sentences and, because, but, since, so that, although, even though, whereas
3. Reference and substitution Avoid repetition and point back to earlier ideas it, they, this, these, such, one, the (for something already mentioned)

The descriptors reward a range of devices used flexibly. At Band 6 the linking is often mechanical (the same words at the start of every sentence) or faulty. At Band 7 there is clear progression throughout, with some over- or under-use allowed. The three kinds need to appear together: a paragraph with only moreover and however is not cohesive.

B. Linking words by function

Choose the function first, then the word.

Function Inside a sentence Between sentences (comma after it)
Addition and, as well as, not only... but also... In addition, / Moreover, / Furthermore, / What is more,
Contrast but, whereas, while, although, even though However, / On the other hand, / In contrast, / Nevertheless,
Reason because, since, as, due to, owing to (+ noun) The reason for this is that...
Result so, so... that..., such... that... As a result, / Consequently, / Therefore, / Thus,
Purpose so that, in order to, to, so as to For this reason, (result)
Condition if, unless, provided that, as long as Otherwise,
Concession although, even though, despite / in spite of (+ noun or -ing) Nevertheless, / Even so,
Example such as, including For example, / For instance, / To illustrate,
Sequence before, after, once, when First, / Secondly, / Finally,
Clarification that is, meaning In other words,
Summary In conclusion, / To sum up, / Overall, / On balance,

The same idea three ways. A flexible writer chooses a different pattern each time.

Relationship Pattern 1 Pattern 2 Pattern 3
Contrast Although online shopping is convenient, it creates risks. Online shopping is convenient. However, it creates risks. Online shopping is convenient, but it creates risks.
Reason Because public transport is cheap, many people use it. Many people use public transport because it is cheap. Many people use public transport owing to its low cost.
Result Prices rose, so fewer people bought cars. Prices rose. As a result, fewer people bought cars. Prices rose so sharply that fewer people bought cars.

Punctuation rules for linkers

Rule Right Wrong
A sentence adverb starts a sentence with a comma after it Cars are convenient. However, they cause pollution. Cars are convenient. However they cause pollution.
Two full sentences cannot be joined by a comma and an adverb Cars are convenient; however, they cause pollution. Cars are convenient, however they cause pollution. (comma splice)
A conjunction joins two clauses and needs no comma before because Many people use buses because they are cheap. Many people use buses, because they are cheap. (the comma is not needed)
When a subordinate clause comes first, a comma follows it Although prices rose, people kept buying. Although prices rose people kept buying.
Despite and in spite of take a noun or -ing, not a full clause Despite the high cost, people kept buying. Despite the cost was high, people kept buying.

C. Reference and substitution

The examiner looks for a writer who can refer back without repeating.

Pronouns and demonstratives. Use it, they, this and these to point back, but make sure the reader knows what they refer to.

Summary nouns. A summary noun sums up an earlier idea in one word. It is more precise than this alone.

Idea mentioned Summary phrase
Many people now work from home. This trend / This development / This change
Cities have too many cars. This situation / This problem
Governments could tax plastic bags. Such measures / This approach / This step
Prices rose and wages did not. This gap / This imbalance

Weak: This causes problems. (Causes what? What causes?) Better: This trend causes problems for local shops.

Substitution. one/ones (a cheap one), do so (Those who wish to do so can apply), the former / the latter (for two items mentioned in order).

The definite article. Use a when you first mention something and the when you mention it again. A student who borrows money should read the conditions. The student may repay more than expected.

Before and after

Repetitive: Public transport is cheap. Public transport reduces traffic. Public transport is also good for the environment.

Cohesive: Public transport is cheap and reduces traffic. It is also better for the environment, because one bus can replace dozens of cars.

D. Cohesion across a paragraph

Progression. Each sentence should move the idea forward. A reliable pattern in body paragraphs is: idea, explanation, example, result. The linking words follow the pattern: The main reason is that... (idea), This is because... (explanation), For instance,... (example), As a result,... (result).

Mechanical linking: the Band 6 problem. If every sentence starts with a linker, the paragraph sounds like a list. Compare.

Mechanical: Firstly, online shopping is convenient. Secondly, it saves time. Moreover, it is cheap. Furthermore, it gives choice. In addition, it is safe. However, it has disadvantages. Therefore, I think it is good.

Flexible: Online shopping is convenient because customers can order at any time without travelling, which also saves money. Prices are easy to compare, and a wider choice is available than in most local shops. However, some customers worry about fraud, so secure payment systems are essential.

The second paragraph has three linkers (because, and, However... so) and uses reference (which, customers). It reads as a connected argument.

Three rules for linking

  1. Start no more than about half the sentences in a paragraph with a linking adverbial.
  2. Do not use the same linker twice in one paragraph.
  3. Join short, related sentences with and, because, which or although.

Between paragraphs. A linking phrase at the start of a paragraph tells the reader where you are: On the other hand,..., Another important reason is..., Having considered the problems, it is necessary to look at solutions.

E. Building complex sentences

Both Coherence and Cohesion and Grammatical Range and Accuracy reward sentences with more than one clause. The official guidance says Band 6 needs a mix of simple and complex sentences, and Band 7 needs a variety of complex structures, including passive forms, modal verbs, comparative structures and complex noun phrases. These ten patterns are the most useful. Aim to use at least six of them in every essay.

# Pattern Formula Example Typical error
1 Relative clause noun + who / which / that / whose + clause Students who study abroad often become more independent. Students which study... (use who for people)
2 Contrast and concession Although / While / Whereas + clause, clause Although online shopping is convenient, it has risks. Although... but... (use only one)
3 Conditional If + present, will; If + past, would If schools taught money skills, young adults would make safer choices. If schools will teach...
4 Passive be + past participle Money management should be taught in every school. The problem can solve. (needs be solved)
5 Modal verbs for care may, can, could, tend to, is likely to Long working hours may damage health. Long working hours damage health. (too absolute)
6 Comparatives more / less + adjective + than; the more..., the more... Public transport is far cheaper than owning a car. more cheaper
7 Participle clause -ing or -ed phrase Rising prices have forced many families to cut spending, leading to a drop in sales. A dangling subject: Walking down the street, the shops were closed.
8 Noun clause that / whether / what + clause It is clear that education improves job prospects. It is clear, that... (no comma)
9 Result clause so + adjective + that; such + noun + that The cost was so high that few people could pay. so high that missing that
10 Complex noun phrase noun + prepositional phrase or modifiers The rapid growth of online shopping in rural areas Strings that are too long and unclear

Combining sentences. Practise turning two simple sentences into one complex one.

Two simple sentences One complex sentence
Many people work from home. They save time on travel. Many people who work from home save time on travel.
Prices are rising. Wages are not rising. Although prices are rising, wages are not.
The air in the city is polluted. This causes breathing problems. The polluted air in the city causes breathing problems. (or: The air in the city is polluted, which causes breathing problems.)
Cities need more parks. Parks improve health. Parks reduce stress. Cities need more parks, because green spaces improve health and reduce stress.

Fixing common sentence-level errors

Error Example Correction
Comma splice Cars are useful, they cause pollution. Cars are useful, but they cause pollution. / Cars are useful; however, they cause pollution.
Run-on sentence Many people use phones they check them all day. Many people use phones, and they check them all day.
Fragment Because it saves time. Many people prefer online shopping because it saves time.
Subject–verb agreement The number of cars are increasing. The number of cars is increasing.
Missing article Government should build more roads. The government should build more roads.

Tenses in Task 2

Use Tense Example
General truths and opinions Present simple Education improves job prospects.
Change up to now Present perfect The number of students has risen sharply.
A finished example Past simple In 2020, many offices closed.
Predictions and possibilities will, may, could Printed newspapers may disappear.
Imagined situations Second conditional If cities banned cars, the air would be cleaner.

Articles in three rules. (1) Use a/an the first time you mention a singular noun. (2) Use the for something already mentioned, something unique (the government), or something made specific (the problems of the city). (3) Use no article for general plural or uncountable ideas (Pollution harms children.).

F. Try it yourself

Exercise 1: choose the best linker.

  1. Public transport is cheap. (Moreover / However / Therefore), it reduces traffic.
  2. (Although / Because / So) many people enjoy shopping online, some prefer going to shops.
  3. Prices rose sharply. (As a result / In contrast / For example), fewer people bought houses.
  4. Some people think cities should ban cars. (On the other hand / In addition / Therefore), others disagree.
  5. Many young people use social media, (such as / because of / despite) Instagram and TikTok.
  6. He studied hard (so that / although / unless) he could get into university.
  7. (Despite / Although / Because) the high cost, many students choose to study abroad.
  8. Governments should plan ahead. (Otherwise / Moreover / In conclusion), cities will become overcrowded.
Show the answer key
  1. Moreover (adds an advantage)
  2. Although (contrast within a sentence)
  3. As a result (result)
  4. On the other hand (contrast)
  5. such as (example)
  6. so that (purpose)
  7. Despite (followed by a noun phrase, the high cost)
  8. Otherwise (warning of a consequence)

Exercise 2: combine each pair into one complex sentence. Use the pattern in brackets.

  1. Many students study abroad. They return with new skills. (relative clause)
  2. Online shopping is convenient. It has some risks. (although)
  3. Governments invest in public transport. Cities become less crowded. (conditional)
  4. Fast food is popular. It is cheap and quick. (because)
  5. Many jobs are done by machines. Workers need new skills. (result: so... that or as a result)
Show the answer key

Answers vary. Models:

  1. Many students who study abroad return with new skills.
  2. Although online shopping is convenient, it has some risks.
  3. If governments invested in public transport, cities would become less crowded.
  4. Fast food is popular because it is cheap and quick.
  5. Many jobs are now done by machines, so workers need new skills.

Exercise 3: correct the errors (comma splices, fragments, run-ons, agreement).

  1. Some people prefer cities, others prefer the countryside.
  2. The number of tourists are rising every year.
  3. Because tourism creates jobs.
  4. Cars are expensive to buy they are also expensive to run.
  5. However they have problems, many people like electric cars.
Show the answer key
  1. Some people prefer cities, while others prefer the countryside.
  2. The number of tourists is rising every year.
  3. Many governments encourage tourism because it creates jobs.
  4. Cars are expensive to buy, and they are also expensive to run.
  5. Although they have problems, many people like electric cars.

Exercise 4: rewrite this paragraph so that it is cohesive and not mechanical.

Firstly, working from home saves time. Secondly, it saves money. Moreover, it gives flexibility. Furthermore, it helps families. In addition, it reduces traffic. However, it can be lonely. Therefore, a mix is best.

Show the answer key

Answers vary. A model:

Working from home saves both time and money, because employees no longer need to travel to an office, and it also gives them the flexibility to look after their families. In addition, fewer commuters mean less traffic on the roads. However, some people find home working lonely, so a mixture of office days and home days may be the best solution.
