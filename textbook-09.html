Module 9: Unusual and less common tasks

Some tasks show a visual you may never have seen: a working mechanism, a cross-section, a timeline, a scatter graph. They are not a different test. They ask for the same four things (accurate selection, clear organisation, varied vocabulary and controlled grammar), and every unfamiliar visual can be handled by borrowing a method you already know.

A. Understanding the question

What it shows. Any visual that does not fit the standard types in Module 1: how a device works, a cross-section, a timeline, a schedule, a scatter graph, a radar chart, a histogram, a pictograph, a map with shaded regions, or a chart with two scales.

How to recognise it. You cannot name the visual with the table in Module 1, or it combines features of two types.

What to look for. What is measured or shown (units, labels, key); whether it shows change over time, comparison, sequence or position; and any symbols or colours that need the key.

What the task asks. The wording does not change: Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. The examiner does not expect you to know the subject. The mark depends on what you do with the visual: select, group and compare.

Common wordings.

Wording What it means for your answer
“The diagram shows how a solar water heating system works.” A mechanism: parts and flow, with a loop
“The graph shows the relationship between income and spending on food.” Scatter graph: describe the pattern of the dots
“The timeline shows the main events in the history of a museum.” A sequence of dated events
“The chart shows the schedule of a building project.” Gantt chart: order, duration and overlap
“The cross-section shows the layers of a dam.” Parts from top to bottom and what each does

A short catalogue of less common visuals.

Visual How to recognise it What to describe Language and traps
Scatter graph Dots on two numerical axes The overall pattern (rising, falling or no pattern) and any dot far from the rest tended to, the higher …, the higher …, an outlier. Do not join the dots or describe a trend over time
Radar chart A web of spokes, with a shape for each group Which categories are strongest and weakest, and how the shapes differ peaked in, was weakest in. Read each spoke separately
Timeline Events along a line of dates The order, the gaps between events, the first and last followed by, two years later, the earliest. Past simple
Gantt chart Bars along time, one for each task Which tasks start first, last longest, overlap began, lasted, ran alongside, was completed
Histogram Touching bars for ranges (for example ages) The largest range, the spread, any gaps the largest group, the vast majority fell between. Bars are ranges, not separate items
Chart with two scales A left axis and a right axis Treat it as a mixed chart: say which measure uses which axis measured on the left-hand axis. Read the correct scale
Pictograph Small symbols, each worth a number Use the key to calculate the values, then describe as a bar chart each symbol represents …. Check the key before counting
Cross-section A cut-through of a structure Parts from top to bottom, and what each does lies beneath, is located between, supports
Mechanism Parts joined by arrows, often with a loop The parts, the direction of flow and the loop See the model answer below
Shaded map A map with regions in different shades The regions in each shade, grouped, with compass directions the darkest regions, in the north-east

B. How to analyse an unfamiliar visual: five steps

  1. Name what is shown. Read the title, labels, key and units, and write one line: this shows ….
  2. Ask which question it answers. How much? How has it changed? How does it work or how is it made? Where is it?
  3. Borrow the method of the nearest standard type (see the table below).
  4. Find the two or three biggest features and the exception. Every visual has a main pattern and something that does not fit it.
  5. Choose tense and voice from the date and the kind of visual: past dates take the past simple; mechanisms and processes take the present simple passive.
If the visual mostly shows … It behaves like … Borrow the method from …
Change over time in an unusual shape (timeline, Gantt) A dynamic chart Module 2 and section 5.2
Two groups across categories (radar) A comparison chart Section 5.3
How something works or is made A process, with a loop if the arrows return Module 7
A layout or a place A map or a plan Module 8
Two measures for many items (scatter) A static chart Section 5.1
Two scales A mixed chart Module 6

Three traps. (1) Spending time trying to name the visual instead of reading it. (2) Explaining how the device works using your own knowledge: report only what the diagram shows. (3) Describing every label: select and group, as in every other task.

C. Writing structure for an unusual visual

Paragraph What to write Why
Introduction Paraphrase the task, naming what the visual shows Shows you understand it
Overview The structure of the visual (how many parts, the main pattern or flow) and any special feature such as a loop, an outlier or a backup The big picture, whatever the type
Body 1 The first group of features in logical order Starts the description
Body 2 The remaining features, and the exception Completes it

D. Band 7+ model answer

The diagram below shows how a solar water heating system works.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features.

Write at least 150 words. (About 20 minutes)

Model answer · 176 words

The diagram illustrates how a solar water heating system provides hot water for a house.

Overall, the system works in two connected parts: a closed loop in which a fluid is heated by the sun and carries its heat to a storage tank, and a separate water supply that is warmed in the tank and delivered to the taps. An electric heater is also available when there is little sunlight.

The process begins when sunlight heats the fluid inside the roof panel. The hot fluid then flows to the storage tank, where it passes through a coil and heats the water around it. After that, the cooled fluid leaves the tank, and a pump sends it back up to the roof panel, so the cycle starts again.

At the same time, cold water enters the bottom of the tank, where it is heated by the coil. The hot water then leaves the top of the tank and flows to the taps. If there is little sunlight, an electric heater inside the tank heats the water instead.

E. The model answer explained

Paragraph 1: introduction

Sentence Why it works
“The diagram illustrates how a solar water heating system provides hot water for a house.” Illustrates how … provides replaces shows how … works, and the purpose (hot water for a house) is taken from the diagram's last stage, the taps.

Paragraph 2: overview

Sentence Why it works
“Overall, the system works in two connected parts: a closed loop in which a fluid is heated by the sun and carries its heat to a storage tank, and a separate water supply that is warmed in the tank and delivered to the taps.” The structure of the whole mechanism in one sentence: two parts, and how they connect (at the tank). A closed loop names the cycle in stages 1 to 4. This is the big picture that makes the details easy to follow.
“An electric heater is also available when there is little sunlight.” The backup, reported in the overview because it is a special feature that is easy to miss.

Paragraph 3: the loop

Sentence Why it works
“The process begins when sunlight heats the fluid inside the roof panel.” A clear starting point for the loop.
“The hot fluid then flows to the storage tank, where it passes through a coil and heats the water around it.” Where links the place to the action, and the water around it paraphrases heating the water without repeating it.
“After that, the cooled fluid leaves the tank, and a pump sends it back up to the roof panel, so the cycle starts again.” The loop is closed in words, as the arrow closes it on the diagram. So states the consequence of sending the fluid back.

Paragraph 4: the water supply and the backup

Sentence Why it works
“At the same time, cold water enters the bottom of the tank, where it is heated by the coil.” At the same time shows that the water supply runs alongside the loop. The passive (is heated) is right because the coil does the heating.
“The hot water then leaves the top of the tank and flows to the taps.” The end of the water's journey, with the position (the top) taken from the diagram.
“If there is little sunlight, an electric heater inside the tank heats the water instead.” A conditional sentence reports the backup and closes the answer. Instead shows the alternative to solar heating.

How the answer meets the four criteria

Criterion Evidence in the answer
Task Achievement A clear overview of the structure and the special feature; every stage reported in order; the loop closed; nothing added from outside the diagram.
Coherence and Cohesion Two paragraphs for two parts (the loop, then the water supply); then, after that, at the same time, so, instead; it, the tank as references.
Lexical Resource closed loop, storage tank, coil, cooled fluid, delivered, instead; the diagram's labels are paraphrased.
Grammatical Range and Accuracy Present simple active and passive; relative clauses with which and where; a conditional (if); so result clause; participle phrase (delivered to the taps).
